## 1. Summary

### Takeaway

FMG's human layers are one strictly ordered, single-pass pipeline over a packed Voronoi cell graph: a per-cell **suitability score** (biome habitability, river flux, elevation, coast type, nearby goods) is computed once, and everything else is placed or grown from it with the same two primitives — **quadtree-spaced greedy placement** (culture centres, capitals, towns, religion cores, markets) and **cost-limited Dijkstra flood fill** (cultures, states, provinces, religions, markets, several zones). The political layer (states, diplomacy, forms, provinces, military, treasury) is a one-shot *tableau*, not a simulation: FMG itself says "The map is static. Over-time simulation is planned, but not yet implemented" [STATED, fmg-wiki/Knowledge Base.md:465] and the economy "is a single calculated cycle, not a running simulation" [STATED, fmg-wiki/Knowledge Base.md:761]. The newer economic layer (goods → markets → burg production → global trade → taxes) is the most simulation-like piece, but it is explicitly frozen at one post-generation cycle with no ticking, spending, or population feedback [STATED, docs/prd/state-taxes-and-treasury.md:24-26, 158-162].

For SettlementForge the clean split is: **cell suitability, burg siting, port detection, route cost surfaces, river navigability, goods placement, market territories, and the burg→city-generator record are geography-driven and belong in the map engine**; **cultures, states, diplomacy, forms, provinces, religions, military, treasury, production/trade, zones, journeys and lore are political/simulation content that SettlementForge already owns and should feed in**, with FMG's cost-surface mechanics reused only as *materialisation* (how a political territory flows over terrain).

### The burg → Watabou handoff in one paragraph

[OBSERVED] A burg's preview is chosen by its **burg group** (capital/city/town → Watabou City Generator; village/hamlet → Watabou Village Generator; a `watabou-dwelling` option exists for the smallest), and the link is built from the burg record plus cell geography: name, absolute population, a size class derived from population and urban density, a deterministic seed (map seed + 4-digit burg id), and flags for river (estuary only when coastal), coast, farms (biome), citadel, urban castle, hub (crossroads), plaza (market centre), greens, temple, walls, shanty town, a fixed "natural" style, and a sea direction angle for coastal burgs. Section 4 lists every parameter with its derivation and citation.

## 3. Layer-by-layer

### 3.1 Population / cell ranking (`Population.rankCells`) — PHYSICAL

**Inputs** [OBSERVED, src/generators/population-generator.ts:20-61]: `pack.biomes[].habitability`, `cells.fl` (flux), `cells.conf` (confluence), `cells.h`, `cells.t` (distance-to-coast ring), `cells.r`, `cells.haven`, `cells.harbor`, `cells.f` + `features[].type/subtype`, `cells.area`, `cells.good` + goods values.

**Algorithm in words** [OBSERVED, population-generator.ts:30-60]:
1. Water cells get no score. Base score = biome habitability; a zero-habitability biome (marine, glacier) stays 0 [lines 31-33; biome table at src/generators/biomes-generator.ts:51 gives habitability 0..100 per biome, glacier and marine 0].
2. Add a river bonus: (flux + confluence) normalised between the median non-zero flux and the max, times 250 [line 35].
3. Subtract (height − 50)/5, so lowland is favoured [line 36].
4. Coastal cells (t = 1) add a score by what they are coastal to, from a constant table: estuary (river at coast) +15, ocean coast +5, "safe harbour" (exactly one adjacent water cell) +20, freshwater lake +30, salt lake +10, frozen +1, dry/sinkhole −5, lava −30 [lines 6-16, 38-47].
5. Suitability `cells.s` = score/5 [line 49].
6. Goods bonus: if the cell or a neighbour holds a bonus good, add (own good value + 10, if any) plus the mean good value of neighbours [lines 52-56].
7. Rural population `cells.pop` = suitability × (cell area / mean cell area), 0 when suitability ≤ 0 [line 59].

**Parameters**: the COAST_SCORES table; the 250 flux weight; the /5 and /5 divisors; biome habitability (user-editable in the biomes editor [biomes-generator.ts:145-148]).

**Outputs**: `cells.s` (Int16), `cells.pop` (Float32) in "population points"; absolute people = points × `options.map.units.population.scale` (default 1000) [OBSERVED, src/components/options-model.ts:78].

**Determinism**: pure function of earlier layers, no RNG [INFERRED from the code path: no random calls].

**Depended on by**: cultures (centres and spread), burgs (siting and population), states (cost), religions (candidate cells), military (troops), production (rural output), markers/zones (population thresholds). The pipeline doc confirms "Everything placed by population (cultures, burgs, states) reads it" [STATED, docs/architecture/generation-pipeline.md:274-277].

**Regeneration**: `Population.regenerate` re-ranks and re-derives burg population as suitability/8 + unrounding, ×1.3 capital, ×1.3 port, × gauss(2,3,0.6,20,3) [OBSERVED, population-generator.ts:64-76] — note this formula differs from the pipeline's `Burgs.definePopulation` (3.3 below). `Population.setArea` rescales a region's rural and urban totals to user-given people counts [lines 90-116].

### 3.2 Cultures (`Cultures.generate`, `Cultures.expand`) — SHARED (centre placement physical; identity political)

**Culture sets** [OBSERVED, src/generators/cultures-generator.ts:10-19]: eight named sets with a maximum count and a weight for random selection: All-world (max 32), European (15), Oriental (13), English (10), Antique (10), High Fantasy (17), Dark Fantasy (18), Random (100). The wiki gives the defined-vs-used counts: All-world 33 defined/32 used; Antique 16/10; Dark Fantasy 34/18; English generates 10 names on the fly; Random picks a random name base per culture [STATED, fmg-wiki/Culture-sets.md:3-16, 90-95, 138-139, 186-189]. Shape of a set entry (not reproduced): a display name, a name-base index, an `odd` (inclusion probability), a `sort` function scoring cells for the centre, and a shield shape [OBSERVED, cultures-generator.ts:80-186 for the European set; the `getDefault` method spans 62-1033].

**Centre scoring helpers** [OBSERVED, cultures-generator.ts:70-77]: `n` = normalised suitability (ceil of s/sMax×3); `td` = temperature-difference fee to a target °C; `bd` = biome-mismatch fee (default 4, some 7 or 10); `sf` = not-on-sea-coast fee (default 4). Each culture's sort combines these with `t` (coast distance) and `h` (height), e.g. coastal/naval cultures divide by `sf`, mountain cultures multiply by `h`, cold cultures use `td(i, 5)` or `td(i, -1)`; a fantasy giant culture sorts purely by distance from −10 °C, dragons sort by negative suitability [OBSERVED, representative lines 85-185, 485-497, 935-939].

**Generation** [OBSERVED, cultures-generator.ts:1035-1223]:
1. Count = min(requested limit, set maximum) [1040-1042]; if populated cells < count×25 the count drops to floor(populated/50); with zero populated cells only the "Wildlands" culture 0 exists and a UI warning fires [1045-1089].
2. Selection: locked cultures are kept; otherwise cultures are drawn at random from the set, each accepted by a `P(odd)` roll with up to 200 tries [1091-1114].
3. Centre placement: spacing = (map width + height)/2/count; the populated cells are sorted by the culture's sort function, the candidate is drawn with a power-biased random index in the top half (`biased(0, half, 5)`), and the spacing shrinks ×0.9 per failed attempt (max 100) against a quadtree of existing centres [1124-1139].
4. Culture **type** is defined from the centre cell's geography, in order: Nomadic (h < 70 and biome hot desert/cold desert/grassland), Highland (h > 50), Lake (facing a lake of > 5 cells), Naval (coastal: 10% any sea coast, 60% if a one-cell harbour, 40% if on an "isle" feature), River (river with flux > 100), Hunting (t > 2 and biome in savanna/rainforests/taiga/tundra/wetland), else Generic [1142-1156]. The wiki restates the same order [STATED, fmg-wiki/Culture-types.md:3-26].
5. **Expansionism** = (random × sizeVariety/2 + 1) × type base, with bases Generic 1, Lake 0.8, Naval 1.5, River 0.9, Nomadic 1.5, Hunting 0.7, Highland 1.2 [1158-1167]; also in the wiki [STATED, Culture-types.md:31-42].
6. Each culture also gets a colour, a 1-3 letter code, `origins=[0]`, and a random shield when the emblem shape option is "random" [1192-1199]. Culture 0 "Wildlands" is unshifted at index 0 [1205-1212]. Name-base indices are taken modulo the name-base count [1220-1222].

**Expansion** [OBSERVED, cultures-generator.ts:1269-1359]: a single priority queue seeded with every culture centre at cost 0; each neighbour's entry cost = (biome cost + biome-change cost + height cost + river cost + coast-type cost) / expansionism, accumulated along the path; a cell is claimed by the cheapest arrival; expansion stops when the accumulated cost exceeds `cells × 0.6 × growthRate` [1276, 1347-1356]. Only populated cells are assigned (unpopulated land stays culture-less) [1353]. Cost functions:
- Biome: 10 if the target biome equals the culture centre's biome; else biome base cost ×5 for Hunting, ×10 for Nomadic in forest biomes (5..9), ×2 otherwise; +20 whenever the biome changes between source and target [1295-1300, 1343].
- Height/water: Lake culture crossing a lake 10; Naval crossing water 2×area; Nomadic crossing water 50×area; any other water 6×area; Highland on land h<44: 3000, h<62: 200, else 0; others h≥67: 200, h≥44: 30, else 0 [1302-1315].
- River: River cultures pay 0 on rivers and 100 off them; others pay flux/10 clamped 20..100 on river cells [1317-1321].
- Coast ring: t=1 free for Naval/Lake, 60 Nomadic, 20 others; t=2 30 for Naval/Nomadic; deeper inland 100 for Naval/Lake [1323-1328].
The wiki documents the same numbers and the default biome base costs (hot desert 200, cold desert 150, savanna 60, grassland 50, forests 70/70/80/90, taiga 200, tundra 1000, glacier 5000, wetland 150) [STATED, Culture-types.md:44-85; OBSERVED source table at biomes-generator.ts:71].

**Outputs**: `pack.cultures[]` {name, i, base, shield, centre, type, expansionism, origins, color, code}, `cells.culture` (Uint16). **Depended on by**: burgs (name base, culture id), states (type, native biome, cost), religions (folk religion per culture, naming), emblems (shield shape), military (cultural dividers), production (cultureType multipliers), names everywhere.

**Determinism**: driven by the seeded global PRNG (`Math.random = aleaPRNG(seed)`) [OBSERVED, src/components/seed.ts:20]; `regenerate` re-runs generate+expand and re-binds state/burg/religion cultures to their centre cells [cultures-generator.ts:1495-1508].

### 3.3 Burgs (`Burgs.generate`, `assignPorts`, `specify`) — PHYSICAL siting, SHARED attributes

**Siting inputs** [OBSERVED, src/generators/burgs-generator.ts:74-189]: populated cells that have a culture (`cells.s > 0 && cells.culture`) [80]; `cells.p` positions; options `states.limit` (capital count) and `burgs.limit`.

**Capitals** [89-126]: every cell's suitability is multiplied by a random factor in [0.5, 1); cells sorted descending; the top cells are taken in order and accepted when no existing capital is within spacing = (width+height)/2/capitalsNumber (quadtree); if the list is exhausted the whole pass restarts with spacing/1.2 [97-114]. Each capital gets `state = its own id`, culture from its cell, a *short* culture name, the landmass feature id, `capital = 1` [116-125]. Capital count = `states.limit`, reduced to floor(populated/10) when there are too few populated cells [174-183].

**Towns** [128-166]: scores randomised by gauss(1, 3, 0, 20, 3) (so long-tailed noise); spacing = (w+h)/150/(n^0.7/66); each candidate uses its own minSpacing = spacing × gauss(1, 0.3, 0.2, 2, 2) "to make placement not uniform"; after a full pass the spacing halves and the pass repeats until the count is reached or spacing ≤ 1 [134-165]. Town count: with the "auto" limit (1000 = AUTO_BURG_LIMIT [src/components/options-schema.ts:10]) it is populated/5/(gridPoints/10000)^0.8; otherwise min(limit, populated) [185-188]. Towns start with `state = 0` and are assigned a state later by `States.expandStates` [states-generator.ts:430-434].

[INFERRED] The "score function" is therefore not a separate burg scoring formula: it is the cell suitability from 3.1 (habitability, river flux, lowland, coast type, goods) with multiplicative noise, plus a spacing constraint that scales with the map size and the requested count. Distance to other burgs enters only through the quadtree spacing, never as a score term.

**Burg type** (`getType`, geography not rank) [191-211]: port → Naval; facing a lake → Lake; h > 60 → Highland; river with flux ≥ 100 → River; for small/unsettled cells, pop < 5 in biomes 1-4 → Nomadic, biomes 5-9 → Hunting; else Generic.

**Ports** (`assignPorts`) [214-320]:
- Candidates: coastal burgs with a `haven` and `harbor` > 0 facing a water feature of > 1 cell that is not a non-navigable lake subtype and whose cell temperature is > 0 °C [254-269]; river burgs on navigable cells (river with flux ≥ 100, `Rivers.isNavigable` [src/generators/river-generator.ts:774-777]) with a resolvable drain feature [272-276]. The port feature for a lake with an outlet is the final receiving body (ocean or closed lake), walked through outlet rivers [264-267; river-generator.ts:780-827].
- Selection per water body [282-311]: "preferred" candidates (capital harbour, one-cell safe harbour, river ports) are promoted unconditionally; every landmass touching the body gets at least one port (the lowest rank = capital first, then smallest harbour count); if fewer than 2 ports exist on a body the best remaining candidates are added up to 2; a body with < 2 ports gets none "a lone port is useless" [308].
- A promoted coastal port is moved 95% of the way from the cell centre to the shared edge midpoint with its haven cell [322-333]; river burgs (port or not) are shifted perpendicular to the local river course by min(flux/200, 0.6) map units, side chosen by cell parity [336-376]. Tests cover lake-drain promotion, the two-ports-minimum rule, the island rule and the river-bank shift [OBSERVED, src/generators/burgs-generator.test.ts:67-422]. The PRD explains the widened meaning of `burg.port` as "the final reachable water body" [STATED, docs/prd/navigable-river-routes.md:14-16, 137-146].

**Specification** (`specify`, run after routes/states/religions) [588-605]:
- **Population** [378-387]: suitability/5, ×1.5 if capital, × route connectivity rate (0.8 + 0.2 per road or sea route link + 0.1 per trail link [src/generators/routes-generator.ts:983-1002]), × gauss(1, 1, 0.25, 4, 5), plus a sub-thousandth "unrounding" term from burg and cell ids, floor 0.01. Units are population points; people = points × scale × urbanization rate [OBSERVED, burgs-generator.ts:617-619, 946-951].
- **Emblem** [389-403]: kinship to the state's coat of arms 0.25, +0.1 capital, −0.1 port, −0.25 if the burg's culture differs from the state's; emblem "type" is Capital (20% for capitals), City for Generic, else the burg type (which steers charge choice, 3.11).
- **Buildings** [405-415]: citadel = capital, or pop > 50 at 75%, pop > 15 at 50%, else 10%; walls = capital, or pop > 30, or pop > 20 at 75%, pop > 10 at 50%, else 10%; shanty = pop > 60, or pop > 40 at 75%, or (pop > 20 and walls) at 40%; temple = (cell has a religion and the state is a Theocracy) at 50%, or pop > 50, or pop > 35 at 75%, or pop > 20 at 50%. **Plaza is not set here**: it is set to 1 for market-centre burgs by `Markets.expandMarkets` [OBSERVED, src/generators/markets-generator.ts:172, 295, 316] and cannot be removed while the burg centres a market [burgs-generator.ts:965-971].
- **Group** [522-586] from the default group list [435-506]: capital (feature capital), city (top 10 percentile by population, min 5 points), fort (citadel without walls/plaza/port, ≤ 1), monastery (temple without walls/plaza/port, ≤ 0.8), caravanserai (plaza, no port, ≤ 0.8, desert/savanna biomes), trading_post (plaza, ≤ 0.8, forest/tundra/wetland biomes), village (0.1..2), hamlet (no plaza, ≤ 0.1), town (default). Groups are evaluated in descending `order`; a group can also be restricted by states/cultures/religions [562-575]. Each group names its preview generator (`watabou-city` / `watabou-village`) [442, 450, 488, 496, 503].

**Outputs**: `pack.burgs[]` record (documented field by field in docs/architecture/data-model.md:144-176, including that citadel/plaza/shanty/temple/walls are "Used for MFCG" [STATED, lines 169-173]).

**Determinism**: seeded PRNG; `regenerate` keeps locked burgs and market centres, re-sites the rest with score × random and the town spacing formula, re-assigns capitals to states lacking one, and re-runs ports, specify and routes [OBSERVED, burgs-generator.ts:822-925].

### 3.4 States — POLITICAL (expansion is terrain-costed)

**Creation** (`createStates`) [OBSERVED, src/generators/states-generator.ts:296-326]: one state per capital burg, id = burg id; expansionism = random × sizeVariety + 1; name from the capital's name when it is < 9 characters and the cell id is divisible by 5, else a short culture name, then run through `Names.getState` (culture-specific suffixing, 3.9); type = the culture's type; a fresh coat of arms with the culture's shield; `salesTax/pollTax/treasury` start at 0. State 0 is "Neutrals". Default `states.limit` is 18, `sizeVariety` 4, `growthRate` 1 [OBSERVED, src/components/options-model.ts:96].

**Expansion** (`expandStates`) [371-436]: priority-queue flood fill from each capital cell with budget `growthRate = cells/2 × growthRate option` [380]; per-neighbour cost = max(cultureCost + populationCost + biomeCost + heightCost + riverCost + typeCost, 0), and the accumulated cost = parent + 10 + cellCost/expansionism [411-418]:
- culture −9 if the cell's culture equals the state's, else +100 [411];
- population: water 0; populated land max(20 − s, 0); unpopulated land 5000 [412];
- biome: 10 if the cell's biome equals the *culture centre's* biome; Hunting ×2 biome cost; Nomadic ×3 in forest biomes; else ×1 [328-333];
- height/water: Lake crossing lake 10; Naval water 300; Nomadic water 10000; other water 1000; Highland h<62 1100 else 0; mountains h≥67 2200; hills h≥44 300 [335-345];
- river and coast-ring costs are identical to the culture ones [347-358].
Only land cells are assigned [423]; a capital cell is never overwritten [409]; locked states keep their cells [383-387, 408]. Then `normalize` flips isolated land cells (not burgs, not adjacent to a capital) to a neighbour state when ≥ 2 neighbours belong to other states and ≤ 2 to the same [438-455]. Poles of inaccessibility are computed per state for labels [458-466], neighbours from shared land borders [468-495], and greedy 6-colour assignment with jitter [497-517].

**Campaigns** [543-571]: one named past war per neighbour (or one against "neutrals"), with weighted kinds (War 6, Campaign 4, Conflict/Invasion/Rebellion/Conquest 2, Intervention/Expedition/Crusade 1), start = gauss(year − 100, 150) clamped to ≤ year − 6, end = start + gauss(4, 5) [558-561]. These feed battlefield markers, invasion zones and regiment notes.

**Diplomacy** [574-759]: every pair of states gets a relation drawn from weighted tables by adjacency: neighbours {Ally 1, Friendly 2, Neutral 1, Suspicion 10, Rival 9}; neighbours-of-neighbours {Ally 10, Friendly 8, Neutral 5, Suspicion 1}; far states {Friendly 1, Neutral 12, Suspicion 2, Unknown 6}; two Naval states on different landmasses {Neutral 1, Suspicion 2, Rival 1, Unknown 1} [590-593, 630-637]. A neighbour becomes a **Vassal** with 80% chance when the bigger state is above mean area, the smaller below, and the area ratio > 2 [640-641]; vassals copy their suzerain's relations [604-619]. Wars: each state with a Rival, that is not a vassal and not already at war, picks a random independent rival; the attacker proceeds only if area×expansionism ≥ defender's × gauss(1.6, 0.8, 0, 10, 2); vassals join their side, defender allies join unless frightened (then they sever the pact), attacker allies join unless the defender is not their rival and (20% or the attacker is already ≥ 1.2× stronger), and all participants become Enemy; the narrative is pushed into a chronicle stored in state 0's `diplomacy` [648-757]. The relation vocabulary (Ally, Friendly, Neutral, Suspicion, Enemy, Unknown, Rival, Vassal, Suzerain) with chronicle event text lives in src/data/diplomacy.ts:11-66.

**Forms** (`defineStateForms`) [768-908]: the government form is Theocracy when the religion at the state centre has `expansion === "state"`, or 10% when it is Organized/Cult; Anarchy with probability 0.01 − tier/500; otherwise weighted Monarchy 25 / Republic 2 / Union 1 (Naval states: 25/8/3) [772-773, 826-833]. The *form name* is picked by an area tier = min(floor(area/median area × 2.6), 4), with tier 4 ("Empire") only for the largest states by a count^0.4 rule [775-783]; Monarchy tiers Duchy → Grand Duchy → Principality → Kingdom → Empire, with culture-base substitutions (e.g. Khanate, Tsardom, Shogunate, Caliphate, Emirate, Despotate, Satrapy) and vassal variants (Marches, Dominion, Protectorate) [785, 838-865]; Republic names are weighted (Republic 75, Federation/Trade Company 4, …) with Free City/City-state for one-burg states [786-796, 867-877]; Union, Anarchy and Theocracy have their own weighted lists, with Greek/Ruthenian/Arabic/Nigerian theocratic names [797-819, 879-898]. The full form-name catalogue is in STATE_FORMS [79-151]. `fullName` is "Adjective Form" for a listed set of forms, else "Form of Name" [918-943].

**Taxes** [161-168, 910-916]: base rates per form (Monarchy sales 0.15/poll 0.20; Theocracy 0.25/0.10; Union 0.07/0.13; Republic 0.05/0.15; Anarchy 0/0) jittered by gauss(base, 0.15×base, 0.5×base, 1.5×base) [STATED and OBSERVED, docs/domain/taxes.md:18-30; states-generator.ts:910-916]. **Collection** [1400-1430]: treasury reset to 0; every deal's `tax` is credited to the seller's state (burg seller → its state; market seller → its centre burg's state); then poll tax × (rural + urban) is added; neutrals never collect [tests at states-generator.test.ts:39-157].

**Editing API** (relevant for a host that owns politics): `found` (new state from a capital, with inverse relations and an "Independence declaration" chronicle entry) [1052-1113], `remove`, `merge` (with `asProvinces` demotion) [1116-1210], `setCells` with province splitting [1268-1385], `setRelation` [997-1008], `setTaxes/setTreasury` [1226-1240]. `recreate` (regeneration) seeds a fresh PRNG and chooses capitals by population × random with quadtree spacing [191-294].

### 3.5 Provinces — POLITICAL (terrain-costed)

[OBSERVED, src/generators/provinces-generator.ts:84-333]. RNG is re-seeded from the map seed on generation (fresh seed on regeneration) [85-86]. Budget `maxGrowth` = 1000 when the ratio option is 100, else gauss(20, 5, 5, 100) × ratio^0.5 [111]; default ratio 20 [options-model.ts:97]. Per state: burgs of the state without a province are ordered capital-first then population × gauss(1, 0.2, 0.5, 1.5, 3); a state needs ≥ 2 such burgs; province count = max(ceil(burgs × ratio/100), 2) [120-127]; each province is centred on a burg, named after it 50% of the time (else a state-style name), with a form name drawn from a weighted table per government form (Monarchy: County 22, Earldom 6, …; Republic: Province 6, …; Theocracy: Parish 3, Deanery 1; Union, Anarchy, and a "Wild" table of Territory/Land/Region/Tribe/Clan/Dependency/Area) [39-77, 130-158]; each pick raises that form name's weight by 10 so a state's provinces tend to share a style [137-138]. Expansion is a priority-queue flood confined to the province's own state over land, with entry cost 100 for h ≥ 70, 30 for h ≥ 50, 10 for other land, 100 over water, and deep ocean (t = 0) impassable [172-191]. A shape-justification pass reassigns cells surrounded by a competing province [194-212]. Cells of a state left without a province become "wild" provinces grown from the first burg cell or any cell, with costs 3 inside the state, 20 on other land, 10 on coastal water, 30 on deeper water [215-261]; such a province is an "Island"/"Islands" when it coincides with an isle feature, a "Colony" (50%) when not land-reachable within the same state, else a Wild form; colonies are named "New <state or core province>" 80% of the time [263-287]. Emblems: kinship 0.8 (named by burg) or 0.4, dominion canton for colonies 95%, isles 70%, wild 30% [141-144, 289-293]. Poles of inaccessibility per province [528-536]. **Outputs**: `pack.provinces[]` {state, center, burg, name, formName, fullName, color, coa}, `cells.province`, `state.provinces`.

### 3.6 Routes — PHYSICAL (graph from burgs)

[OBSERVED, src/generators/routes-generator.ts]. `generate` re-seeds the PRNG from the map seed, rebuilds the directed river-edge map from `pack.rivers[].cells`, registers locked routes' connections, and creates three groups [221-231, 696-724]:
1. **Main roads**: per landmass feature, the capitals' positions form a Delaunay triangulation reduced to an **Urquhart graph** (drop the longest edge of each triangle) [256-298, 419-441]; each edge is routed with the land cost; the cells along it become "connections" that discount later paths.
2. **Trails**: the same per landmass over *all* burgs [454-476].
3. **Sea routes**: per water body (`burg.port` feature id), Urquhart over ports, routed with the water cost [478-500]. Computation order is sea, roads, trails, so sea routes are built first; output order is roads, trails, searoutes [697-721].

**Land cost** (`getLandPathCost`) [300-314]: water cells and zero-habitability biomes are impassable; cost = squared distance × habitability modifier (1 + max(100 − habitability, 0)/1000) × height modifier (1 + max(h − 25, 25)/25, so ≥ 2 and rising with height above 50) × 0.5 if the step is already a connection × 3 unless the target holds a burg. [INFERRED] The inline comment claims the height modifier spans [1, 3], but as written its floor is 2; the practical effect is still "mountains cost up to ~2× lowland, existing routes half price, burg cells one third".

**Water cost** (`getWaterPathCost`) [316-344]: a land cell is enterable only if it is a navigable river cell *and* the step follows a recorded river edge (cost = squared distance × 1.5); leaving land requires following the river's outlet or, for a coastal port, the burg's haven; sea cells below −4 °C are impassable; otherwise squared distance × a ring modifier (coastline 1, sea 1.8, open sea 4, ocean 6, far ocean 8) [13-24, 316-344]. A water route may only *arrive* at a port via its haven or along the river [379-390]. Tests lock the watershed invariant (no hopping between Voronoi-adjacent cells of different rivers) [routes-generator.test.ts:56-181].

**Pathfinder**: `findPath` in src/utils/pathUtils.ts:337-372 is a uniform-cost (Dijkstra-style) search with a FlatQueue and no heuristic [OBSERVED]; the journeys module has its own A* with an admissible heuristic (3.13). Paths are split into segments at already-connected cells [350-376], merged end-to-start [674-694], smoothed by pulling non-burg vertices with angles < 135° (strongly < 115°) toward the chord midpoint [634-671], and sea routes are given river meanders by splicing the river polygon's own geometry along "river runs" in canonical source→mouth order [518-626]; the PRD explains the phase-matching rationale [STATED, docs/prd/meandering-river-routes.md:17-23, 247-249]. Route names come from weighted models (burg adjective + suffix, prefix + suffix, "The <descriptor> …") with group-specific suffix pools (road/route/way/highway; trail/path/track/pass; route/lane/passage/water way) [1004-1026, 29-174].

**Outputs**: `pack.routes[]` {i, group, feature, points [x, y, cellId], name}, `cells.routes` adjacency index [758-779]. **Depended on by**: burg population (connectivity), religions (passage cost), markers (inns at crossroads, brigands on roads, pirates/sea monsters on sea routes, lighthouses), zones (disease spread, avalanche), journeys, trade animation. Helpers `isCrossroad` (> 3 links or > 2 road links) [850-859] and `getConnectivityRate` [983-1002] feed the Watabou handoff.

### 3.7 Religions — POLITICAL (culture/state-gated flood fill)

[OBSERVED, src/generators/religions-generator.ts]. Four types (Folk, Organized, Cult, Heresy) and three expansion scopes (global, state, culture) [36-37]. Forms are weighted per type (Folk: Shamanism/Animism/Polytheism 4 each, Ancestor Worship 2, Nature Worship/Totemism 1; Organized: Polytheism/Monotheism 7, Dualism 3, Syncretism/Pantheism/Non-theism 2, Philosophical/Ethical/Deism/Henotheism 1; Cult: Cult/Dark Cult 5, Sect 1) [411-440]. Expansionism: Folk 0, Organized gauss(5, 3, 0, 10), Cult gauss(0.5, 0.5, 0, 5), Heresy gauss(1, 0.5, 0, 5) [616-621].

**Pipeline** [628-646]: (1) one Folk religion per culture at the culture centre [648-657]; (2) Organized/Cult cores: count = `religions.limit` (default 6 [options-model.ts:98]) minus locked; candidates are burgs sorted by population (or cells with s > 2 when burgs are too few), accepted with quadtree spacing (w+h)/2/limit; 10-40% of cores become Cults [659-718]; (3) naming (3.9) also decides the scope: "Place + …" names are state-scoped, "Culture + …" names culture-scoped, others global; a state-scoped religion in neutral land becomes global [720-746, 1437-1450]; deity names are "<culture name>, The <meaning>" from 14 phrase approaches over word pools (numbers, beings, animals, adjectives, genitives, colours) [54-409, 1410-1474]; (4) folk religions with an organized culture-scoped competitor are renamed "Old …" [847-860]; (5) **expansion** [1027-1075]: folk religions first take every cell of their culture [1175-1194]; then organized/cult cores flood with budget cells/20 × cultures.growthRate, blocked outside their culture or state when scoped, with cost = 10 per culture change + 10 per state change + passage cost (water 500, or 50 along a route; land biome cost, 1 on a road, biome/3 on a trail) and total = parent + 10 + cost/expansionism; only cultured cells are assigned [1053-1070, 1165-1172]; (6) **origins**: folk → [0]; an organized religion is folk-based 50% of the time (by cell parity) when culture-scoped, else its origins are the up to 2 (organized) or 3 (cult) lower-id religions found within a 100- or 50-cell BFS, with cycle checks [864-1024]; (7) **heresies** [909-970]: organized religions with expansionism ≥ 3 spawn gauss(0, 1, 0, 3) heresies at random boundary cells; each inherits form and deity, takes a mixed colour, and floods only over its parent's cells (cost 0 inside parent, 2000 outside) [1077-1115]; (8) centres are moved into the religion's area or reset to the culture home when extinct [1196-1208]. Tests cover heresy parentage and locked-religion origin preservation [religions-generator.test.ts:41-383].

**Outputs**: `pack.religions[]` {name, type, form, culture, center, deity, expansion, expansionism, color, code, origins}, `cells.religion`. **Depended on by**: state forms (theocracy), burg temples, military dividers, markers (sacred sites), zones (proselytism, crusade), production (religion multipliers).

### 3.8 Military and the battle simulator — POLITICAL

**Generation** [OBSERVED, src/generators/military-generator.ts:54-457]. Per state: `alert` = clamp(expansionRate × diplomacyRate × neighborsRate, 0.1, 5), where expansionRate = clamp((expansionism/Σexpansionism)/(area/Σarea), 0.25, 4), diplomacyRate = 1 with any Enemy, 0.8 Rival, 0.5 Suspicion, else 0.1, and neighborsRate = clamp(0.5 + Σ per-neighbour stance modifiers {Ally −0.2, Friendly −0.1, Suspicion 0.1, Enemy 1, Rival 0.5, Vassal 0.5, Suzerain −0.5}, 0.3, 3) [61-72, 211-223]. Each unit type gets a state multiplier from a type × culture-type matrix (e.g. Nomadic mounted 2.3, Naval naval 1.8, Hunting ranged 2) ×2 mounted for "Horde" forms, ×1.2 naval for Republics, × alert [74-139, 227-237]. Per rural cell: base = pop/100, divided by 2 (1.2 for Unions) when the culture is not the state's, by 1.4 (2.2 for Theocracies) when the religion differs from the state centre's, by 1.8 (1.2 for Naval states) on another landmass; × unit rural %, × a cell-type matrix (nomadic/wetland/highland by biome and h ≥ 70) × state multiplier × population scale [240-313]; naval rural units only in cells with a haven and are placed on the haven water cell. Per burg: base = population × urbanization/100, ×1.2 capital, same dividers, urban % and a burg-type matrix; naval only for ports [316-370]. Platoons are merged into regiments by a quadtree sweep: a platoon within 20 units of another is merged; otherwise it searches a radius (expected − size)/20 (or /40 for "separate" units like fleets) for a partner below the expected size 3 × population scale; separate units only merge with the same unit [372-450]. Regiments get ordinal names ("1st (Province) Regiment"/"Fleet"), an icon (crown for royal regiments in Monarchy capitals, else the dominant unit's icon), and a note dated from a random campaign [515-559, 729-742]. Default units: infantry (rural 0.25%, urban 0.2%, power 1), archers (0.12/0.2), cavalry (0.12/0.03, crew 2, power 2), artillery (0/0.03, crew 8, power 12), fleet (0/0.015, crew 100, power 50, separate) [459-513]; the wiki restates all tables [STATED, fmg-wiki/Military-Forces.md:32-118].

**Battle simulator** (a controller, not a generator) [OBSERVED, src/controllers/battle-screen.ts]: battle type from the two initial regiments in order naval → air → landing → siege (defender in a burg with walls or citadel) → ambush (10% in forest/wetland biomes) → field [451-471]; per side strength = Σ units × unit power × phase modifier / adjuster, adjuster = max(population scale/10, 10) [720-892]; initial morale = clamp(100 − (strength ratio)^1.5 × 10 + 10, 50, 100) minus a supply-line fee min(mean distance to base/50, 15) [894-903]; each side rolls a d6 that multiplies strength by die/10 + 0.4 [921-931, 1105-1106]; per iteration total casualties = random × max(phase casualty rate), split inversely to strength (attackers lose casualties × defense/(attack+defense)), and morale drops by casualties × 100 + 1 [1104-1147]; phases change by morale (retreat when P(1 − morale/25)) and ranged share [933-960]; applying writes a result note to each regiment, replaces units by survivors, and (per the wiki) adds a battlefield marker [1251-1299; STATED fmg-wiki/Battle-Simulator.md:28]. All phase modifier tables are in the wiki [STATED, Battle-Simulator.md:30-121].

### 3.9 Names (Markov) — SHARED (data political, mechanism generic)

[OBSERVED, src/generators/names-generator.ts]. A **name base** is {name, index, min, max, allowed duplicate letters, deprecated multi-word rate, comma-separated sample names} [src/data/name-bases.ts:1-10]; there are **43 default bases** (indices 0-31 real-world languages, 32-41 fantasy races, 42 Levantine) [OBSERVED count by grep; fantasy range confirmed by the "no suffix for fantasy bases" rule at names-generator.ts:223 and base 42 used by the Levant culture at cultures-generator.ts:1027]. The chain is built by splitting each lowercased sample into pseudo-syllables (≤ 5 chars, breaking on vowel patterns with a few diphthong exceptions, CJK characters as single syllables) keyed by the preceding letter [19-62]. Generation walks the chain up to 20 steps enforcing min/max length, then post-processes (no trailing apostrophe/space/hyphen, collapse disallowed doubled letters and triple letters, "ae"→"e", capitalise after space/hyphen, join one-letter parts) and falls back to a random sample name when too short [73-153]. `getCulture`/`getCultureShort` pick the culture's base (short = min−1, max−2) [156-179]; `getState` strips place-name endings (-berg, -ton, Ruthenian -sk/-ev/-ov), applies Japanese vowel/-u and Arabic "Al" rules, no suffix for fantasy bases, probabilistic vowel trimming, then a culture-specific suffix (default "ia", with base-specific "land", "terra/terre", "eia", "maa", "orszag", "yurt/eli", "guk", " Guo", "tlan/co", "a") [199-274]; `getMapName` and `getEra` [277-297]. Every other layer calls Names for burgs, states, provinces, religions, deities, markers and journey heroes.

### 3.12 Economic layer: goods, markets, production, taxes — SHARED (placement physical; markets/production simulation)

**Goods catalogue** [OBSERVED, src/generators/goods-generator.ts:76-968]: **71 default goods**, each {name, tags, icon, color, value, unit, chance, distribution (raw), biomeOutput, recipes (manufactured), multipliers, demandCoverage}. Raw goods have a `distribution` expression and a `chance`; manufactured goods have `recipes` (alternative input maps) and chance 0; Coal and Oil are hybrids (both) [docs/domain/goods_schema.md:5-15]. Demand categories in priority order food/utilities/construction/military/luxury with per-capita targets 0.2/0.15/0.1/0.08/0.07 [goods-generator.ts:54-62].

**Placement** [986-1025]: seeded from the map seed; cells shuffled; the goods list reshuffled every 10 cells; per cell the first good that has a distribution and chance, is below its cap ceil(200 × cells/5000), passes a chance% roll and whose distribution evaluates true is placed (one good per cell); glacier cells skipped [998-1021]. Distribution expressions are plain JS over a method table: random, nth (cell id parity), minHabitability, habitability (probabilistic), elevation (probabilistic by height), biome, minHeight/maxHeight, minTemp/maxTemp, shore (coast ring), type (feature type/subtype), river [1075-1094]; the wiki documents each and lists the default distributions [STATED, fmg-wiki/Goods-spread-functions.md:14-103]. [OBSERVED discrepancy] the wiki attributes a distribution to Tar, but in source Tar has no distribution and is made from Wood; the listed expression belongs to Saltpeter [goods-generator.ts:458-479].

**Markets** [OBSERVED, src/generators/markets-generator.ts]: centres chosen by burg population ×2.5 capital ×1.2 port × random(0.5..2.5), accepted with quadtree spacing ((w+h)×2/burgs^0.6) that grows by 1 per market [55-98]; territories by priority-queue flood with cost 10 per step, +50 on water (+50 more if the water is not the centre burg's port body), +100 crossing to another landmass, +100 into another state; water cells only join a market when they hold a good; every burg gets `market` from its cell and centre burgs get `plaza = 1` [114-176]. [OBSERVED discrepancy] docs/domain/trade_schema.md:9-11 says capitals ×2, ports ×2 and that routes/rivers/provinces/mountains affect the flood; the source has only the costs above.

**Production cycle** (`Production.produce`) [OBSERVED, src/generators/production-generator.ts:36-63]: (1) rural cells seed their market's stock with biome output × population × modifiers plus a bonus-good channel min(pop × 0.25, 5) [727-758; markets-generator.ts:178-193]; (2) initial prices: raw goods by (demand+5)/(stock+5) clamped 0.1×..5× value, manufactured by local ingredient cost + value-add [markets-generator.ts:204-246]; (3) burgs in ascending population run a worker loop capped at 1000 workers: each fractional worker picks the productive good with the best projected gain per worker, planning missing ingredients recursively with reservations and a cycle guard, with a demand multiplier 1 + coverage × shortage for the highest unmet category; the previously active goal is kept when its gain is not lower [94-155, 503-698]; ingredients come from inventory then market buys (deal recorded, treasury debited); output = yield × modifiers; the whole inventory is then sold to the local market with sales tax recorded on the deal [157-236]; (4) global trade moves surpluses above a reserve = demand × 1.2 between markets, greedily by total profit, with landed cost = exporter price + (octile distance/diagonal) × value + exporter sales tax per unit and a minimum profit of 1 [markets-generator.ts:425-557]; (5) burgs buy goods to cover personal demand, cheapest cost-per-coverage first, within treasury [320-378]. Prices move ±1% of base value per unit traded, floor 0.1× / ceiling 5×, with a 10% bid/ask margin [559-572]. Modifiers multiply six dimensions: culture type (burg type if a burg, else culture type), culture id, state id, religion id, biome id, zone membership [760-789]. [OBSERVED discrepancy] docs/domain/production_schema.md:55 describes a 0.85 "goal stickiness" factor; the source keeps the active goal only when its normalised gain is ≥ the best candidate's [production-generator.ts:694].

**Taxes/treasury**: see 3.4. The PRD lists what is deliberately out of scope: ticking, spending (administration, military upkeep, corruption), form-name modifiers, import tariffs, and a "Regenerate Production" action [STATED, docs/prd/state-taxes-and-treasury.md:156-162].

**Outputs**: `pack.goods`, `cells.good`, `pack.markets[].goods{stock, price}`, `cells.market`, `pack.deals[]`, `burg.production/product/treasury`, `state.treasury`. **Coupling to geography**: goods placement reads only biome, height, temperature, coast ring, feature type and rivers [STATED, Goods-spread-functions.md:1]; rural output is per-biome; market territories cost water, islands and borders; trade distance is straight-line between market centres (not route length) [markets-generator.ts:436-451]; the trade *animation* paths over routes separately [STATED, trade_schema.md:157-167].

### 3.13 Journeys and transports — SIMULATION-flavoured narrative over routes

[OBSERVED, src/generators/journeys/journeys-generator.ts:52-67]: every new map gets exactly one random journey (regeneration keeps existing ones). The story generator [journey-story.ts:66-85] picks one of **18 archetypes** by weight (Quest 10; Wandering, Caravan, Military campaign, Pilgrimage 4; Mercenary contract, Courier ride, Expedition, Refugee flight, Treasure hunt, Monster hunt 3; Embassy, Exile's flight, Smuggling run, Raid, Royal progress, Airship voyage, Arcane errand 2) [src/data/journey-lore.ts:595-961]; each archetype carries domain weights (land/water/air), transport preferences, off-road/camp/rest probabilities and naming templates. Origin: population-weighted (+1), ×2.5 capital, port bonus scaled by the archetype's sea share, penalty for lone-island burgs; top 8 then random [journey-story.ts:121-146]. Legs: 1-6 by weight, candidates scored by log population, capital +0.8, foreign state +0.8, port/sea fit, within a distance band of 8-40% of the map diagonal [149-188]; each leg is routed in the archetype's rolled domain order, water legs needing ports at both ends [195-212]; segments are built with optional muster, harbour wait, mid-leg camp (split at 35-65%) and stopovers, max 15 segments [290-365]; names are phrase templates over lore pools with terrain words by height and biome [419-501]. **Pathfinding** [journeys-generator.ts:442-529]: land legs first try a BFS over the existing route network (rejecting sea links), else A* over cells with cost = distance × terrain (biome cost/50 clamped ≥ 1 × height factor 1 + max(h − 25, 0)/25) × 0.5 on routes (or ×5 when avoiding roads) and an admissible heuristic scaled by 0.5 [477-513, 531-577, 669-675]; water legs reuse `Routes.findWaterPath` and the meandered geometry [515-529]; air/stay legs are straight lines. Travel time = distance/speed with a per-transport "hours per day" converting travel hours into calendar days; off-road halves speed [280-346]. **Transports**: 20 defaults in four domains (land, water, air, stay) with speed km/h and hours/day [src/generators/transports-generator.ts:30-51]. The wiki describes the user-facing model [STATED, fmg-wiki/Journeys.md:20-38, 66-96].

### 3.14 Markers — PHYSICAL candidates, political/economic colouring

[OBSERVED, src/generators/markers-generator.ts:299-668]. **37 marker types**, each with {icon, min candidates, each (place one per `each` candidates), multiplier, list(pack) predicate, add(marker, cell) legend writer}; quantity = ceil(candidates/each × multiplier), 0 when candidates < min/multiplier, capped by candidate count; candidates are drawn at random and a cell is used once; a marker on a burg cell takes the burg's position [674-727]. Fantasy-only types (portals, rifts, disturbed burials) have multiplier 0 unless the culture set name contains "Fantasy" [300, 613, 623, 631]. Predicates (selection) [729-1389]: volcanoes h ≥ 70; hot springs h > 50 and cultured; water sources h > 30 on a river; **mines only at burgs whose last production run extracted an ore/mineral good** [778-806; test markers-generator.test.ts:93-121]; bridges at burgs with pop > 20 on an above-mean-flux river not on the coast; inns at populated crossroads; lighthouses on harbour > 6 cells next to a routed water cell; waterfalls where a river drops below 40 from ≥ 50; battlefields inside states at 25 < h < 50 with pop > 2, dated by a state campaign; dungeons at pop < 3 with a Watabou One-Page-Dungeon iframe seeded by map seed + cell id [929-939]; lake monsters per freshwater lake; sea monsters on routed ocean cells; hill monsters/caves on populated h ≥ 50; sacred mountains on lonely peaks with a cultured neighbour; sacred forests/pineries/palm groves by biome with culture and religion present; brigands on road cells; pirates on routed water; statues 20 ≤ h < 40; ruins 20 ≤ h < 60 cultured; libraries at burgs with pop > 10; circuses on routed cultured land; jousts at burgs pop > 20; **fairs at small burgs naming their top produced goods** [1195-1227]; canoes on rivers; migrations at pop ≤ 2 by biome animal table; dances at burgs pop > 15; mirages in hot desert; portals at the first 10% of burgs; rifts at pop ≤ 3 habitable; disturbed burials pop > 2; necropolises pop < 2; encounters pop > 1 with a Deorum iframe online or a procedural legend offline [1364-1389]; a single "party" marker at a burg. **Inn fare** reads the local market's stocked/traded goods [850-880]. Lore pools live in src/data/markers.ts (550 lines of word lists; not reproduced). The wiki lists the criteria in prose [STATED, fmg-wiki/Markers.md:44-82] and the settings dialog multipliers [Markers.md:18-19].

### 3.15 Zones — POLITICAL/event overlays

[OBSERVED, src/generators/zones-generator.ts:32-44, 117-125]: eleven types with expected counts (invasion 2, rebels 1.5, proselytism 1.6, crusade 1.6, disease 1.4, disaster/eruption/fault/flood/tsunami 1, avalanche 0.8), each drawn as gauss(expected, expected/2, 0, 100) × a global modifier. Invasions start at a border cell of an *ongoing* campaign's defender and grow 5-30 cells mixing BFS/DFS [128-189]; rebels grow 10-30 cells from a state's border with a random neighbour [191-245]; proselytism grows an organized religion into a neighbouring faith's populated cells [247-291]; crusades cover all cells of a random heresy [293-314]; diseases spread from a burg along routes (cost 5 on a route, 100 off, budget 20-40) [316-377]; disasters from a burg with random costs [452-501]; eruptions grow from a volcano marker and rename its note to "Erupting" [503-536]; avalanches on routed cells h ≥ 70 [538-571]; faults on 50 < h < 70 avoiding rivers [573-606]; floods along a big river from a burg cell [608-656]; tsunamis along a sea coast [658-695]. Zones are the only generated layer that production reads back (zone multipliers) [production-generator.ts:779-786].

### 3.16 Determinism summary

[OBSERVED] The map seed seeds the global PRNG (`Math.random = aleaPRNG(seed)`) [src/components/seed.ts:20]; goods, markets, routes and provinces additionally re-seed a local Alea from the map seed on first generation (so they are reproducible independent of upstream RNG consumption) [goods-generator.ts:987; markets-generator.ts:45; routes-generator.ts:222; provinces-generator.ts:85-86]. Regeneration paths deliberately use fresh seeds (states `recreate`, provinces, goods/markets/routes regenerate) [states-generator.ts:192; provinces-generator.ts:85; goods-generator.ts:982; routes-generator.ts:216-218]. Religions, cultures, burgs, military, markers, zones and journeys consume the global PRNG in pipeline order, so any change upstream changes them [INFERRED]. The glossary warns that the seed is "Not an identity: regenerating with the same seed makes a different Map" because options and version also matter [STATED, docs/domain/glossary.md:14]. Goods distributions use `nth(n)` for *deterministic* thinning by cell id, as opposed to `random()` [STATED, Goods-spread-functions.md:20].

### 3.17 Creator's stated limitations and plans

- "The map is static. Over-time simulation is planned, but not yet implemented" [STATED, fmg-wiki/Knowledge Base.md:465].
- "The Generator doesn't simulate wars" beyond diplomacy relations and the battle simulator [STATED, Knowledge Base.md:661].
- The economy "is a single calculated cycle, not a running simulation" [STATED, Knowledge Base.md:761]; taxes PRD out-of-scope: recurring ticks, spending, import tariffs, form-name modifiers, a regenerate-production action [STATED, docs/prd/state-taxes-and-treasury.md:156-162]; production "is a single-pass simulation: there is no recurring tick… no carried-over inventory between runs" [STATED, docs/domain/production_schema.md:8].
- Routes: river routes are not visually distinct from sea routes, no flux threshold UI, no upstream/downstream cost asymmetry, no river-aware `Routes.connect` [STATED, docs/prd/navigable-river-routes.md:237-244]; meandering not persisted, no per-river meander factor [docs/prd/meandering-river-routes.md:237-243].
- Pipeline: no declared dependency graph by design (the sequence "has never been anything but a straight line"); the Keep/Risk/Resample paths rebuild slices by hand and "can silently fall behind" [STATED, docs/architecture/generation-pipeline.md:48-50, 177-178].
- Emblems: no heraldry editor inside FMG (Armoria stays the editor), no custom charges for the generator [STATED, docs/prd/emblems-icon-library.md:289-293].
- Markers: "Criteria and types of randomly added markers are defined in the generator and can change between versions" [STATED, fmg-wiki/Markers.md:45].
- Localisation is "planned, but not ready… not a current priority" [STATED, Knowledge Base.md:201]. A separate "Medieval Dynasty simulator" meta-project is "on hold" [STATED, fmg-wiki/Q&A.md:72-73].

## 4. The burg → city-generator handoff in full

### 4.1 Which generator a burg goes to

[OBSERVED, src/generators/burgs-generator.ts:770-782] `getPreview(burg)` returns a custom `burg.link` if set; otherwise it looks up the burg's group's `preview` key and dispatches to one of three builders: `watabou-city` (Medieval Fantasy City Generator), `watabou-village` (Village Generator), `watabou-dwelling` (Dwellings). Default groups map capital, city and town → `watabou-city`; village and hamlet → `watabou-village`; fort, monastery, caravanserai and trading_post have no preview [435-506]. The Burg Group editor exposes the three options [OBSERVED, src/controllers/burg-group-editor.ts:128-130]. The Burg Editor embeds the preview URL (with `&preview=1`) in a sandboxed iframe and supersamples canvas-backed Watabou frames [OBSERVED, src/controllers/burg-editor.ts:624-648]; "Open" launches the full link [651-656]. The wiki confirms: "a live preview generated by Watabou's generators: the City generator for cities, the Village generator for smaller settlements and Dwellings for the smallest ones" [STATED, fmg-wiki/Knowledge Base.md:885].

### 4.2 City Generator link (`createWatabouCityLinks`) [OBSERVED, burgs-generator.ts:607-673]

Base URL `https://watabou.github.io/city-generator/` with query parameters, in this order:

| Parameter | Value and derivation | Source lines |
|---|---|---|
| `name` | burg name (empty string if none) | 652 |
| `population` | round(population points × `units.population.scale` × `urbanization.rate`) — absolute people | 617-619, 653 |

| `seed` | `burg.MFCG` if the burg came back from MFCG, else map seed + burg id zero-padded to 4 digits (so the seed is 13 characters and round-trippable) | 610, 655 |

| `coast` | 1 when the burg is a port *and* has a haven (an inland river port is not coastal) | 621, 657 |
| `farms` | 1 when the cell biome is arable: biomes 1-8 if a river is present, else 5-8 (savanna/grassland/deserts need a river) | 636-637, 658 |
| `citadel` | burg.citadel (0/1) | 639, 659 |
| `urban_castle` | 1 when citadel and the burg id is even | 640, 660 |

### 4.4 Dwellings link (`createWatabouDwellingLinks`) [OBSERVED, burgs-generator.ts:744-768]

Base `https://watabou.github.io/dwellings/` with `pop`, empty `name`, the same `seed`, and `tags` by population: `large,tall` > 200; `large` > 100; `tall` > 50; `low` > 20; else `small`.

### 4.5 Return path from MFCG to FMG [OBSERVED]

- A URL with `?from=MFCG&seed=<13 chars>` regenerates the map from the first 9 characters and focuses the burg named by the last 4 digits [src/components/seed.ts:14-16; src/services/url-params.ts:77-85].
- Without a 13-character seed, `findBurgForMFCG` picks the burg best matching the referrer's `coast`/`port`/`river` flags (with fallbacks) and closest `size`, then **adopts every referrer parameter onto the burg** (name, size as population, seed as `MFCG`, shantytown as `shanty`, and any other key as a numeric field) and highlights the renamed label [url-params.ts:114-162].
- Older maps' `MFCG` numeric seeds are migrated into a stored `link` [src/services/io/auto-update.ts:1116-1123]. The data model documents `MFCG` and `link` [STATED, docs/architecture/data-model.md:161-162]. Changelog entries: "Integration with Medieval Fantasy City Generator" and later "Improved integration with MFCG" [STATED, fmg-wiki/Changelog.md:407, 303].

### 4.6 What SettlementForge should take from this

[INFERRED] The record FMG hands over is: identity (name, deterministic seed), scale (people and a density-derived size class), **water adjacency** (coast flag, sea direction, river flag distinguishing mouth vs. passing river, estuary/confluence/island/pond tags), **hinterland** (arable biome → farms; forest/desert/snow style), **connectivity** (hub/highway/dead end/isolated), and **institutions as booleans** (citadel, urban castle, walls, plaza/greens, temple, shanty). Everything except the booleans is computed from cell geography and routes at link time, not stored; the booleans are stored on the burg and are the only place FMG's political layer (theocracy → temple, market centre → plaza, capital → citadel/walls) leaks into the city map. A SettlementForge settlement-map record should carry the same geographic fields natively and let the host's institutions set the booleans.

## 5. Implications for a native SettlementForge world regime

Classification: PHYSICAL = map engine owns; POLITICAL/SIMULATION = SettlementForge host owns, map engine only materialises; SHARED = split. Verdicts: KEEP (port as is), GENERALIZE (keep the mechanism, parameterise the content), REJECT (do not port), REPLACE (host supplies).

| Layer | Class | Verdict | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cell suitability / rural population (3.1) | PHYSICAL | KEEP, GENERALIZE the weights | Pure geography; small, deterministic, and the root of all placement. Expose the coast-type table, flux weight and goods bonus as parameters; let the host override `pop` after its own demographics run (FMG itself exposes `setArea` for this) [population-generator.ts:90-116]. |
| Culture centres and spread (3.2) | SHARED | GENERALIZE mechanism, REPLACE content | The centre-scoring helpers (temperature target, biome affinity, coast affinity) and the typed cost surface are good geographic "where does a people fit" tools; the sets, names, shields, types and expansionism are host content. SettlementForge should pass cultures with a type-like cost profile and let the engine flood them. The 7 types are really terrain affinities and can become per-culture cost vectors. |
| Burg siting, spacing, ports, bank/edge shifts (3.3 siting) | PHYSICAL | KEEP | Suitability × noise + quadtree spacing + landmass-aware port promotion is exactly the physical half of settlement placement; the two-ports-per-water-body rule and lake-drain resolution are worth keeping verbatim. |
| Burg population, buildings, groups (3.3 specify) | SHARED | REPLACE population and buildings; GENERALIZE groups | Population is a thin formula over suitability and connectivity that a real simulator supersedes; walls/citadel/temple/shanty are probability tables keyed on population and theocracy — the host's institutions should set them. The percentile/feature/biome group classifier is a reasonable materialisation rule for icons/labels/previews. |
| States (3.4) | POLITICAL | REJECT creation/diplomacy/forms/campaigns; GENERALIZE expansion cost surface | FMG's states are a tableau (random relations, one-shot wars, form by area tier). SettlementForge owns factions; the engine should only grow a faction's territory from given seats with a terrain cost surface (culture match, population, biome, height/water, river, coast ring) and a budget, then normalise borders and compute poles/neighbours. |
| Provinces (3.5) | POLITICAL | GENERALIZE expansion, REPLACE naming/forms | Same split: the state-confined flood, wild-province fill, island/colony detection and shape justification are useful materialisation; the form tables and "New X" colony naming are content. |
| Routes (3.6) | PHYSICAL | KEEP | Urquhart over burgs per landmass + Dijkstra with habitability/height/existing-route/burg costs, river-course-constrained sea routes with haven exits, and meander splicing are the strongest physical mechanisms here. Keep the road/trail hierarchy (capitals first) as a parameter of which settlements are "primary". |
| Religions (3.7) | POLITICAL | REJECT generation; GENERALIZE gated flood | The host owns belief systems; keep only "flood a religion over cells gated by culture/state with route-discounted passage cost" if SettlementForge wants spatial faith maps. |
| Military + battle simulator (3.8) | SIMULATION | REJECT | Troop counts are a product of population, diplomacy and type matrices; the battle screen is an interactive dice game. SettlementForge's world-pulse should own forces; the only reusable idea is the quadtree platoon→regiment merge for *rendering* dispersed forces. |
| Names (3.9) | SHARED | GENERALIZE | The Markov generator is small and language-agnostic; name bases are content the host may supply (keep the 43-base shape {min, max, duplicates, samples}); the state-suffix rules are culture content. |
| Lore / calendar (3.10) | POLITICAL | REPLACE | The host has a world clock; FMG only needs a year and era string for notes. |
| Labels / added labels / notes / measurers (3.10) | PHYSICAL presentation | KEEP labels (poles of inaccessibility, group zoom bounds), KEEP notes as a per-entity field | Pure presentation; the entity-owned note design (no orphan notes) is a good pattern. |
| Emblems + Armoria (3.11) | SHARED | GENERALIZE | The heraldic generator with kinship/dominion inheritance is self-contained and fun; the host supplies the kinship graph (which burg belongs to which faction). The Armoria session protocol is reusable if an external editor is wanted. |
| Goods placement (3.12) | PHYSICAL | KEEP, GENERALIZE catalogue | The distribution-expression DSL over biome/height/temp/shore/feature/river with caps and deterministic `nth` thinning is a clean way to seed resources; the 71-good catalogue, recipes and demand coverage are economy content SettlementForge already owns. |
| Markets, production, trade, taxes (3.12) | SIMULATION | REJECT simulation; GENERALIZE market territories | SettlementForge owns the economy. Keep only the market-territory flood (cost by water, landmass, border) as a way to materialise catchment areas for the host's markets. The frozen single-cycle, 1000-worker cap and straight-line trade distance are explicit FMG simplifications [production-generator.ts:15; markets-generator.ts:436-451]. |
| Journeys / transports (3.13) | SHARED | GENERALIZE pathfinding and time model; REPLACE story | A* land routing with terrain + road discount, water routing via sea routes/navigable rivers, and the hours-per-day calendar conversion are generic travel tools; archetypes and lore are content. |
| Markers (3.14) | SHARED | GENERALIZE candidate predicates; REPLACE legends | Candidate selection is mostly geographic (height, river, coast, routes) and worth keeping as a POI sampler; political/economic ones (mines from production, fairs from produced goods, battlefields from campaigns, sacred sites from religion) should read the host's data. |
| Zones (3.15) | SIMULATION | REJECT | Event overlays drawn from FMG's own campaigns/religions; SettlementForge's world-pulse produces real events. Keep only the "grow N cells from a seed along routes/rivers/coast" growers as rendering helpers. |

Cross-cutting recommendations [INFERRED]:
1. Keep FMG's **two primitives** (spaced greedy placement; budgeted typed Dijkstra flood) as engine services with cost vectors supplied by the host instead of hard-coded culture-type tables.
2. Preserve FMG's **ordering constraint**: suitability → placement → routes → everything that reads connectivity. In SettlementForge, routes must exist before the host computes anything connectivity-dependent (FMG's burg population, inns, brigands, disease zones, journeys all read routes).
3. Treat `burg.port` as FMG now does — "the final reachable water body" — so river towns share a shipping network with the coast [docs/prd/navigable-river-routes.md:139-146].
4. Hand the settlement-map module a record shaped like 4.6: geography computed by the engine, institution booleans owned by the host.
