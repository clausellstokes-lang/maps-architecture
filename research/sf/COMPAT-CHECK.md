## In plain words

SettlementForge's town file (the "dossier") and our map's reader of it fit. Every top-level key our reader demands is one SettlementForge always writes — the two 41-key lists are identical — so no lawful town is turned away, and nothing crashes. Where the two sides disagree, it is in the small print: a field spelled differently than our design wrote it (`replacedNpc`, not `replacedBy`), a word our tables never listed (seven income words, thirty-seven government names, the worst crime rung `capture`), a unit read wrong (condition durations count quarter-weeks, not weeks), a shape read wrong (festival records carry a motif and a start week, not a type and a from–to window), and one new catalogue kind (metropolis housing) that our content table lacks. Every one of these is fixed on our side — a table row, a field name, a key list, a fallback — and none asks SettlementForge to change anything (J121). Until those fixes land, a lived town would read partly wrong (a monster-plagued town drawn as besieged; condition dates off by a factor of four; subsistence hamlets losing their largest income share); after them, the port reads SettlementForge at `00b648c9d` seamlessly.

Two things only the owner can settle: which SettlementForge commit the first adapter is cut against (the design pinned `5d699cc68`; the owner runs `00b648c9d`, and the two cannot be told apart by their version stamps), and whether the owner's SettlementForge runs its default week-by-week advance (if that switch is off, a "tick" is a month, and every date read from the dossier shifts).

## 1. Verdict

**Seamless after fixes.** No blocker stands against the pinned adapter. One blocker stands against an adapter cut at `00b648c9d` (P030: the new kind `housing_5000_structures` fails V11's catalogue-cover gate T-V11-2 until one row is added). 15 major and 13 minor mismatches and 4 absences with fallbacks, each with its fix below; 99 of 131 read paths match. Nothing refuses a lawful dossier; nothing needs SettlementForge to change.

Severity is graded by the coherence pass's standard (J141): **blocker** — the design cannot be built as written on a common path (a refusal of a lawful input, a wrong or impossible result, a red build gate); **major** — two builders would build different behaviour, or an input has no producer, or a test bar cannot pass or passes a wrong result (here: a dossier fact read into a wrong map outcome on a normal path, or a word every fixture carries that T73-10's totality test would redden); **minor** — nothing built or tested changes beyond a note, a label or a rarely reached path.
