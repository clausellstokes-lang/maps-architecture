**985. The savepoint of O(change) — §3.3 / §3.4: an undo log of the operation's own writes replaces the before-image for every op savepoint and dry run; the dry run becomes an operation that leaves no trace at O(what it wrote).**

**985 (a). What a savepoint and a dry run cost today.** The architecture has no sentence on the savepoint: `grep -i savepoint docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` finds none. Its current statement is the build record C-0185 (1), verbatim: "the batch's overlay is the arena itself over a **before-image** (`tx/savepoint.ts`: every table, the slot counters, the handle table, the version and the index's mutable state — grids, tombstone sets, grid shares, partition lists — copied when the batch opens and put back when it rejects, so a rejected batch leaves the arena at its version bit for bit, rule 8). The image costs a memory copy of the arena per batch (O(arena), charged to no work counter — work is the batch's operation count): the copy-on-write overlay with overlay-local slots and its merge are the derivation pool's (§3.3 steps 1–5, §4b's pool row, M1, `E_SCOPE` declared), which must replace this image before M1's per-block and per-plot batches (S10, S11) run at CITY — flagged to the session as a cost risk, PLAUSIBLE until M1 measures it." The code: `takeSavepoint` (`tx/savepoint.ts:160` `image`) copies every column of every table and `imageIndex` (`:52`) copies, per level, every dynamic-grid cell as a new array, the `large` set, the `entries` map, every tombstone set and `byKey`; `restoreIndex` (`:76`) rebuilds them all as new `Set`s and `Map`s. It is taken once at every batch's open (`tx/tx.ts:96`) and once at every op savepoint (`faces/common.ts:464` `opSave`, "O(arena), charged to no counter") — which the kerb read takes at its first insertion (`arr/kerb.ts:1401` `dryInsert`) and `splitFace` takes for its own atomicity. **985 (b). What they become.** Target: `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` §3.3, a new paragraph **"The savepoint and the dry run (O(change))"** after *Expectations* (insertion; no current text is replaced, because none exists — C-0185 (1)'s "put back when it rejects" is the only statement, and it stays true of the stage image). Proposed text:

> **The savepoint and the dry run (O(change)).** An open transaction keeps an **undo log** of its own writes: every write to an arena column (`vertices`, `halfEdges`, `roles`, `faces[l]`, `indexHome`, the handle table) records (column, slot, old value) once per slot per frame; the slot counters, `holesTop` and the version are saved by value at the frame's mark; every index write — the grid's `gridPut` / `gridTake`, a tombstone added, a `byKey` entry set — records what it replaced (the `IndexBatchLog` the batch already keeps, `index/spatial.ts:143`, extended by the old entry); the journals, the chain journal, the log and the warnings are cut to their marked lengths, as `rollback` cuts them today. A **frame** is a mark on that log; frames nest last-in first-out (the batch's frame, then an op's, then a dry run's inside it — the LIFO rule C-0299 already states for images). **Undo to a mark** replays the log backwards to it and costs O(entries since the mark): what the op wrote, never the arena. A **dry run** is an operation that opens a frame, writes through the ordinary doors, reads, and undoes to its mark: it leaves no trace — every column, counter, set and map as it was — and charges its own work on its own shell (as `insertTx` does today), never the batch's. The **stage image** the pipeline runner takes before a stage and puts back after (`takeSavepoint` / `restoreSavepoint`, `tx/index.ts:47`) stays an O(arena) image: it is taken once per stage, it is the oracle the undo log is tested against (below), and a restore across committed batches is what moves the restore generation (C-0299, C-0313) — an undo inside an open batch never moves the version and never touches the generation. A rejected batch undoes to its open frame; the result is what §2.2 rule 8 states: the arena at its version. Invariants kept: index freshness (§3.10) — a grid entry put back is the entry that was there, a tombstone removed is the one the frame added, so no query sees a stale or missing item; the debug build's slot stamps (`arena/debug.ts`) — the counters are restored before any slot the frame allocated could be read; the stage-end partitions — the rebuild moments are unchanged; determinism — an exact undo and an image restore are observationally identical (no output reads a slot number, §3.3), so the model root is the same by construction (NO-SHIFT). The API the kernel offers every stage: `mark(tx)` / `undoTo(tx, mark)` on the open transaction (the op savepoint `opSave` / `rollback` become these), and `dryRun(tx, fn)` — `fn` writes and reads on the open overlay and its every write is undone on return, its result and warnings handed back — the form the kerb service (§3.7), the multi-cut partitioners and an edit's preview read use.

> `kerb` is also a read-only service on the faces of an open transaction's overlay, served from **kerb state**: S8 plans every cut on `kerb(F)` of the current L1 faces, inserts each accepted lane at L1 in its own district transaction, and the kernel derives the two children's kerbs from the parent's at the cut; S9 then commits `corridorUnion` for those L1 faces from the same state — the same geometry the cuts were planned on — so the block S8 cut and the block S9 keeps are one face (tested, §13.2).
>
> **Kerb state and the derived kerb.** The kernel keeps, for each L1 face of an open transaction and of the committed arena, its **kerb state**: the rings of `kerb(F)` as §3.7 defines them, the spines they were read against with each spine's sampled half-widths, and the arena version they are current at. The state of a face no cut has touched is read once, by the construction above (the dry run of item 985, the offset parts laid and undone), and kept. **Under `splitFace`** the children's state is **derived**, never re-read: the lane is a new bounding spine of both children with the half-width its `way` row declares; `kerb(child) = (kerb(F) ∩ the child's side of the cut) − buffer(lane)` clipped to the child, with the lane's band ending at the crossed spines without a cap (a T-junction end carries no cap; the junction's `corridor_split` is S9's) — one exact clip of a lattice polygon by one strip, every output vertex a lattice point by §3.2's rounding of the intersection points (the same `round` and `square` rules as the construction). **Under `connectHole`** (a slit) the one child's kerb is the parent's less the slit's band. **Under `mergeFaces`** and **`removeEdges`** (a lane taken out again) the state of the faces beside the removed edges is dropped and the parent's state restored by the transaction's undo log (item 985: the state table is a logged column). **Under `splitThrough`** on a bounding spine (a T-junction from either side of a shared chain) the state is unchanged, because the new vertex carries no knot and changes no half-width (the sentence above on the T-junction S8 inserts); any other write to a face's boundary edges or to a bounding spine's width profile invalidates its state, which the next read rebuilds by the construction. **What the arrangement offers**: face state that follows a face through split, slit, merge and removal in the transaction's undo log, and an exact lattice clip of a ring by a strip (the boolean of §3.7 on two operands of which one is a single strip, computed on the rings' coordinates with `orient2d`, no arena written). **Correctness criterion**: on every face of the corpus, the derived kerb and the construction's read of the same face agree — exactly (equal canonical rings) where the construction's read was unattracted, and within N-43's bound (`DRY_MOVE_CM` = 2 · √2 · `attractCm` + sagitta) where attraction moved the construction's parts (KI-13's class (b)): the construction is the arena-attracted reading of §3.7's definition, the derived kerb its exact geometry, and `corridorUnion`'s commit through `insertChain` is what attracts the committed block within the same bound. **What S9 gains**: `corridorUnion` reads each committed L1 face's kerb from its state — O(1) per face, where P13 item 4 found S9 re-reading every block's kerb as a fresh arrangement (1 412 / 1 213 arrangements per seed) — and commits it once, as today.
