## The host-change list, by milestone

SettlementForge (the host) makes thirty changes, H1 to H30, in seven groups, and builds them in the numbered order (V13 §8.5, lines 3704–3705). One rule binds every row: a change starts switched off and switches on only when its data exists; absent data leaves the host's output byte-for-byte as before (V13 §8.5, lines 3705–3706). This is the design's only host-change list; the realm review's list and the proposal's older items are replaced by it (V13 §8.5, line 3704).

A few words used below, in plain terms. The *fork* is the host's own copy of the old map generator (V13 §8.5, H1, line 3726). The *canon* is the map's frozen geography, handed to the host once and only changed by a receipted *re-canonize* (V13 §11 Q4, lines 4288–4291). The *port* is the message channel between the map and the host (V13 §8.5, H4, line 3729). A *seat* is a settlement as the map sees it; an *advance* is one simulation time step; a *window* is the packet of facts the host sends after each advance (V13 §8.5, H9, line 3734). A *lens* is a themed map view (plague, sway, trade and so on), and the *DM* is the game master running a campaign (V13 §8.5, H23, line 3748).

Every row also carries, in the host's own files, its tier line, its degraded arm (what happens when data or hardware is missing) and its coupling row; its first-visible line; measured spreads or the word UNMEASURED for every numeric band it creates; and its felt sentence, what the player notices (V13 §8.5, lines 3705–3708). Every row lands as a lazy leaf (code loaded only when first needed) under the host's first-paint ratchet, the rule that the first screen may never load slower (V13 §8.5, line 3708).

| # | What the host changes (plain words, then the host program or law) | Why the map needs it | Waits on | Kind |
|---|---|---|---|---|

| Phase 1 | H18: freeze every saved map's capture with its export (line 3743). | Every old map readable by the import before the fork goes. | M-W3; H1. | Transition (line 3743) |
| Phases 2–3 | Nothing in the host; these phases are the map's. The map imports only on request, never on load; a legacy campaign's world view writes nothing to its simulated state, its overlays converted through the stored capture and the import's transform (lines 3757–3758). | An old campaign can be shown on the map without its simulation ever changing. | H18's stored captures. | Map's own work (lines 3757–3758) |
