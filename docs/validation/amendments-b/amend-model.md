(`ElaborationChunk`), `derived.elaborations`, the `elaborate` view key. §2.2 rule 7 (with item 29): the `entrance` role
indexed.

**Mechanism in plain words.** A building's floor plan is the fourth step of its ladder — outline, wings, rooms, plan. It
is never invented from a random shape: it starts from the walls, storeys, doors and towers the building already has and
from what the building is for and who lives in it, writes the list of rooms it needs (sized in real square metres, with
the rules each room carries), places the stair first so it runs straight up through every storey, and only then cuts each
wing of each storey into rooms to fit that list, on a scratch drawing the kernel throws away, so the plan never touches
the map. Chimneys stand over hearths, dormers over attic rooms, the porch over the front door, and every storey's outer
walls are exactly the wings seen from above. A plan is drawn when the user clicks a building (or automatically when the
viewer chooses so), kept as a cache that can always be thrown away because the same seed always gives the same plan,
marked "changed" when the simulator alters an input, and re-derived on the next click as a small change of the earlier
plan, never a re-roll. Re-rolling the walls never changes the outside; re-rolling the room names never moves a wall.

**Rulings taken.** J-A28.21 (records; rooms as values; module and scale; four lod values), J-A28.22 (the record as a
sixth non-canonical part outside both digests, `elaborate` a view key — the synthesis's placement inside the records

### Item 28 (f) — the interior has no floor (HOME §2.6; §2.7 `Household`, §2.8 the record's inputs)

**Where it landed.** §2.6 "The interior ladder": the programme's inputs (the plot's field band and value, the household
count), the rule table by band name (six default rows: unit form, households per shell clamped into the capacity, the
unit's rooms, shared rooms, staff rooms with their size share and placement, circulation, room-size factor), the dwelling
unit (`Room.unit`, `Plan.units`, `UnitForm`, the unit-door rule), ONE capacity function in exact integers, the never-omit
rule, the re-cut both ways with its fixed set, kept-room rule and the canonical chain (rows first, then households, from
the as-built plan), staff and shared rooms, editability, the §13.2 bars. §2.6 `Building.households`; §2.7 `Household.building`'s
comment names its unit and the field band; §2.8's elaboration record lists the band and value and the households among the
plan's inputs; §2.9's cache memoises chain steps.

**Mechanism in plain words.** The shell of a house obeys item 31's floor — it never looks poorer than the town's middle
class once built that well — but its rooms follow the people in it. The plan reads who lives there (the street's field
band) and how many households the roster put there, and cuts the inside for them: one family with small staff rooms in
the attic when rich; two families with a kitchen and parlour each at the middle; a lodging house with a stair hall that
gives every family its own door, a shared kitchen and a common room when middling-poor; one-family apartments of a
bedroom and a cooking place off a corridor when poor; single rooms off the corridor with no cooking place when destitute.
How many families a shell can take is one number computed one way for everyone — from what the shell was built for and
its floor area — so a house built rich holds the most families when let out. The re-cut keeps the outer walls, the stair,
the doors and the chimneys and re-draws only partitions, corridors and doors, with the least change; it runs both ways,
re-uniting the families restores the earlier plan exactly, no family is ever left without a room of its own with its own
door, and the same occupants always give the same plan whatever the order of clicks.

**Rulings taken** (the route's eight, in order): 1 the owner's last sentence read as the shell's CAPACITY — J-A28.23 (the

### Item 29 — entrances and basements (HOME §2.6; §2.2 rule 7, §2.5, §2.3)

**Where it landed.** §2.6: `Building.entrances: Entrance[]` replaces `entrance`; `Entrance` and `Basement` records;
`Heights.floorsBelow`; `Building.basement`; the paragraph "Entrances" (one derivation function `deriveEntrances`; the five
rules; the parity default; §5.3's rows) and "Basements" (the class rows, the prosperity and size terms, the terrain gate
with the water table defined from §2.3's facts, half and walk-out cellars, the inset ring, storeys, access, edits, the
sewer rule with J-A12.42, the emulation note). §2.2 rule 7: `entrance` indexed. §2.5: `BuildingSlot.entranceOn`'s meaning
widened to the set (the frontage for a front slot, the court or path for a set-back one). §2.3: the facts the gate reads
named where they live.

**Mechanism in plain words.** Every building has exactly one front door on the street (or on the court a set-back house
faces, which itself has a street gate). A side or back door appears only where a lane, alley or passage actually touches
the plot, with a class-by-class chance and a cap of one each for ordinary houses; a kitchen door onto the yard is the
default when the yard is big enough. Each door is a named point on the wall that survives every move and edit, changes
when the building changes its trade, and becomes a real door in the floor plan, the front one the root of the circulation.
A cellar is decided, not rolled: inns, breweries, manors and temples tend to have one, hovels never; wealth, size and
height raise the chance; wet or flood-prone ground forbids it, low ground allows only a half-cellar, a slope makes a
walk-out, rock makes it costlier; it lies inside and smaller than the ground floor, has its own storeys, and is reached by
the main stair, a trapdoor or an outside stair. It never becomes a room of the sewers; a stair down to a tunnel is a
separate access record.

**Rulings taken.** J-A29.21 (the entrance set, five rules, one function; the caps per class from the template's
`entrances` columns), J-A29.22 (the basement record and `floorsBelow`; the water table from §2.3's existing facts — no new
raster; never a sewer room). The route's three: the water-table definition (J-A29.22); `Heights` versus a separate record
(a separate `Basement` record plus `floorsBelow` — J-A29.22); the caps (the template row's `capSide` / `capRear`, one
each for ordinary classes, institutions more through `service` — J-A29.21).
