**Vetoable deviations from the verifiers' wording:**

1. **§8.4 (VR2-01).** The verifier kept "casts its span mark … a prism from its wing's `baseCm`". This block reads
   "casts its span marks … each a prism from its wing's `baseCm`" instead. A stepped front has several marks, and "its
   wing's" needs one mark as its antecedent.
2. **§8.3 and §11.1 (VR2-01 sweep, not asked for).** The validator named only §8.5 and §8.4. However, the passage
   census row ("its span ring") and the glTF and CityJSON rows ("over its span ring from `headroomCm` above its base to
   the eave"; "the span ring's prism") still said one ring per passage and used the building's base and eave. That
   leaves the same count and heights undetermined for a stepped front. Rule (1) requires one covering that the mass, the
   shadows and the 3-D export all read, so the rows were brought into line. J-A30.87 was restated for the same reason,
   because its index row still said "the passage's span ring under the base wing that spans it".
3. **J-A30.87's Part A entry** adds the reason one mark per passage was rejected. `Building.heights` is the maximum over
   the building's wings (§2.6), so a mark at the building's base and eave would set a lower quad's span above its own
   arch and up to another quad's eave.
4. **§8.5 (2), the span mark's height (not asked for).** The verifier wrote "its height that wing's eave". Every
   height in §2.6's `Heights` is measured above its own `baseCm`, and §8.1's `heightCm` and `baseCm` columns mirror that
   convention. Read that way, a mark whose base is raised by the headroom and whose height is the eave would top out
   one headroom above the wing's eave. That contradicts §8.4's "to that wing's eave" and §11.1's
   extrusion "to its eave". The block therefore states the column exactly: "its `heightCm` column that wing's `eaveCm`
   less that `headroomCm`, so the mark's top is that wing's eave". The trailing clause states the intended geometry, so
   an implementer who reads `heightCm` any other way still sees where the top goes.
