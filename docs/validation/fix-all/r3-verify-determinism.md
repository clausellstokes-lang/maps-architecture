## 0. In plain words

This seat re-checked, after the third fix-all round was merged, the part of the design that makes the same seed give
the same town, keeps edits safe and handles failures. **All seventeen items handed to this area are now answered by a
concrete mechanism in the text**: moving a river now prepares its bridges and cuts its streets before the water goes
in; regenerating a district at the edge of town rebuilds its own side of the border strip exactly; a river's bend
points can be dragged and found again after an update; fountains and moved trees travel with a warped square; harbour
moles pass the "roads cross water only on bridges" rule; undo leaves the town's fingerprint exactly as if the step had
never happened and survives closing the file; a user's own name list travels as a setting pack; a rebuilt bridge
keeps its name; and a saved town can be edited the moment it opens. Every earlier fix in these sections still holds,
and no sentence here postpones anything.

The check found **eight new problems**, two of them serious. **First, opening a saved town checks the wrong thing.**
The file's copy of the town is shown and edited at once, while a background replay checks the town's fingerprint —
but the check compares the replay with the fingerprint written in the file, never with the copy actually being edited,
so a damaged or altered copy whose recorded fingerprint was left alone passes as "verified". The fix: fingerprint the
opened copy too, and trust it only when both agree. **Second, choosing your own name list (or editing the naming rules)
changes no existing name,** because the new rule that names follow the thing they belong to also blocks renaming when
the naming inputs change; the fix is to re-draw the unedited names whose naming inputs changed and list them for
confirmation. The six smaller ones: a name list selected after the town was made can slip into a shared link or be
dropped from the file after being deselected; in a shared town the owner's "keep the saved town" leaves the other
players on a different town; background checking and a big edit can need more memory than counted; one rule's list
of allowed exceptions leaves out canals; one programming interface cannot express "keep the saved town"; and one list
of re-rollable settings is typed more loosely than the other three.
