## 0. In plain words

The kernel is the part of the design that does the shape mathematics. All eight kernel items of round 4 are genuinely
fixed: the points where a new canal or a moved river meets a town wall now have exact, permanent names, and the
temporary names used at other crossing points can no longer clash; removing a street now puts back every point the
street had borrowed from its neighbour; the points one drag of the warp brush moves are placed in a fixed order; every
hand-over of the town to the helper workers first tidies the search index, also after edits; the helpers' copies are
counted with their search index; three wording slips are gone; part of a street or wall can be named and removed on
its own; and the rule about how far a hidden point may stand from its line is now stated as what the kernel can keep.

Looking for new problems found six small ones, none serious and none a deferral. A bridge built with the town keeps its
"ground level" tag for a moment after the river moves away from it, although three sentences and one test say it loses
it at once. The old, withdrawn rule about hidden points is still written in three places. In about one case in 170
where two street changes touch the same stretch, removing a street fails to hand a borrowed point back, because the test for handing it back is stricter than
the kernel's own tolerance. A water gate made with the town and one made by an edit sit at different levels and behave
differently under the warp brush. The search index can briefly answer with a point's old level after that point changes
level. And three sentences are inexact (the map's frame across new water is a stretch, not always one straight line;
who numbers a temporary crossing name; which saved chunks a coarse edit rewrites). Each carries a concrete change.
