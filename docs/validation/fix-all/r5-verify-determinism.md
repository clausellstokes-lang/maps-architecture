## 0. In plain words

This seat re-checked, after the fifth fix-all round was merged, the part of the design that makes the same seed give the
same town, keeps edits safe and handles failures. **All eleven items handed to this area are now answered by a concrete
mechanism in the text.** The serious one is closed: a saved town's fingerprint is now checked only by the program
version that made it. So an old town is shown for viewing without a false warning that it is damaged. A town kept
from its saved copy stays editable after an upgrade, because the upgrade — the one moment both versions are loaded —
checks it with the old version and records its new fingerprint with the new one. The ten smaller ones:
- On a slow machine, "trust the saved town" and saving wait for the fingerprint check.
- A name you renamed or locked keeps the record of which list it came from, and it is offered its list's new name when
  you undo or unlock.
- The renames you confirm are stored with the edit.
- A street list chosen for one culture renames only its streets.
- The saved records that steer faithful regeneration and renaming have their own fingerprint.
- A name list you can still redo stays in the file.
- A town kept from its saved copy is shared as its file, never as a link.
- An upgrade saves a setting pack the new version stops shipping.
- One test line spells a water setting the one way.
- A rebase reason nothing could produce is gone.

Every earlier fix in these sections still holds, and no sentence here postpones anything.

The check found **seven new problems, all small**. The first three:
- After an upgrade, every later opening of the town would repeat a warning that the renames differ from what was
  confirmed, for edits made before the upgrade, because those edits are replayed with the new version's rules.
- When an upgrade cannot fit a no-longer-shipped setting pack inside the file's size cap, it offers to remove the pack.
  That cannot help, because a removed pack stays in the town's history.
- A town imported from a very old version keeps name records that point at old naming rules. Its names would then
  change here and there during unrelated edits, instead of once, with confirmation, at the import.

The other four:
- The new records fingerprint does not say where street names belong or what happens when a block moves to another
  district. As written, that would raise a false warning that the town is damaged.
- An adjustment made while a town is still being checked writes its records differently from a later replay. That
  also raises a false warning, and no test compares the records.
- One sentence in the naming section still states the old rule for keeping a name list in the file.
- The build plan offers keeping the saved town one milestone before it stops such towns being shared as links.
