### PRB7F-GEN-01 — MAJOR · (a), (c) — law 1 (9) and (10) find a commit's lines by date, and *Order* dates a castle's walls by another commit's step

7755–7762). Three readings of "its `laidAt` that commit's": (i) the commit's own steps — at S7's end the curtain's

## Part 2 — J149's forms at S6 and under a regeneration

### PRB7F-GEN-03 — MAJOR · (b), (a) — law 1 (2) picks over or merged by where a support stands, not by the aqueduct's level

**Law or term.** Law 1 (2): "crosses it at the aqueduct's level, in one of two forms read from what stands …: **over**,
where no standing support stands in the band there and every standing span above the band clears the wall's top, the
band passing beneath the arch; else **merged**, the band built up to and around the aqueduct's arch or pier there at the
wall's own top, the water passing through it in the aqueduct's channel" (adj l. 401–406); correction 3 ("a support is kept
off by nothing (law 1 (2), (3): … an aqueduct's merged; J149)", adj l. 587–589) and correction 7 ("Every example that keeps
an aqueduct's supports off a wall's band (*the edge*, a support slid off the band, …) … reads law 1 (2)", adj l. 610–615);
*J149 applied*: "**Merged** is written because, with over, it makes every crossing of an aqueduct's deck line lawful, so
no op bends, slides, waits or is refused for one" (adj l. 3158–3164); the support step's (3), "an enceinte's band
(`Enceinte.band`, …), whatever its runs' condition" among the obstacles a station slides off (base l. 2534–2543), (M5)
keeping it (adj l. 1263–1266). The owner's rule, PROGRESS J149 (l. 208): "an aqueduct crosses a wall at its own level, in
one of three forms — OVER: its channel above the wall's top, it passes on an arch that clears the wall, …; MERGED: its
channel within the wall's height, the wall is built up to and around the aqueduct's own arch or pier at the crossing and
the water passes through that arch …", given in answer to the program's proposal, which said "an aqueduct's level is fixed
by its fall from its source, so its level at the wall picks the form" (the program's words); and the program's recorded
reading, "a wall laid later than a
standing aqueduct takes the same form at the aqueduct's level (built around its arch, or passing beneath an arch that
clears it)".

**Case.** (i) S6's aqueduct at a first generation, its channel well above a CITY ring's top (an arcade of the common
height across a town wall), its pitch (base l. 2531–2533) putting a station in the band at one crossing of the ring and an

pulled trace (E-P9's impassables, adj l. 1981–1984) crossing its deck line at a pier. (iii) Either with a ring the user
shape-locked (law 1 (10): "no such aqueduct merged into it").

**Claim.** (b): J149's rule makes the level the deciding variable — an aqueduct "crosses a wall at its own level", OVER
for a channel above the wall's top ("passes on an arch that clears the wall"), MERGED for a channel within its height —,
as the program's proposal it answered put it ("its level at the wall picks the form"). Law 1 (2) decides the form by the supports: any standing support in the band makes the
crossing **merged**, whatever the level. So in (i) a channel above the wall's top crosses the ring over at one crossing
and merged at the other — the same aqueduct, the same level —, and at the pier the law calls "the water passing through
it" a crossing where the water passes over the wall; in (ii) the later wall is "built up to and around" a pier, where the
program's own reading of J149 has a later wall at a high aqueduct "passing beneath an arch that clears it". The pier-in-
band crossing of a high aqueduct is none of the owner's three forms (no arch clears the wall there; the channel is not
within its height), and law 1 (2) files it under the form whose defining condition it fails. Nothing forces it: J149's
"Nothing hard moves (J148)" cites J148, whose tier text (the program's) has the newer Tier-2 structure bend itself
locally (adj l. 341–342), so — on this probe's reading (J122), never the owner's — a newer aqueduct's station may slide

the band among its obstacles, while corrections 3 and 7 read "a support slid off the band" as law 1 (2) and *J149

## Verdict

Run as one resolver along the pipeline, the laws as J148, J149 and pass 5 leave them decide most of this lens uniquely:
one order of steps with stage-distinct dates, law 1 (3) read from stored fields, beyond the foot as a region with its
first point, the gate routes' re-solve, S4's and S5b's decks coming down, the castle's siting, the fixed places along
their ties, the S5a pins and the rung's reach, every Tier-3 move, three raised ways at one crossing, the creation
prefix, the chamber's timing and every law 2 (c) outcome with its rung and key. The tiers removed every case that
followed a moved gate, and the over form removed the gate and tower bends at S6. They are not yet complete and
determinate. No blocker remains, but five majors do: law 1 (9) and (10) find a commit's lines by a date that, on a
generation event of many commits, belongs to another commit — a castle's walls are never "later" while they stand
(GEN-01); the check queries only touched parts, so a regeneration's raised ways across a ring the user locked are never
checked (GEN-02, an invariant unchecked); law 1 (2) decides over and merged by where a support falls rather than by the
aqueduct's level, against J149's rule that the level picks the form (GEN-03, an owner rule crossed on one reading, and a
slide left unsaid); *Not yet built* puts a ring's turn-vertex towers on both sides of J148's line between a Tier-1 thing
read and a part a later step lays (GEN-04); and *Carried way*'s forced pick and pin leave a kept carried way's gate with
no place and no count (GEN-05). Each closes by one sentence in Part L, by class — one meaning of "a line a commit lays",
one query side, one form per level, one side for S7's towers, one gate rule for a kept way — and four minors correct
examples to the laws (GEN-06 – GEN-09). In all, 5 majors (0 blockers) remain in the generation and regeneration lens; the
laws should take these refinements before the family's repair seats write against them.
