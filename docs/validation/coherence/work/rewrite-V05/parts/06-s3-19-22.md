
### 3.19 The island's shade strip (item 102 on the island's side; J98's underside)

`islandShadeStrip(doc, island, samples, onto) → IslandShadeStrip` (`@plat/sky`, V05 §2.3a) — a pure function, no draw:

1. **The samples.** §4.5's model sampler — its sun positions at the site latitude on the equinox, its sample points per
   32 m field cell, its point-major layout, its reach cap of 8 × the caster's height shared by every model-side source,
   and its digest — read as it stands (J-V5.42, J-V5.44). Each sun gives the horizontal unit vector ŝ toward the sun in
   the model frame (x east, y north) and the shadow reach `c = min(cot altitude, 8)`. Below, "sample `s`" is one sun and
   "the cell's point" one of the sampler's points. Wherever a reader counts "the samples a cell is blocked for" (the
   clerk, the shade map's three areas, the CSV, T-V5-19), it takes the mean over the cell's points of the suns blocked
   at each — with one point, the count itself.
2. **The solid — from the caster's `shadeBasis`, never the live island** (J-V5.47). The tiers without roughness —
   `undersideTiers(basis, { rough: false })`, the view's own function (V05 §3.9 (2)) evaluated on the basis's rim polygon,
   underside, anchor and elevation with the roughness term at 0, so the model never reads view data (J-V5.30); an island
   without a basis has no solid here and casts nothing: tier 0 the rim from the basis's `surfaceTopCm` (V05 §3.9 (4)) down
   to `elevation − rim`, tiers 1 … n down to the apex (none for `flat`). Each tier's ring is rasterised once into a bit
   mask of `shade.ringMaskM` cells in the island's local frame (scanline fill; a cell is in when its centre is), with the
   tier's span [`b_i`, `t_i`] in centimetres above the ground datum.
3. **The receivers.** Onto the ground: the field raster's 32 m cells (§4.5), each at each of the sampler's points `x` in
   it and the ground's height `g(x)` there (bilinear on the committed terrain raster **as the field reads it — S1's
   heights with the T2 terrain strokes, unlevelled**; item 110 (a)'s levelling is a platform datum, never a receiver
   height (§4.7), so a strip computed at S10a, before S10 and S11 level anything, equals the one a load rebuilds after
   them; J-V5.65). Onto an island: the receiving island's 32 m lattice over its land face at the receiver's own
   `shadeBasis` (its anchor and elevation), each at the island's terrain height there plus that datum — a receiver
   without a basis receives no strip; only an island whose apex lies above the receiver's surface top casts onto it — an
   island never shades upward.
4. **The test.** Point `x` is blocked at sample `s` when the ray from (`x`, `g`) toward the sun meets a tier. For each
   tier with `t_i` > `g` (one wholly below the receiver blocks nothing), in order from tier 0 down: the ray's horizontal
   track across the tier's span runs from `x + (max(b_i, g) − g) · c · ŝ` to `x + (t_i − g) · c · ŝ`; it is walked in
   steps of at most `shade.stepM`, both ends included, each point moved into the island's frame (less `anchor`) and looked
   up in the tier's mask; the first hit sets bit (point · suns + `s`) and ends that point's test for that sample. All
   arithmetic is integer centimetres and `dmath` (§6.5), in a fixed order: samples in the sampler's order, cells
   row-major, points in the sampler's order, tiers 0 → n.
5. **The window.** Only cells that can be blocked are tested, sample by sample. The corridor is the solid's bounding box
   (the union of its tier rings) swept away from the sun by `ŝ · c` × [`b_n` − `g_max`, `t_0` − `g_min`] (`g_min`, `g_max`
   the terrain's extremes over the frame — a safe bound), in receiver cells, clipped to the receiver raster
   (F-SKY-SHADE). **Each corridor cell is first pre-tested in O(1) at its own height** `g(x)`: its ray's track across the
   solid's full span, from `x + (b_n − g) · c · ŝ` to `x + (t_0 − g) · c · ŝ`, must meet the solid's bounding box, else
   the cell is skipped without a tier walk — so in mountainous relief the low cells far from the island cost one
   segment–box test each, and no blockable cell is missed (the pre-test is exact for the box, which contains every tier;
   J-V5.59). The strip's stored `window` is the bounding box of the cells that passed the pre-test, over the samples.
6. **When — the shade evaluations, the one schedule** (J-V5.27, J-V5.71, J-V5.72). A **shade evaluation** first writes
   the `shadeBasis` of every island it covers from the island as it then stands (`by` naming the evaluation, `epoch` the
   document's current epoch), then computes every strip from the bases. There are four, and no other writer of a basis or
   a strip:
   (a) **`generation` / `rederive` — SKY's last step**, at generation and at any re-derivation that runs SKY: every live
   island's basis, then the strips onto the islands and `shadeSky`; the strips onto the ground follow at S10a from the
   same bases.
   (b) **`rederive` — S10a's start**, at a re-derivation starting after SKY and at or before S10a: before step (i) and
   every hook, the bases of every live island, then the strips onto the islands — never by pass 2, so no hook of S10a
   reads a strip built from the old bases.
   (c) **`epoch` — each epoch's S6 re-evaluation**, for every island not fallen or removed, from the island as it stands
   when the epoch begins (V05 §3.15).
   (d) **`update` — Update shade now** (V05 §4.2), for its one island.
   **The strips onto the ground are computed once per evaluation, at their first read** — volume 1's S9b at S10a's
   `afterDrivers` hook, between step (i) and field pass 2, where it is registered (V01 §3.5.5), else field pass 2 — by the
   one pure call, and **held** for pass 2's fold and every later reader of the same evaluation (the hood pins' shade gate
   and `DistrictContext.shade.islandShare`, V01 §3.8.2, §2.3): S9b's strips and pass 2's are the same held strips, bit for
   bit (T-V5-21 (h)). An island without a `shadeBasis` — raised or declared since the last evaluation — casts and
   receives no strip until the next evaluation writes one. The strips are computed whatever `w_sun` is (0 included) — the
   fold multiplies by the weight — so the shade view always reads held strips (V05 §5.1). **Never** by an edit, a scoped
   regeneration of an island or SKY-F: an island edit leaves every basis and every strip where they were until one of
   these evaluations, and on load or replay the strips are rebuilt from the persisted bases (§2.9). **A ground terrain
   edit** (T2 on the ground key, a terrain conversion of §4.11) moves the receiver, not the caster: over its effective
   scope it re-reads every strip's bits there from the unchanged bases, with the field it recomputes, so the session's
   strips always equal a load's rebuild (T-V5-21 (h)); item 110 (a)'s levelling moves no receiver at all.
7. **The fold into the field** (J-V5.38). For each ground cell, each bit of the daylight term's mask — pass 1's terrain
   and wall shade and, after an epoch, volume 6's building shade mask on the same layout (V06 §3.4 (6)) — is OR-ed with
   the same bit of every strip covering the cell, so a point shaded by a hill, a house and an island at the same sample
   counts once; the term's shade share is the mask's popcount ÷ `bits` (§4.5). The **island-free value** — the same
   composition with the strips left out, composed at an epoch's evaluation and never stored — is what the supply law reads
   at an epoch (V05 §3.15 (1)); at generation S7b (5) reads pass 1, which has no island bits by construction (item 102
   (c)). **On an island** the same fold makes `shadeSky`: the strips onto it OR-ed, sample by sample, with — after an
   epoch — volume 6's building shade mask run on the island's 32 m lattice, the island's own buildings casting onto its
   cells by the same law and cap (item 102 (b); J-V5.33; V06 §3.4 (8)).
8. **The basis.** Each strip records the §6.2 hash of the bases it read (V05 §2.3a). The shade map view (volume 9) and the
   clerk compare the caster's with the hash of the same fields read from the live island: equal — the field shows the
   island where it hangs; different — the field still reads it where it hung at the last evaluation, the view draws the
   current strip as pending (V05 §5.1; the one reader of the live island's shade) and the clerk answers "The ground
   beneath will catch up the next time the town changes over time", offering **Update shade now** (V05 §4.2, §4.5).
9. **What it amounts to** (the owner's "not a hard and fast rule"). The strip only sets bits; the weight is §4.5's
   `w_sun` (item 111 (c)'s climate cell sets it per climate row). With the sampler's default at latitude 50° and the
   equinox the samples stand at 6.4°, 18.7°, 29.5°, 37.2° and 40° (computed from the solar geometry): the five midday
   ones reach 1.2 to 1.8 times a caster's height, the mid-morning and mid-afternoon ones about 3 times, the first and last
   are capped at 8 times. So an island whose underside hangs 100–160 m above its ground blocks the cells just north of its
   footprint for about three to five of the nine samples and sweeps far to the west and east for one sample each at the
   day's ends: its under-hang is a crescent north of it (south of it in the southern hemisphere), lowered by a third to a
   half of item 102's all-day amount. A `root` underside lengthens the crescent under the low samples and a `flat` one
   shortens it — J98 carried into the model as it is into the picture (V05 §5.3). This paragraph is a derivation from the
   defaults, PLAUSIBLE until T-V5-19 measures it.

### 3.20 The siting rulebook on and across the island (items 103 and 104 where they reach islands)

The one placement solver (§4.7 S10b) places everything on an island, in the unit run, with item 103's rulebook as every
other district reads it; this volume adds only what the rulebook cannot know — that the island is reached by a ride, and
what an island never carries.

1. **The route distance** `skyRoute(doc, from, to) → { lengthCm: Cm; access: Id }` (`@plat/sky`; the implementer of
   §4.7's distances between arenas; J-V5.36). From a point `p` on island I to a class `c` on the ground: the least, over
   I's admitted accesses `a`, of `d_I(p, attach_a) + transferM + run_a + d_G(foot_a, c)` — `d_I(p, attach_a)` the island
   leg, the straight distance × the rung's detour factor (§4.7's `street` law for a point-to-point leg); `transferM` the
   route's allowance for the wait and the loading (`routing.transferM`); `run_a` the access's run (V05 §2.2); and
   `d_G(foot_a, c)` the base's mode distance from the foot to the class (§4.7 S10b's *Supply distances by mode*): the
   `street` distance (the ground's class distance raster for `c` read at the foot, × the detour factor) where the foot and
   the class stand on the same side of the town wall, else the `road` distance through the gate by which the class is
   reached. A class on the island itself is read on the island's own class distance rasters, which the unit run's S10b
   builds as the base's does. `access` is the access of the least sum (ties by access ordinal), which the clerk names
   (8). From a ground point `q` to a class on an island: the same sum taken the other way; inside the pooled S10b —
   before the island's own placement exists — the island's class stands at the island's land centroid
   (`d_I(attach_a, centroid)`), so the ground pool reads only what exists before it (§4.1's laziness contract); after
   generation (post-build requests, epochs) the island's placed plots are used. Between two islands: through the ground,
   both legs and two transfers. A `river` or `sea` supply edge never crosses arenas (its distance is ∞): the island's
   conjured river and spring pond carry no trade and **satisfy no NEEDS row** — a class needing running water, a river
   bank or a harbour finds none on an island (J-V5.23; V05 §3.5 (3) (a)).
2. **Admitted accesses.** An access in state `open` (its `unserved` reading included); never a `closed` or `ruined` one.
   A `secret` access admits only the pulls of classes whose clientele is clandestine (item 103 (a)); a `guarded` access
   only its institution's own supply edges and the law-and-enforcement classes' rows.
3. **Which families read it.** The route distance serves SUPPLY (the supply edges under the `street` and `road` modes),
   CLIENTELE (the island's `dist.market`, `dist.plaza` and `dist.gate`, V05 §3.11), COMPETITION and COVERAGE (the
   distance between members; the route to the ground passes every walking radius, which is why a walking-radius row
   counts the island as one unit, V05 §3.5 (3)), ALIGNMENT (law against crime, sacred against profane, elite against
   common: people walk and ride) and every signature pair of item 103 (b) whose two ends stand on different arenas. The
   nuisance families (EMITS against SENSITIVE-TO; upwind and downwind) read the straight distance in three dimensions —
   the horizontal distance and the elevation between the two points — since smoke and smell rise and noise carries
   through the air; upstream and downstream never cross arenas.
4. **What an island never carries** — the rows of `IslandRule.carry.never` (V05 §3.5 (3) (a)); what may split is (11)'s.
5. **The landings.** `sky_landing` and `sky_landing_top` are placed by their accesses (V05 §3.10), never by a pull; their
   siting facts (V05 §2.4) act on their neighbours through the families, so noise-sensitive homes keep a little away from
   a hoist house and the classes that serve the island gather near its landing yard by the route distance.
6. **Custom institutions** (item 104). A custom kind on an island reads its resolved profile as any class does; the
   island institution rows take it by its scene archetype (V05 §3.4). Nothing custom can stall the sky: an unplaceable
   preference is reported with the closest options, as item 104 (d) requires.
7. **The island's own placement is not the solver's** (J-V5.34). V05 §3.7's score places the island as one body at S7b
   (5), before any plot exists, so it reads no family; its purpose terms are the island-scale reading of those
   institutions' profiles, and volume 11 writes their values with the profile as their provenance.
8. **"Why is this here"** (item 103 (e); V05 §4.5): a use on an island names the rows that pulled it, and a pull that
   crossed arenas names the landing it went through and the route length ("near the mill below, by the east hoist, 340 m
   by route").
9. **Supply chains across the island** (item 104 (h); J-V5.39). An ACTIVE custom chain (item 104 (b)) — and, for a
   dossier-backed town, each of the dossier's `activeChains[]`, its processors resolved by kind family (item 105 (i)) — is
   placed as one connected thing by the base's chain rules; the island changes five things and nothing else.
   (a) **What goes up.** The sky fill never carries a chain's RESOURCE node — a resource sits at its terrain feature on
   the ground (item 104 (h) (1)); the island's conjured river and spring pond carry no trade (1) and are never a chain's
   feature — nor its PROCESSING nodes, whose demand stays on the ground with the chain's working steps (V05 §3.5 (3)). Its
   FINAL SELLER and its CONSUMERS follow V05 §3.5 (3)'s proportional share like any class. The one exception is the
   island's own institution (V05 §3.4): a custom kind carried as a `tower`, `palace` or `cathedral` that is also a chain
   node, at any step, stays on its island and keeps every link by the route.
   (b) **The links cross by the route.** Every link between a node on an island and a node on the ground is a supply edge
   of mode `street` measured by `skyRoute` (1) — so the chain's last ground node leans toward the landing foot whose route
   to the island's node is shortest, and the island's node toward that landing's top. Item 104 (h) (2)'s natures act on
   the route as on a street: a perishable link's short reach holds its ground end close to the landing; a bulky link's
   water and main-road preferences never cross arenas (a `river` or `sea` leg is ∞, (1)); a valuable link's security pull
   counts a `guarded` landing as a secure site and an `open` public one as none.
   (c) **Order.** Placement runs upstream first (item 104 (h) (4)). At generation the island's unit run reads a ground
   supplier at the block whose S10a allocation holds it (§4.7 S10b's *Supply distances by mode*), and the ground pool
   reads an island node at the island's land centroid (1); a post-build request and an epoch read the placed plots on both
   arenas. A chain's final seller on an island stands at a smaller route distance to its market than the chain's resource
   does, in at least the share the base's ground-only bar declares for the same chain (T-V5-24).
   (d) **Shape** (item 104 (h) (3) as revised by item 107 (e)). A chain whose in-town nodes lie on two arenas sits close at
   street scale on the ground and STRETCHES through its landing, the landing one step on its road; it is never read as
   close across the arenas. It is named a quarter only past item 72's quarter-identity threshold, or by the dossier or
   the author — and then on the ground (volume 1's reading); no quarter is ever assigned to it by rule.
   (e) **Endpoints, services, change, declarations.** An import or export endpoint's store stands at the ground's gate or
   quay facing the partner (item 104 (h) (6)), never at a landing. A custom service `providedBy` an island institution
   sits inside it on the island (item 104 (h) (5)). A chain that deactivates leaves its island nodes where they stand,
   their pulls removed; one that activates places its new nodes against the standing ones, upstream first, an island node
   by the solver's positive placement on the island's arena (§7.7's `placeAt`) — never a unit run (V01 §3.10.3 (6)), never
   by moving, resizing or re-deriving an island (item 104 (h) (7)). An author's map-side declaration of a custom kind onto
   an island (item 104 (a) (1)) is T6's `assign` with `criteria.sky` (V05 §4.2), keyed by the kind's `localUid`, and is
   held as every declaration is — it may place a processing node up, the author's word outranking (a), with the fate
   "this chain now crosses the hoist" listed at its dry run.
10. **The supply web across the arenas** (item 105 (a)–(e); J-V5.50). Every link binds, pulls and explains across an access
   exactly as on the ground, with the route as its distance and the access as the place where its end is felt:
   (a) **Binding.** Each consumer member binds to its NEAREST provider member of the good by the edge's mode (item 105
   (a), item 107 (c)), measured by `skyRoute` when the two stand on different arenas, ties by provider address.
   (b) **Both ends pulled, at the access.** An island consumer counts toward its ground provider's weighted centre (item
   105 (b)) at the **foot** of the access its route used, weighted as its link; a ground consumer bound to an island
   provider counts at that access's **top** — never at a world position beneath the island, where no goods go.
   (c) **Orientation, several outputs, hubs** (item 105 (b)–(e)). A class's orientation and its per-good pulls act across
   the arenas through the same substitution; a balanced class's "junction where its input and output routes meet" may be
   a landing — its foot on the ground, its top on the island — scored, never forced.
   (d) **Status.** A link of one of the dossier's `activeChains[]` that is `impaired` or `vulnerable` weakens by item 105
   (i)'s status table on the route as on a street (9).
11. **Split works and shops across the arenas** (item 105 (h); J-V5.51). A split class carried by V05 §3.5 (3) (c) is one
   item on two plots on two arenas: its works on the ground by its nuisance and input rows, its shop on the island by its
   output and clientele rows, the shop's `Use.branchOf` naming the works' use; the route between them is its own link.
   Every lifecycle path covers both, as item 105 (h) requires: creation by S10b as one item (the pooled S10b places the
   works, the island's unit run the shop, reading the works at its S10a block by (12) (a)); a state, closure or removal of
   the institution reaches both; a relocation moves each by its own rows; a fall or a delete of the island relocates the
   shop (the works stays); undo restores both; the author or the clerk may merge them back onto one plot by a map-side
   declaration (on the ground; up only by an author's held T6 declaration, with the fate listed); migration: an absent
   `branchOf` is no split. Every split across the arenas is reported.
12. **Order, the district term and spread, per arena** (items 105 (f), (g) and 107; J-V5.52).
   (a) **Order and relaxation.** At generation each arena runs item 105 (f)'s order and its one bounded relaxation on its
   own: the pooled S10b on the ground, the unit run's S10b on each island. Each reads **only the other arena's proxies** —
   the ground reads an island's classes at its land centroid ((1)), the island reads a ground provider at the block its
   S10a allocation holds ((9) (c)) — so neither order waits on the other and both relaxations are byte-stable whatever
   the worker's interleaving (T-V5-22 (j)). After generation, both read the placed plots on both arenas, the relaxation
   of one arena holding the other's placements fixed.
   (b) **The district term** (item 105 (g); J-V5.75). S7b (1)'s supply term reads a sky pseudo-district as one consumer
   location at its island's **land centroid's world point** (`anchor` + the outline's centroid, fixed by S7b (5)'s
   placement, V05 §3.7) (§4.5 S7b (1)) — the proxy the ground pool reads for an island's classes ((1)) — since the landing
   feet are sited only at SKY, after S7b. Its carried classes' links are scored there against the providers already
   allocated, by the centroid distance volume 1's χ̃ reads at S7c (V01 §3.2 (5)) — no route, since no access exists yet —
   at the term's low weight, never past J-A72.46's dispersal.
   (c) **Near enough** (item 107 (b)). A cross-arena link's satisfaction plateaus on the route length: full within the
   good's `comfortM` of the summed legs (`transferM` counted inside), decaying to 0 at `maxDistanceM`.
   (d) **Spread** (item 107 (a), (d), (f)). Coverage counts an island's districts as districts (V05 §3.5 (3); J-V5.37), and
   a spreading class's same-kind spacing applies inside each island district as inside a ground district; an everyday
   service's link to its supplier across the arenas keeps its generous `comfortM`, so an island apothecary is never pulled
   down to the herbalist below.
   (e) **Quarters never assigned** (item 107 (e)). No island district is made a quarter by rule. A dossier QUARTER record
   (item 108 (a)) or district-defining record (item 72) binds to ground districts when records bind, before any island
   district exists; the author may bind one to an island district by T5 with `sky` and the record's kind (V05 §4.2), held
   with its fate.

### 3.21 The rim as a barrier (item 106 where it reaches islands; J-V5.49)

An island is barrier all round: its rim is a sheer drop, and its only approaches are its landing yards.
1. **The rim is a closure arc.** For item 106 (a)'s closure and every "enclosed" or "sealed" test, the island's `rim`
   chain is a natural barrier of barrier kind `sheer` — one of the six closure-arc kinds (§2.7's `BarrierKind`; J-VI.3) —
   its defensibility class volume 11's `walls.barriers` row keyed `sheer` (wall `none`): closure only, no wall. The clerk's `closure` answer
   names it as barrier `sheer` (volume 10's `WhyAnswer.closure`).
2. **Generation never walls an island.** No stage lays an enceinte on a sky arena; the island's defence is its rim and
   its landings. A prison island's is its rim plus its `guarded` landing, whose role already admits only its
   institution's and the law-and-enforcement classes' rows (V05 §3.20 (2)).
3. **An author's wall on an island** (T3 `wall` in one of its **enclosure** kinds with the `key` parameter naming the
   island; `drawWall` with `key`, V05 §4.2) is an `Enceinte` of closure `barrier` on the island's arena — T3's
   `retaining` kind and a T2 terrace stroke's edge are never enclosures and lay an island `RetainingWall` instead (V05
   §3.22 (4)): its approachable arcs are the landing yards' frontages — each landing yard is closed across its inner side
   by one stretch on the rim strip's `setback` line, both ends anchored on the rim (a tower at each anchor, item 106 (a)'s
   anchor rule) and a gate at the yard's attach — and every other arc of the rim is closed by the barrier, with zero wall
   edges. The tool snaps the stroke's ends to the rim and shows the rim arcs as part of the circuit (item 106 (g)); a
   stroke drawn along the rim away from a landing is bent at its dry run to the landing stretches only
   (`bend.wall.drop`, V05 §4.2), and a stroke that crosses the island's interior is an inner wall laid as drawn (a
   castle's or a close's, §4.8's furniture). The stretch profile (item 106 (c)) grades each stretch: the full curtain
   across a landing yard, the strongest towers where it meets the rim. Adding or removing an access re-evaluates the
   circuit by item 22's least disruption: a new landing gets its stretch and gate, a removed one's stretch is removed
   (listed fates).
4. **The clerk** explains an unwalled rim: "the drop is the wall here" (V05 §4.5).
5. **The no-op pin.** With item 106's `barrier` closure and the stretch profile off, an author's island wall is the base's
   ring along the rim strip's line, byte-identical to the base (T-V5-27).

### 3.22 Item 110 on, under and against the island (the owner's "they go in")

The base carries item 110 (a)–(d) (§2.7, §4.3, §4.7); volume 1 carries terraced wards and the unit run's levelling,
volume 2 stepped massing and deck buildings, volume 4 the vaults under raised ways, and volume 6 all of it over epochs.
The island meets item 110 in the places below; each rule here is this volume's, and none of it changes a ground value
while the island setting is off.

1. **Casters under an island.** Everything item 110 raises above the ground is a caster to the island's clearance: a
   raised part's deck top with its parapet, or an aqueduct's channel wall, over the deck's plan, and its raised-way piers
   (item 110 (b)); a retaining wall's high-side top and a levelled platform's datum where fill raises it (item 110 (a));
   a stepped building's highest massing tier, read from volume 2's stack envelope (item 110 (c); V02 §3.15.2). They are
   read at SKY for what exists then (V05 §3.9 (3); every generated raised part, §4.3's slot), at SKY-F for what the pool
   and the unit runs placed (V05 §3.9 (5)), after every epoch's growth (`sky.clearance`, V05 §3.15 (7)), and by any edit
   that adds one beneath (V05 §4.3). Their heights are volume 11's raised-way, terrace and massing-tier rows; the caster
   allowance's P0 measurement runs with them on (V05 §3.9 (5)).
2. **The island yields, never the city** (J-V5.68). No raised part, retaining wall or building is moved, lowered or
   refused because an island floats above it: the island's `groundRefCm` rises to clear it and its elevation follows
   only where the floor needs it. The raised-way generator treats an island's projected footprint as open air; only the
   accesses constrain its courses ((3)). A caster removed beneath an island re-reads `groundRefCm` and never lowers the
   island (V05 §4.3).
3. **The accesses and the decks** (J-V5.60, J-V5.61).
   (a) **Feet on part-0 land only.** Admissible ground for a foot, a landing yard and S7b (5)'s reachability transform
   excludes every raised-way pier footprint, every raised deck's plan dilated by `accessSiting.deckGapM` and every deck
   face of item 110 (d) (V05 §3.7 (2), §3.10 (3)–(4)); "the nearest way" is a walkable part-0 way.
   (b) **Rope lines clear the decks.** A `ropeway` or `lightBridge` keeps `accessSiting.lineClearM` over every raised deck
   and raised-way pier its segment crosses, at SKY and after (V05 §3.10 (3)).
   (c) **The accesses as overhead obstacles** (§4.3). Every raised part laid after SKY reads `skyAccessObstacles(doc)`
   (V05 §2.3b) through its two callers: item 110 (b)'s course solver — at an epoch's raised-way pass (V06) and at a new
   stage's S4 in an epoch (no generation stage after SKY lays a raised part, §4.3's slot) — which keeps the deck, its
   parapet and its raised-way piers out of every column dilated by `deckGapM` and under every segment by its `clearCm`,
   re-routing by its own ladder and otherwise not building the part (reported "would cut <island>'s hoist"); and T4
   `road`'s `raised` dry run (and the aqueduct class), which re-routes or, failing that, ends the course at its last
   ground vertex before the column (`bend.raised.hoist`, J-VI.4 (f)). An access placed or moved later (`placeSkyAccess`,
   a move, `setAttr(ground.at)`) meets the standing raised parts by (a) and (b) at its own dry run.
   (d) **No raised way reaches an island.** A raised part's two ends are ramps or stairs joined to ground vertices (item
   110 (b)); a T4 `raised` or aqueduct stroke that ends on an island's rim makes no way and is bent into a sky access
   (V05 §4.2; J-VI.4 (d)), so II.1's "sky: no portals, no roads in or out" stands. **No raised part stands on an
   island**: item 110 (b)'s decks live in ground parts ≥ 1, and the sky layer has one part per island (V05 §2.1); a T4
   `raised` or aqueduct stroke on an island's key is bent (J-VI.4 (e)). **No access is a way** (V05 §2.2).
4. **The island's own retaining walls** (J-V5.69). Volume 1's unit run levels island ground by item 110 (a)'s thresholds
   (`terrace.slopeMin`, `terrace.stepMinCm`, volume 11's rows, read unchanged), which on the island's gentle dome and
   quarter-amplitude echo (V05 §3.8 (3)) bite mainly on an `own` relief of a steep class; a terraced edge is laid only
   where the island's relief class — `terrainEcho.reliefClass` for `own`, `plain` for `echo` and `none` (V01 §3.17.7
   (2)) — asks for terraces. Each retaining wall it raises — and each one an author draws with T3's `retaining` kind or as
   a T2 terrace stroke's edge with the island's `key` — is a `RetainingWall` on the island's arena (§2.7: a chain on the
   arena whose terrain it levels). **Never on the rim strip or along the rim:** the levelling stops at the rim strip's
   `setback` line, and a retaining stroke that meets the rim strip or runs along the rim is bent inward at its dry run
   (`bend.retaining.rim`, J-VI.4 (g)) — every point of the stroke lying in the rim strip moves along the island's radial
   direction to 1 m inside the strip's `setback` line, the other points unchanged (V09 §4.20.9). It is never an
   enclosure, never snapped to the rim and never a member of a barrier circuit (V05 §3.21 (3)). It draws in the island's
   frame and is displaced with it under `offset` (§8.4), its shadow on its low side; it tombstones with the island at a
   fall (V05 §3.16 (7)) and exports in the island's `sky` collection (V05 §5.5). The surface top and SKY-F's crypt re-test
   read the levelled heights through `levelledGroundCm` (V05 §3.9 (4), §3.10 (5)). A way that crosses it becomes a stair or
   a ramp by item 110 (a)'s way-class rule, on the island as on the ground.
5. **What the raised-way generator reads of the sky** (§4.3; J-V5.63).
   (a) **Endpoints bind to reservations.** An elevated road's endpoints are the reservations — the citadel's site, a great
   temple's or cathedral's precinct — never the institutions in them. S7b (5)'s hand-over keeps the reservation (V05
   §3.5), so a road raised to a precinct whose cathedral later rose to an island stands, and its reason reads through the
   hand-over ("links the citadel and the old cathedral close, whose cathedral rose to <island>").
   (b) **Ground arena only.** At an epoch and in every post-build request, the generator reads ground-arena entities only
   (`PlatKey.layer` `'ground'`, any part) — for its endpoints and for its evidence — never a sky ward, an island
   institution or an island landmark. The test is by arena, never by a list of `sky-*` kinds.
   (c) **The water need by arena.** At generation the aqueduct's need reads the S2 population whole, since S7b (5) has not
   run — a small, deterministic overestimate (a full island carries at most 3 × `H_d` households). At an epoch it reads
   the ground households only: an island's households are never in a ground water need, its water being its own — its
   conjured river or spring pond (V05 §3.8) and any well the unit run sinks on the island's own arena, which reads nothing
   of the ground — and no aqueduct serves an island.
   (d) **Ground hydrology only.** The aqueduct's source search reads S1's springs and rivers on the ground terrain (an
   oasis's spring among them, item 111); an island's conjured river, spring pond and waterfall are never "a higher spring
   or upstream river", and no aqueduct terminal, basin, cistern or fountain stands on an island.
6. **Stepped massing and deck plots on the island.** An island institution may take volume 2's stepped rows (a stepped
   temple, a terraced palace, a tiered tower) through its composition and class rows, as on the ground; the surface top
   reads each point's highest massing tier (V05 §3.9 (4)), and a cathedral's crypt lies under its lowest massing tier,
   inside underside tier 0 (V05 §3.10 (5)). The island's bridges over its conjured river (V01 §3.10.3 (4)) may carry deck
   plots by item 110 (d)'s mechanism, and they fall with the island (V05 §3.16 (7)).
7. **Removals.** A removed generated raised way, retaining wall or deck plot beneath an island holds by §2.8's
   suppression; this volume adds only the island's own form — an island or an access that stays removed (V05 §3.2 (6),
   §3.10 (1), §4.2).
8. **The landfall** covers raised spans, raised-way piers, retaining walls and deck plots (V05 §3.16's table).
9. **The no-op pin and the declared shift** (J-V5.67). With item 110's terrace, raised-way and deck-plot rows off, every
   island value — its altitude, ground reference, accesses, strips and landfall — is byte-identical to this volume without
   item 110. With them on at their defaults (the owner's default at CITY and METROPOLIS), both sky goldens of T-V5-15
   change once, a declared shift naming both fixtures.
