an access reads `closed` (a bar glyph), `unserved` (alpha 0.5) and is not drawn once `ruined`; every other kind the
landfall covers draws by its own state row, as V05 §3.16's table's third column names it; the deck plots tombstoned with
their ruined decks draw nothing. A `removed` island draws nothing, labels nothing and has no shadow. The island's own
retaining walls (V05 §3.22 (4)) draw by the base's retaining-wall semantic in the island's frame, on the sky layer's list,

against the ground beneath it in every family. The island's own fabric resolves by the ground's semantics.

### 5.2 Occlusion

`ghost` by default (§8.4), `opaque` and `offset` as the sky `LayerSpec` sets them or an island's own `occlusion`
overrides. In `offset` the island's marks, labels and shadow-free silhouette draw displaced by `elevation ×
offsetPerMetre`, while its cast shadow stays where the sun truly puts it; the sky base is **lit at the island's true
position** and drawn displaced (V05 §5.3 (2)), and every pointer and tool point on it is un-displaced before the anchor
conversion (V05 §4.2).

### 5.3 Light and shadow — the underside's shadow (the owner's J98)

1. **The slab list** (§8.4 (a)) holds, per island and **under every occlusion mode** — `opaque` as well as `ghost` and
   `offset`, so an island is never written into the ground's surface layer as a column, whose shadow would start at the
   footprint's edge and lose the gap and the profile — underside tier 0 and underside tiers 1 … n of V05 §3.9 (2), each
   its world ring, its top and its underside, so the island's shadow is the shadow of its whole rock: a `root` casts a
   long tapering tail under a low sun, a `flat` only its rim; the ground right beneath is shaded from above yet lit from
   the side through the gap under the root; and the penumbra widens with the distance from the underside, so a higher
   island's shadow is softer (§8.4 (c)'s law).
2. **The sky surface layer** (§8.4 (f); J-V5.16). Where an island's drawn land face touches a tile, the tile worker rasters
   that tile's sky marks into a second base and a second surface layer (the island's terrain at its datum, its roofs,
   towers and crowns), each with its max-height pyramid. The light pass lights the sky base over the sky surface layer —
   its rays reading the ground's pyramids, the other islands' slabs and its own island's surface — and the ground base
   over the ground surface layer with the whole slab list; then composes: `opaque`, the lit sky over its coverage;
   `ghost`, the lit sky × (1 − `ghostAlpha`) over the lit ground × `ghostAlpha`; `offset`, the lit ground everywhere and
   the lit sky over its displaced coverage. The CPU reference and the GPU pass run the same law. A tile no island's land
   touches takes no second base, but it is **re-lit where an island's shadow falls on it** — the shadow reaches up to 8 ×
   the island's height from its footprint. **The global light's window** (§8.4 (c)) reads the surface layers' own top
   (`surfaceLayerTopCm`, §8.1), without the slabs, and each slab whose shadow sweep crosses the ray's plan is tested exactly
   over its own reach (J-V5.56), so an island lengthens no ground ray's window; a tile culls the slabs whose sweep's
   bounding box misses it.
3. **Rung 3** (the vector rung; J-V5.55): the island's shadow is the union over its underside tiers of each tier
   projected in the **`prism` form** — its ring swept along the sun vector from its underside's offset `b · c` to its
   top's `t · c`, then unioned — whatever `vector.projected` says for other casters, since an island is a floating solid;
   the rim slab sweeps from its base to the surface top. So a `root`'s thin lower tiers join into one tapering tail
   instead of a row of beads. Only when §8.4's rung-3 budget fallback fires is the `drop` form used, and then the tiers
   are subdivided so that no tier's sweep `(t − b) · c` exceeds its ring's inradius, which keeps successive drops
   overlapping.
4. **The cursor sun under an island** (§8.4 (d); J-V5.14). The cursor sun stands at `H = h₀ · D · s`; at close zooms that
   can fall below an island's top. **Whenever the sky layer is visible and the document holds an island that is not
   fallen**, `H` is `max(h₀ · D · s, 1.5 × the list's surfaceTopCm above the view's ground)` — whatever is in view — so
   no sun ever stands below a floating island and panning never changes `H`; the path and fixed suns are unchanged.
5. **The picture and the model agree.** The light pass's shadow (from the rough tiers, at any sun) and the model's strip
   (from the smooth tiers, at the sampler's fixed suns, V05 §3.19) read one solid: under the path sun set to the equinox
   at a sample's hour, the style's `sun.latitudeDeg` set to the site latitude, the rung-3 shadow of the smooth tiers on
   flat ground covers exactly the cells the strip marks for that sample, within one field cell at the boundary (T-V5-20)
   — so the shade map and the drawn shadow never disagree about where an island's shade falls.

### 5.4 Labels

The island's `quarter` label draws at the city band along its long axis (§9.3's area rule on its land face), its
districts' labels from the district band, an institution's `landmark` label, a landfall's `place` label; accesses carry
no label, and neither does a strip (the shade map view's legend names the islands). Candidates of sky entities are held
in the island's local frame and translated by S14 (§9.1). §9.2's candidate set: sky rows only while the sky layer is
visible; under `opaque`, a ground row whose anchor lies inside a visible island's drawn land face is no candidate; under
`ghost` both are candidates, the sky rows at 1.5 × their priority; under `offset`, sky candidates at their displaced
positions at the stop. An island too small for its label at a stop draws none there (§9.2's fit test).

The strip rows assume the sampler's default (one point per cell); at four points per cell (§4.5's fallback) they scale by
four — ≤ 16 ms at CITY and ≤ 24 ms at METROPOLIS per island — and the memory row's strips by two and a half (two bytes
per cell become five). The chain rule of V05 §3.20 (9) and the node term add no measurable work: the one is a filter on
the sky fill and the route reads already costed, the other one addend per evaluation. **Pre-decided fallbacks:** the
strip row missed → `shade.stepM` 8 → 16 m and `shade.ringMaskM` 4 → 8 m, re-measured (T-V5-20's one-cell agreement
re-checked), then the samples' reach cap for the strips 8 → 4, recorded as a declared shift of the term; the model rows
missed at M6-sky → the placement lattice coarsens 40 → 80 m and the island units join the pool as units (pool size ≥ 1),
re-measured; the rendering row missed → the sky surface layer at half resolution (rung 2's method), then rung 3 for the
sky base only, reported in the view telemetry; any other row missed is re-declared at its measured value for the owner's
veto (D-18). Volume 6 sums its century from these rows by citation (V06 §6.2).
