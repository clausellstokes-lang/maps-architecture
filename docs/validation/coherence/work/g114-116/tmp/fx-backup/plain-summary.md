# Your three new rules, in plain words (2026-09-30)

**Rule 114 — the Economy caps how rich a town can be.** When a map is first made, the economy slider sets the richest
any household, house or neighbourhood may be. At 50 or above nothing changes; below 50 the top tiers disappear step by
step, down to "modest" at 0. After that first generation the cap stays, though real later wealth can lift it.

**Rule 115 — floating islands need high magic and a high economy.** A city or metropolis gets floating islands only if
its world has magic, its magic is high, and its economy is 75 or more. Following your words "on the slider", high magic
means the magic slider at 75 or more — or SettlementForge itself saying the magic is "high". Islands never appear in a
world without magic.

**Rule 116 — buildings are made of what the land, the ruins and trade provide, and what people can afford.** A town
builds with its own timber, stone, clay, reed and earth; anything else only arrives from a named trading partner that
has it, and costs more the further it travels (sea cheapest, roads dearest). Richer neighbourhoods use the dearer
materials, the middling mix them, even within one house. A town with no partner never gets foreign materials.

**How they fit together.** The three rules were checked against each other and filed as one bundle. One consequence you
should know: in a town whose Economy is below about 33, there are no "very rich", so no house there is built of stone
brought by river or road (only sea-borne stone from a very near port can reach the richest few).
