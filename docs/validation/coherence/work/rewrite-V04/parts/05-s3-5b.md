
**3.5.4 Vaults (J-V4.19; g item 110 (b)).**
* **(a) Bridge vaults:** each deck end of a built bridge — a part-0 `Bridge` deck over water (§2.7), never a raised deck —
  whose `deckCm` stands more than `bridgeRiseCm` over the bank's ground takes one `bridgeVault` chamber under its approach
  way: the way's corridor over `approachM` from the deck end, inset 50 cm, floor `vaultBelowCm` below the street, ceiling
  300.
* **(b) Street vaults:** every part-0 way stretch of at least 12 m in `R` whose along-way grade exceeds `vaultSlope` takes a
  `streetVault` under its corridor, inset 50 cm, floor `vaultBelowCm` below the stretch's lowest street point. The grade is
  measured over the way's ramped profile with its stair intervals left out, so a stair where a way crosses a
  `RetainingWall` is no vault source; a raised ramp is never a street-vault stretch.
* **(c) Cisterns:** each precinct of growth stage 1 and the castle where `min(dist.water, the plan distance to the nearest
  aqueduct delivery point)` exceeds `cisternDistM` takes a `cistern` under its open court (a yard part of its plot; the
  castle's inner ward or bailey face), a rounded square of the row's area, floor `cisternBelowCm` below, with a `well`
  access in the court above; a cistern record forces one under its precinct (V04 §2.3a). `aqueductDelivery(doc)` is each
  aqueduct's terminal point and every public fountain the base records it feeds (item 244). This reads only the base's
  eager aqueduct records, and the base's aqueduct need test reads none of V04's placed cisterns (item 244), so the two
  never form a cycle (J-V4.83).
* II.7.3's surface list binds every vault of (a)–(c) and (e), the cistern's own host admitted (a castle's reservation
  excludes sewer dwellings, not its own cistern; item 559). Each street or bridge vault takes a `stair` from the corridor
  at its downhill end, source `system`, `knownTo: 'public'`.
* **(d) Arch vaults — the fourth source** (g item 110 (b): "the spaces between piers stay open or hold the base's
  vaults"; J-V4.79 – J-V4.81). The base fixes each span's `use` eagerly by the span rule (item 244), `country` spans
  included; V04 reads it and never decides it. `ArchVaults.reconcile(doc, raised, cause)` visits each raised way of
  `raised` (every one at T2) in address order:
  1. **Lay.** Each span of use `vault` has one chamber — kind `archVault`, key (`arch`, the way's lineage, `a`, `b`),
     `span` its `SpanKey` — found by its key or created: its footprint the span's under-deck rectangle between the two
     raised-way piers' inner faces (the deck's plan outline on ground part 0), inset `archInsetPierCm` at the raised-way
     piers and `archInsetOpenCm` at the two open faces; its floor (`elevationCm`) `levelledGroundCm` at the span's axis
     midpoint (item 309), levelled and cut into the uphill side; its ceiling the span's soffit (item 244) less that floor
     (the ceiling V02's `vaultClamp` reads for a building standing in it). One `door` `AccessPoint` (source `system`,
     `knownTo: 'public'`, cause `{ rule: 'system', system: the vaults system }`, `ground.host` the part-0 face at its
     point) stands at the midpoint of the open face whose `archDoorStripM` strip beyond holds the most part-0 way or open
     ground (no footprint, no water), ties by the lower side index; where neither face opens onto either, no door is made
     and `W_ARCH_VAULT_NO_DOOR` is reported (a walled-up arch).
  2. **Condition.** Its closure follows its span, re-read at every reconcile: `collapsed` where the span's `state` is
     `ruined` or `cleared`, where its V06 condition reads breached or dismantled (item 291), or where a bounding support's
     `state` is `ruined` or `cleared` (item 244) — a landfall's ruin included (V05 §3.16 (4)): the vaults under a span a
     landfall ruins collapse with it; else none. U-FADE never strikes it.
  3. **Remove.** Every arch vault of the way whose span no longer exists or whose use is `open`, and every arch vault of a
     way no longer in the model, is deleted with its door, its links and its accesses in the same commit, each a fate in
     the dry run.

  The chambers sit in the `vaults` system's part (created at the first, §2.9), one L1 batch and one L2 `corridorUnion` per
  reconcile. **They are not undercity voids:** P5, C1, the forced vaults, U-FRONT, F6 and U-FADE's candidates exclude
  them; L3's basement accesses and U-DIG's connector never target one (a cellar reaches it by the street); U-ACCESS (b)'s
  proximity links reach it as any vaults element. §8.2's chamber row reads it as storage (the vault hatch, V04 §5.2). An
  institution stands in one only by declaration: a declared placement naming the chamber (T6 `place` inside its
  footprint, a skeleton `placeAt`) seats the use on one plot equal to the chamber's floor and one single-storey building
  under its ceiling, its frontage entrance at the door on a grade face ((f)) with `faces` by the ground the door opens
  onto (`street`, `lane`, `alley` or `yard`, §2.6's rule; never `tunnel`); V03 plans it as any building (V03 §3.12 (9)).
  The span's recorded `reason` (`way`, `water`, `flood`, `tall`, `low`, `slope`, `country`, `land`, `chance`, `cap`,
  `user`) is the span rule's own vocabulary, a field distinct from V06's `RaisedReasonKey` of the raised way (J-VI.2).
* **(e) The terminal cistern.** An aqueduct whose terminal kind is `cistern` (item 244; a `cistern` terminal only under a
  `conduit_house` the roster or the dossier holds, else `fountain` or `basin`, J-VI.9) takes one `cistern` chamber, key
  (`terminal`, the aqueduct's lineage): under the yard part of its terminal building's plot (V11's `conduit_house`, V02
  §3.17 (b)) where one stands, else under the public ground (V04 §1.2) nearest the terminal point; a rounded square of
  `terminalCisternM2`, floor `cisternBelowCm` below, with a `well` access in the yard or on the public ground above
  (source `system`, cause `{ rule: 'system', system }`). The terminal building's plot's drain need is at least `channel`
  (N8), so the overflow leaves by the sewer where one runs. Where neither a yard nor public ground takes the rounded
  square, it shrinks in 10 % steps down to the precinct cistern's 150 m², else it is skipped and
  `W_TERMINAL_CISTERN_SKIPPED` is reported. The aqueduct's buried stretches (where the terrain stands above its gradient
  line) stay the base's line on ground part 0, never an underground system; they are foundation obstacles (V04 §3.5.0).
* **(f) Grade faces (the view `Chamber.gradeFaces`, exported as `gradeFaces(chamberId)`).** The sides of a vaults
  chamber that stand open to ground part 0: an `archVault`'s two sides of its rectangle parallel to its raised way's axis
  (the arch mouths; its other two sides stand against the raised-way piers); a `streetVault`'s side at its downhill end,
  where its stair opens onto the corridor; a `bridgeVault`'s side facing away from the deck, its open arch onto the bank;
  every other chamber none. Each entry is `{ side: number /* the outline's edge index */; at: [PointCm, PointCm];
  onto: FaceRef /* the ground part-0 face at the side's midpoint, by pointLocate */ }`, in side order. It is derived with
  its chamber, never stored, recomputed whenever the chamber or its raised way changes, so a removed raised way takes its
  grade faces with its vaults. Readers: V03 §3.12 (9) (windows, the frontage entrance, the face flue on a grade face) and
  V03's `ElaborationRecord.inputs`, the reader (§11.2), T-V4-63.

**3.5.5 The buried systems (J-V4.20; item 113 (c)).** The `buried` system holds three sources, one system per kind
(II.7.6), each element keyed by its source (V04 §2.5).

*(a) A scar's buried town and a raised grade.* II.7.6's rule, with its region the growth-stage-1 districts; its galleries
those districts' way centrelines (the old streets under the new, the regrowth-first town having kept their lines), its
`storey` chambers their buildings' footprints, and each square of the region (an L1 `square` face or a `square` block,
V04 §1.2; item 559) a `square` chamber: the old market under the new. Floors at II.7.6's scar depth below the present
grade; headrooms and ceilings by the table above; a building's entrance vertices are kept on its storey chamber's wall as
`portal` rows for the exports. The same builder serves `raiseGrade` at an epoch or by hand (II.7.6), over the raised
region's records. **Terraces under a raised grade (J-V4.112).** The floors follow the old grade point by point:
`levelledGroundCm` (item 309) read on the region's last live state, before the `raiseGrade` batch applies the raise, less
the depth the rule gives. A `RetainingWall` that the same batch tombstones inside the region (cause `convert:raiseGrade`,
V06 §3.19.4 (1)) is therefore a step in the buried floor: the galleries and the `storey` and `square` chambers on its high
side keep the high side's datum — the step being the wall's `stepProfile` (item 245) on that last live state at their
nearest station — and a gallery crossing the wall's line takes two knots at the wall's faces, as N6 places them for a
run, the segment between them a **stair step** (one gallery and one record still, drawn as a stair, V04 §5.2). A `square`
chamber the line crosses is split along it, each side at its own datum (a footprint never crosses a retaining wall). So
the buried town keeps its terraces and its old stairs; with no wall inside the region nothing changes. Such a buried town
is reachable by construction — the raised town's cellars open into its old storeys — so every cluster holding one of its
elements is joined to the surface (J-V4.62; V04 §3.5.7 (g)).

*(b) The buried former settlement (item 113 (c); DR39; J-V4.123).* `BuriedFormer.lay(doc, epoch, former: Id[]): { system:
Id } | null` lays the settlement's own past, buried, when V06's history replay (§4.11 *History-first generation* (5))
reaches the coarse epoch at which a former fabric's ruins first pass the burial threshold (V11's `history.burialYears`
with its factors). V06 passes `former` — the ids, in address order, of the former fabric's records the replay leaves
ruined and no later record reuses (a way trace no live way follows, a ruined building whose footprint no live building
takes, an enceinte stretch `dismantled` or ruined, its gates, a square face or square block of the former fabric) — each
read on its last live state; the surface records' burial (their tombstoning in the same batch) is V06's. In that batch it
lays, in the `buried` system's part (created at the first; one system per kind, so every later burial adds to it):
1. **The rise.** `riseCm = min(formerRiseMaxCm, formerRiseCmPerCentury × ageYears ÷ 100 ÷ burialFactor)`, `ageYears` the
   years since the former fabric was ruined (from the replay's record) and `burialFactor` the product of V11's
   burial-threshold factors that apply to the site (below 1 where floods, river silt or rebuilding on top shorten the
   threshold, above 1 on bare rock): a site that buries sooner buries deeper. Each element's floor is its old ground
   level: `levelledGroundCm` on the former record's last live state, which the present grade stands `riseCm` above.
2. **Its streets** — each way trace's centreline, the stretches of at least `formerMinGalleryM`, as galleries of the old
   way's width, `wallCm` 30, headroom `riseCm − 50`.
3. **Its major buildings** — each ruined building whose footprint is at least `formerStoreyMinM2`, as a `storey` chamber
   of its footprint, ceiling min(its storey height, `riseCm − 50`); a smaller building is rubble within its block and is
   not laid.
4. **Its squares** — each former square as a `square` chamber, ceiling `riseCm − 50`.
5. **Its walls** — each former enceinte stretch as a **wall band**: a gallery of `formerWallCm` width along the wall's
   chain, created `collapsed`, cause `decay` — solid masonry, never a void, never a frontage and never an access target,
   drawn as masonry (V04 §5.2); each former gate on it as a `gate` chamber of `formerGateM2` across the band, a void (the
   passage through the old wall), ceiling `riseCm − 50`.
6. **What the rise leaves.** A void element whose headroom or ceiling `riseCm − 50` falls below `formerMinHeadroomCm`
   is created `collapsed`, cause `decay`: a filled trace of the former plan, drawn, never a void. An element whose floor
   lies below the water table is created `flooded` (II.6's row). A former element is an older void for the foundation rule
   (V04 §3.5.0): one within reach of a present building's basement ring or of a foundation obstacle is created `collapsed`,
   cause `foundation`, or holds the footing as a pillar, by that rule. A former element whose band meets an element of
   (a)'s buried town at a shared depth is not laid (the younger burial holds the ground), counted in the report.
7. **Commit and record.** One L1 batch and one L2 `corridorUnion` batch; every element keyed (`former`, `epoch`, the former
   record's lineage) — a wall band's (…, `wall`), a gate's (…, `gate`) — so a replay of the same seed and history lays
   the same ids byte for byte. No draw is made. It returns the `buried` system's id, which V06 writes into
   `HistoryRecord.buried` (§2.7); with `former` empty, or with no element laid (every record under its minimum size or
   dropped by (6)), it lays nothing, returns `null` and reports `W_BURIED_FORMER_SKIPPED` with the count of records read.
8. **Access (item 109 (j); item 113 (e)).** Unlike (a)'s town, nothing opens into it by construction: its clusters are in
   the excavation reading (V04 §3.5.7 (g)) and start `unexcavated`, reached by a dig from above, a breach by a growing
   system, a well shaft, the `any–buried` proximity links and the user's excavation, and `lost` when every open path
   closes. It hosts F6's dwellings and the undercity as a `buried` source (C1's rank, `RuinRule.sourceWeight`).
9. **Afterwards.** Every later epoch keeps it (the underground is never torn down) and U-FADE ages it at the
   underground's rate; a re-derivation that re-runs the replay with a changed history lays it anew by least disruption
   (§7.7 item 5), its surviving keys kept (V04 §2.6). With the replay off (`history.replay` false, the parity presets) it
   is never called and the document is byte-identical (T-V4-77).

**3.5.6 Forced vaults — a required undercity always has a void (II.7.5; J-V4.61).** Where
`CircumstanceSlice.undercity = 'require'` and U-FORECAST's P6 finds no counted void with capacity for one neighbourhood,
U-SYS lays a `vaults` complex under the **oldest district** (the lowest `growthStage`, then the largest area, ties by
address), after the ordinary vaults: a grid of `streetVault` chambers of `forcedGridM` pitch under that district's public
ground (V04 §1.2; ground part 0, by `pointLocate`), never within reach of a foundation obstacle (V04 §3.5.0) and never
counting or joining an arch vault, at the vault row's floor `vaultBelowCm`, joined by vault galleries along the grid,
grown breadth-first from the district's largest square of either kind (else its centroid's nearest corridor) until the
floor area reaches `max(minHouseholds, H) × m2PerHousehold`, plus one `market` chamber of `mainChamberM2` at the grid's
start. The shallow-element rule binds it; where the ground cannot take it, the complex steps its floor below
`surfaceClearCm` (the surface rule then releases it, J-V4.21) and grows again. So C1's "none formed" cannot occur under
`require` (T-V4-37). Under `forbid`, S7b (7) makes no allocation and U-CITY does not run.

**3.5.7 U-ACCESS — the access pass (T2b; g item 109 (j) (3), (3b), (k) (2), (a) (3), (d); item 113 (e)).** After U-SYS
and before U-FRONT, in this order; every link and access it makes records its `cause` (items 249, 557).
* **(a) Overlap and mine breaches.** *Overlap:* for every pair of elements of different parts whose plan bands intersect
  (each part's static index) and whose vertical intervals — floor to floor plus headroom or ceiling — also intersect, one
  link at the intersection's centroid, `via: 'breach'`, cause `overlap`, naming the growing system and event. Where one
  end is a sealed cavern or ruin element its kind is `open` (the owner's rule); for any other pair the kind comes from
  `LinkRule.kinds`, a builders' grille reading `gated`. Neither element moves: the sewer's course and hydraulics are
  never bent. Where the plan bands intersect and the vertical gap is above 0 and at most `links.overlapShaftM`, a `shaft`
  link exists by a fixed-key draw (§6.1) keyed by the pair against `links.overlapShaftChance`. *Mine breach:* a mine
  working whose wall lies within `breachM` in 3-D of a cavern gallery joins it by a `heading` gallery in the quarry part
  along the straight 3-D segment (floor graded at most `digMaxGrade`, a `stair` link where steeper), with a `breach` link
  of kind `open`, cause `mineBreach`, at the cavern end; the working's shaft is then the cave's access by transitivity.
  *The village working (V07 §3.16 (4); J-V4.113):* at a village rung, where no `quarry` system exists, each mining use's
  outside-plot working — at T2b every one, with a scope those in `scope.workings` — is tested against the `cavern` and
  `ruin` elements whose plan band lies within `breachM` of the working's plot ring and whose top lies at most the
  working's `mine.workingDepthM` row (V07) below `levelledGroundCm` at the plot's centroid; the nearest such element of
  each void system (plan distance, ties by address) takes an `AccessPoint` at the working — kind `shaft`, role `access`,
  source `dig`, `knownTo: 'local'`, state `open`, its `ground.host` the working's plot face and its `ground.at` the plot's
  pole of inaccessibility, its `access_point` vertex on the element's kerb or wall nearest below it — cause
  `{ rule: 'mineBreach', system: <the void's system>, event }` (0 at generation, the epoch's `seq` with a scope), and no
  link record, the working being no system. It is keyed, and so idempotent, by (the working's lineage, the element's
  lineage), and draws nothing; at V07's hand-off up the working becomes a quarry source by V04 §3.5.3 and the access is
  kept. **The same overlap test runs wherever a system grows** (g item 109 (j) (3b)): `SewerStrategy.extend`, a
  catacomb's growth, a new quarry, mine or chain working, U-DIG's passages, a lifeline, and an edit that draws or deepens
  an element — each in its own commit, recording its cause.
* **(b) Proximity links (II.7.6 (2)–(4)).** For each pair of parts (a < b) and each element `e` of part a — tunnels,
  galleries, chambers, open or closed — samples every 5 m of its centreline or outline query part b's static index for
  elements within `linkReachM` in plan whose floor lies within `linkDepthM`; per element pair the stations are taken
  greedily along `e`, each at least `linkSpacingM` beyond the last and beyond any link (a) made; at each, one uniform on
  `sewer` keyed by (the two elements' lineages, the station ordinal) against the pair of kinds' chance (II.7.6); its kind
  by the weights, `flooded` only where both floors lie below the water table; its `via` by the floor difference —
  ≤ 100 cm `breach` (`door` where an end is an underground building's wall), ≤ 300 cm `stair`, else `shaft`; `knownTo` by
  II.7.6 (4); cause `proximity`. Its two `link_end` vertices land on each element's kerb or wall at the nearest points,
  one transaction per part (§3.4).
* **(c) Crypt joins (g item 109 (k) (2)).** A church-class building on ground part 0 (never a sky-layer building, V04
  §1.2; its class's crypt column `always` or `basement`) with `Basement.crypt` (item 238: derived with the basement at S11,
  so it exists at generation without any plan elaborated) whose crypt ring lies over a catacomb element in plan, or within
  II.7.3's `accessReachM` of one, draws once per (church, complex) pair: a fixed-key draw (§6.1) keyed by the pair (the one
  mixer over the church's and the complex source's lineages, as (b)'s pairs are), so a join first reached by an epoch's
  growth gives the answer a regeneration gives; its chance `cryptJoin.own` when the complex's source is the church's own
  churchyard, else `cryptJoin.other` (V11 §3.23.8). A success makes an `AccessPoint` from the crypt, source `crypt`, cause
  `{ rule: 'crypt', system }`: kind `door` where the element's floor lies within `stairDropCm` of the crypt floor, else
  `cellar_stair`; its head stays in the crypt (V03's `AccessHostRow`: catacomb with cause `crypt` → crypt). The join
  replaces item 64's draw for that pair. A building without `Basement.crypt` draws nothing. A church-class building's only
  way to a catacomb is this join: L3 gives it no basement access to a catacomb element, and no catacomb access of source
  `system` is headed in it (V04 §3.5.1 (9); V03 §3.7.2 (6)). Catacomb galleries under a church keep `foundationClearM`
  below its basement floor; a crypt is joined only by this access, never cut. The join set depends on no plan: it is the
  same with no building elaborated as after elaborating every church (T-V4-50).
* **(d) Dig from above (g item 109 (j) (3) (i), (j) (5)).** Every ground building (on ground part 0; a raised-way pier is
  no building and never a dig host) whose footprint overlaps in plan a cavern or ruin element — or an element of the buried
  former settlement — whose top lies below the building's **dig floor** — its basement floor (§2.6's basement depth) where
  it has a basement, else its `baseCm` — or whose basement ring lies within `DigRule.planReachM` in plan of one, draws once
  per void kind: a fixed-key draw (§6.1) keyed by (the building's lineage — a wing V02's mutator adds carrying its wing key
  (V02 §3.9 W6) — `dig`, the void kind), in this stage (the building's lineage already carries the epoch that built it,
  §6.3). `p = min(cap, classRow(class) × basementFactor × depthFactor(d))`, `classRow` by the building's class (V11 fills
  every class by its family), `basementFactor` with a basement and 1 without, `depthFactor(d)` 1 for `d ≤ reachM`,
  `exp(−(d − reachM) ÷ decayM)` beyond and 0 beyond `maxM`, `d` the vertical distance from the dig floor to the element's
  top; the row values are V11 §3.23.7's. A success makes an `AccessPoint` to the shallowest such element under the
  footprint, source `dig`, cause `{ rule: 'dig', system, event }`: a `cellar_stair` where the building has a basement and
  the drop lies within `stairDropCm`, else a `shaft`, and a `well` where the plot's well pin stands over the element; its
  head in the basement (V03's access head). The draw's value is fixed by its key and compared at every derivation: an
  access once made persists (removed only by V04 §3.10's host closures), and one not yet made appears when the building's
  chance later rises above its draw (a basement added, a deeper void reached) — so more buildings, deeper cellars and more
  digging raise the chance of access as the settlement grows, and a building never draws twice for one kind. Item 109
  (d)'s "chance raised over a ruin" is this row. The obstacle test reads V02's footprint and basement floor (V02 §3.14.5
  (f)).
* **(e) Public openings (g item 109 (j) (3) (ii)).** Each candidate V04 §3.5.2 and V04 §3.5.0a name — a `sinkhole`, a
  `mouth`, a `skylight`, a `collapsed_shaft` over a quarry, mine or ruin chamber under a square, a square or green block
  (V04 §1.2) or a field face with cover ≤ 15 m, a `ruin_entrance` at the ruin's marker — draws its `OpeningRow.chance`
  (V11 §3.23.7) by a fixed-key draw (§6.1) keyed by (the candidate element's lineage, the opening kind), candidates taken
  in (element address, opening kind) order. A success makes an `AccessPoint` of that kind (item 554), source `opening`,
  role `access` (a `skylight` is a `mouth` of role `light`), cause `{ rule: 'opening', system }`, `knownTo: 'public'` (a
  `ruin_entrance` `local` unless the dossier says the ruin is known).
* **(f) Reuse (g item 109 (d)).** An element of one part adopted by another system keeps its place, part and kind and takes
  `reusedBy: { system, since }` (item 249), with one link of cause `reuse`, `via: 'breach'`, at the adopting system's
  nearest element; the adopting system counts its area in its extent and draws it with its own program as an overlay, so
  nothing is laid twice. The sewer adopts an ancient drain after the fact: a ruin main street carrying its drain takes
  `reusedBy` the sewer where a laid run's centreline lies within `links.reuseM` of it for at least `links.reuseMinM`, its
  floor falls in the run's flow direction at ≥ `minGradient`, and its crown depth is at most `coverMaxCm` — the run keeps
  its own record and course, so the rule never works against hydraulics. A catacomb's tomb reuse and the quarry ossuary's
  adoption are made by U-SYS and recorded the same way. A well pin whose shaft — to the water table plus 100 cm — passes
  through a ruin, cavern or buried former chamber is always an access: kind `well`, source `dig`, cause `dig` — at every
  rung, V07's village `well` and `pump` pins being this same pin record (V07 §3.16 (3) (ii)). A buried chamber within
  `linkReachM` above a ruin chamber takes a `stair` link at the `any–buried` chance.
* **(g) The excavation reading and the lost void (g item 109 (j) (2)–(4); item 113 (e); J-V4.53, J-V4.122).**
  1. **Clusters.** A cluster is a connected component of a part's elements whose closure is none (a silted element
     passes; a collapsed, sealed or flooded one does not), joined at junctions and chamber walls. The reading covers the
     clusters of every `cavern` and `ruin` part and the clusters of the buried former settlement's elements. The clusters
     of every other part — the sewer, catacombs, quarries, vaults, dug passages, and every cluster holding an element of
     a scar's buried town or a raised grade (V04 §3.5.5 (a); J-V4.62) — are joined to the surface by construction.
  2. **Open paths.** The connection graph's nodes are the clusters of every part and the surface; its edges are the
     **open links** — links of kind `open`, `gated` or `secret`, never `collapsed` or `flooded` — and the access points in
     state `open` or `locked` on an element that is not closed, never `bricked`. A gated link is a gate its keepers open
     and a secret link a way those who know it use: item 113 (e)'s open path is one no closure has cut (the sewer sealed
     or caved in, the cellar filled, the building above lost, a disaster), and gates and secrecy close nothing
     (J-V4.122). A covered cluster is **`accessible`** when the graph joins it to the surface — transitively, so opening
     one void can open those beyond it.
  3. **Lost or unexcavated.** A covered cluster not joined to the surface is **`lost`** when any of its elements stands
     in its system's `ExcavationRecord.reached`, else **`unexcavated`**. In the commit whose reading makes a cluster
     `accessible`, every element of it not yet listed is added to `reached` (V04 §2.2). A lost cluster is refound by any
     commit that makes an open path again: a `reopen` of the sealed tunnel or gallery (V04 §3.10), a new dig ((d)), a
     breach by a growing or caving system ((a); item 113 (e)'s "a collapse"), a public opening ((e)), a reopened access,
     a lifeline or the user's excavation (`placeAccess`, V04 §4.1).
  4. **Dwellings.** F6's dwellings and an undercity neighbourhood in a cluster that turns `lost` or `unexcavated` are
     vacated by II.7.3's vacancy rules (an undercity neighbourhood by II.7.5's) in the same commit or epoch batch as the
     closing — a U-FADE seal of the last access, a `Foundations.apply` collapse, an edit's `bricked` state or delete —
     their plots and buildings kept as vacant, their households returned as displaced (cause `void`) to V06's re-homing at
     an epoch, which re-houses them the same epoch, and to T8 at a derivation; they may return at the next placement after the cluster turns `accessible` again (F6 at a derivation;
     V06's relocation at an epoch). The undo of the closing commit restores the reading, `reached` and the dwellings byte
     for byte.
  5. **Drawing and knowledge.** A lost cluster draws with the `unexcavated` program — faded, remembered as once known —
     for every key that admits its elements, whatever `showUnexcavated` says; an unexcavated cluster draws only under a
     `secret` key with the view key `showUnexcavated` on (V04 §5.2). V10 tells a lost cluster as "once known, its way in
     closed"; V09's panel line reads the same state (V09).
  6. **Computation.** The reading is derived, never stored: a union-find over clusters, recomputed after every commit that
     touches an access, a link, an element's closure or a building over a void, for the clusters that commit touches,
     cached by the underground's digest; `ExcavationRecord` is the only stored part. Readers: V04 §1b.1. V12's
     `undergroundInputOf` reads `unexcavated` and `lost` as `state: 'unexcavated'` and `state: 'lost'`.

**Determinism.** Item 64's basement draw stays in U-LINK (T5); the dig draws are U-ACCESS's; the two stages' stream keys
differ by their stage id (§6.1), and within U-ACCESS each (building, void kind) has its own salt, so no draw's value
depends on another rule's presence. Every key is listed in V04 §3.12.

**With a scope.** `AccessPass.run(doc, scope?: EpochScope)`: without a scope (T2b) it runs (a)–(g) over the whole
document; with V06's scope (`under.access`, `afterGrowth` 20) it runs (a), (b) and (f) over `scope.elements` and (a)'s
village-working test over `scope.workings`, (c) for the pairs whose catacomb element is in `scope.elements`, (d) for the
buildings of `scope.built` and `scope.basementGrown`, (e) for the candidates among `scope.elements` and under the faces of
`scope.surfaceChanged`, then (g). Every step is idempotent — a link or access found by its creation key is kept, never
made twice — so running it after `under.extend`'s own overlap tests adds nothing twice, and each made access or link
records `event` = the epoch's `seq`. Its last step is V01's opening pass over the public openings (e) made (V04 §3.10).
