
### 3.5 U-SYS — the void systems

**3.5.0 Existence, order and what every system shares.** In the order ruin, catacomb, cavern, quarry, vaults, buried —
the ruin laid first, so every later system meets it (g item 109 (c)) — each kind is gated by rung (g item 109 (j) (1)):

| kind | rungs | existence |
|---|---|---|
| `ruin` | every rung, THORP to METROPOLIS | V04 §3.5.0a's sources |
| `cavern`, the fissure variant included | every rung | the geology flag and its row chance × the mining boost (V04 §3.5.2) |
| `catacomb` | TOWN and above | V04 §3.5.1's candidates, rank and count |
| `quarry` (mines and chain mines) | TOWN and above | V04 §3.5.3's volume law; mines where the roster holds a mining class |
| `vaults` | TOWN and above for the generated sources (bridge, street and cistern vaults, the forced vaults); the arch vaults and the terminal cistern at every rung, following their raised way | V04 §3.5.4's rules; the forced vaults (V04 §3.5.6); the base's `vault` spans and `cistern` terminals (item 244) |
| `buried` | TOWN and above for a scar's buried town and a raised grade; every rung for the history replay's buried former settlement | the scar rule and `raiseGrade` (II.7.6; V04 §3.5.5 (a)); `BuriedFormer.lay` (V04 §3.5.5 (b)) |
| `dug` | every rung | U-DIG's closures and U-LINK L5 |

**In a hood document** (V04 §3.1's hood line) the rung column reads: `ruin`, `cavern` and `dug` as at every rung;
`catacomb` at TOWN's rules and count, on the hood's own burial faces; `vaults` the arch vaults and terminal cistern of a
declared raised way only; no `quarry` and no `buried`.

Each chance is one uniform on `sewer` keyed by (the kind, the source's lineage). The parity presets set every chance to 0
(a dossier source's included: a parity run carries no dossier), the ruin toggle `off`, the scar rule off and the history
replay off (T-V4-35). **Dossier forcing (item 386; V04 §2.3a):** a subterranean record whose row names this system kind
makes the system exist with at least one source — its own building where it has one; else a catacomb record the first
burial face in rank order that passes the dry-ground gate (V04 §3.5.1 (10)), a mine record the ore cell nearest the town
(V04 §3.5.3), a cistern record the oldest precinct's court (V04 §3.5.4) — its chance taken as 1. A crypt record forces no
system. A system is created (its part the next unused, §2.9) only when its first element can be laid; it writes its L0
margin ring, then its elements in one L1 batch (galleries as `gallery` chains, chambers as closed `chamber_wall` chains; a
gallery segment inside a chamber is dropped so galleries end on the wall), then one L2 `corridorUnion` batch. **The
shallow-element rule (J-V4.21):** an element of a system laid after the town whose ceiling lies within `surfaceClearCm` of
the ground above keeps `foundationClearM` (II.7.6) from every basement ring of another building and never lies under a
water face; deeper elements are free of the surface. A ruin is older than the town, so the rule runs the other way for it:
a ruin element under a footprint or within `foundationClearM` of a basement ring is created `collapsed`, cause
`foundation` (the town's foundations cut through it; g item 109 (c), (d)); a buried former settlement's element is an older
void too (V04 §3.5.5 (b)). A dry kind (catacomb, quarry, vaults, dug) keeps its floor above the water table; a buried or
ruin element found below it is created `flooded` (II.6's row). An arch vault stands at grade and is exempt from the cover
rows and from the dry-kind rule (V04 §3.5.4 (d)).

**Foundations (g item 110 (a), (b); J-V4.82).** `foundationObstacles(doc)` is derived at every derivation from the ground
as it stands: (1) each raised-way pier's footprint (its ring on ground part 0, for the raised way of every raised part; the raised way's
`supports`, placed by item 244's support step)
from `levelledGroundCm` at its centroid down to `span.supportFoundationCm` below it (item 244); (2) each `RetainingWall`'s
footing — a live wall's, and one a `raiseGrade` tombstoned (V06 §3.19.4 (1)), whose footing stays in the ground under the
buried town — a strip `foundations.footingWidthCm` wide centred on its chain, from its high-side ground down to its
low-side ground (each `levelledGroundCm` on its own side, a tombstoned wall's on its last live state) less
`footingBelowCm`, station by station along the wall; (3) each buried aqueduct stretch's channel band grown by
`conduitClearCm`, from the ground down to its channel floor less `conduitClearCm`. An element lies **within reach** of an
obstacle when its plan band meets the obstacle's footprint grown by `foundationClearM` (II.7.6) and its top lies above the
obstacle's bottom. Every rule that reads basement rings reads these beside them. A system laid after the town (a
catacomb, a quarry or mine working, vaults, the forced vaults, the undercity's carve, a dug passage and U-DIG's connector)
lays no element within reach; a catacomb node so refused records `blockedBy: 'foundation'`. An older void (a cavern, its
fissures, a ruin, a buried former settlement) keeps its plan and the builders fill what they met: a gallery's stretch
within reach is split out by II.6's stretch split and created `collapsed`, cause `foundation`; a chamber whose floor the
grown footprints cover by at least `foundations.collapseShare` is created `collapsed`, cause `foundation`, and one covered
less keeps its floor with the footing standing in it as a masonry pillar — no floor, no plot, no walkway and no pin on it
(drawn by V04 §5.2, explained by V10: "the footing of a raised-way pier of ⟨way⟩"). The reading is re-made at every
derivation from the current raised-way piers and retaining walls, so a removed raised way's fill goes with it and a new
one's comes with it (T-V4-65). A raised-way pier is no building and never a dig host for U-ACCESS (d). The elements of a
scar's buried town or a raised grade (V04 §3.5.5 (a)) are never collapsed by a footing: their old streets and storeys are
the ground the walls held.

**At an epoch (J-V4.109)** no V04 stage derives, so the rule is applied by `Foundations.apply(doc, scope, cause)` (V04
§1b.2), V06's `under.extend`'s first act (V06 §3.18.1 (6)), at every rung, the maintenance mode included: over the new
footprints and basement rings of `scope.built` and `scope.basementGrown`, the raised-way piers of the parts in
`scope.raised` and the footings of the retaining walls in `scope.levelled`, it applies this paragraph's rule to the
elements within reach of those obstacles — an older void's stretch split out `collapsed`, a chamber covered by at least
`collapseShare` `collapsed`, one covered less holding the footing as a pillar, a later system's element `collapsed` (a
catacomb's staying in its extent, V04 §3.5.1 (6)) — cause `foundation` with `event` the epoch's `seq`, one batch per part,
no draw. The plots on a floor it collapses (F6's, an undercity neighbourhood's) are vacated by II.7.3's vacancy rules in
the same batch and returned in `FoundationsResult` (V04 §2.4) — each plot with its households' ids — with the collapsed
elements and the pillars, for V06's re-homing by name inside the participant; the excavation reading of every cluster the
batch cut is recomputed in the same batch (V04 §3.5.7 (g)). An epoch op removes no obstacle — a breached or dismantled
span keeps its piers' footings (V06 §3.19.2), a tombstoned wall its footing — so the element set it collapses is the one a
regeneration of the same state collapses (T-V4-65; T-V6-67).

**3.5.0a Ruins (g item 109 (b), (c), (e)–(h); J-V4.52).** An older civilisation's remnant plan, laid first, at every rung.
1. **Existence, strongest first.** A ruin the document already holds is kept whole whatever its dossier source now says —
   a removed source clears `source.ref` (V04 §2.6; `W_RUIN_SOURCE_REMOVED`) — save a `blueprint` ruin, which a
   re-derivation with `recipe.layers.ruins` off does not lay. Otherwise the sources — `site.ruinEvidence`'s seven kinds as V08 §2.6a states them (DR30) — are read in this order
   and the first that fires sets `RuinRecord.source`: (a) a `site.deposits` entry of kind `ancient_ruins`, or an institution of scene
   archetype `ruined-landmark`: the ruin exists, no draw; (b) a `magical_node` deposit or a ley-line income row:
   `RuinRule.chance.magic`; (c) a `magical` history event, or `history.ageYears ≥ ancientYears` (an ancient founding):
   `chance.history`; (d) with none of these, `chance.blueprint[rung][relief]`, only while `recipe.layers.ruins` is `on`
   (item 404; D-3's named toggle). A delving institution multiplies (b)–(d) by `delvingFactor`, capped at 1, and alone
   gives nothing. One ruin system per settlement; its draw is a fixed-key draw (§6.1; V04 §3.12) keyed by (`ruin`, the
   source's lineage — the deposit's, the institution's, the event's — or the town root for the blueprint). A declared ruin
   element in the creation prefix (V04 §4.7) makes it exist whatever the draw, `source: 'declared'`. The chances' values
   are V11 §3.23.2's.
2. **Culture and axis.** The culture row is one draw over `RuinRule.cultures` by weight on the ruin's stream, or the row V11
   maps to the ancient civilisation the dossier's history names. `axisDeg` follows the row's orientation law: `cardinal`
   0° (north–south) ± 5°; `solar` 90° (east–west) ± 10°; `contour` the mean contour bearing over the outline
   (perpendicular to the mean terrain gradient); `free` uniform on [0°, 180°) — the jitter a draw. With `sharedAxisChance`
   it takes instead the bearing of the present tier-1 way whose spine passes nearest the centre (its OBB axis), and
   `sharedAxis` is set: the old axis surviving as the new (g item 109 (c)).
3. **Place, size and outline.** The centre is the marker's position (the port's ruin decoration or the ruined landmark's
   site); else the magical node's position (DR19's deposit site); else the centroid of the growth-stage-1 districts; else,
   at a village rung with no stage-1 district, the centroid of the village's district (V07's one district); else the town
   root's anchor. The significance is drawn by weight (a `ruined-landmark` record forces `palace`), the extent uniform in
   its `extentHa` range, capped at `maxFrameShare` × the frame's area. The outline is a rectangle of that area on
   `axisDeg` with the row's `aspect`, centred and clipped to the frame; a clip that removes more than half moves the centre
   toward the frame's centre, in 10 m steps, until half remains.
4. **Depth.** The ancient ground `g(p)` is the present terrain smoothed by a `smoothM` box kernel less `burialCm`,
   `burialCm` = `burialByAge(ageYears)` interpolated and clamped to `depthM` (item 563); `ageYears` is the source's (a
   history event's `yearsAgo`, a founding's age) where it gives one, else drawn on [`ancientYears`, 3 000]. A `terraced`
   plan steps its floor by `terraceRiseM` per terrace along the fall line. Each element's floor follows `g` at its vertices
   (a gallery graded, a chamber level at its centroid's value); each chamber's ceiling is its kind's, capped at
   `burialCm − 100`. An element whose floor lies below the water table is created `flooded`.
5. **The ancient plan, by family** (V11's row gives `blockPitchM`, `streetCm`, `aspect`, `roomM`, `columnPitchM`,
   `hallShare`, `terraceRiseM`):
   * `grid` — streets on a pitch drawn in `blockPitchM` along and across `axisDeg`, every third a main street at
     `streetCm[0]`, the rest `streetCm[1]`; each block cut into rooms by recursive splits along its longer side until both
     sides lie within `roomM`; the `hallShare` of blocks nearest the centre become one `hall` each, the central block a
     `temple`, and where the perimeter streets meet the outline a `gate`;
   * `axial` — a processional axis through the centre along `axisDeg` at `streetCm[0]`; along it alternate `court`s and
     rows of rooms mirrored across the axis; a `temple` at its head; `hall`s at a third of its length from the head;
   * `radial` — a ring road at 0.7 of the outline's inradius and spokes every 45° to a central `hall` (the citadel); the
     wedges cut into rooms as `grid` cuts blocks;
   * `terraced` — terraces along the contours `blockPitchM` apart, each a terrace gallery with a row of rooms on its
     uphill side, joined by a stair gallery every `blockPitchM` along the terrace.

   Streets and corridors become galleries; rooms become chambers of kind `room`, those of the outermost row in the quarter
   farthest from the centre along the axis `tomb` (the necropolis), those adjoining a `hall` `vault`; a cistern where the
   row's `hallShare` block lies lowest. Where the culture row's `monument.hosts` holds `tomb`, the necropolis's block
   nearest the centre is not cut into rooms: it is one `tomb` chamber, a mausoleum, so a monument fits (J-V4.86). Each main
   street carries its ancient drain as a channel (the `water` stroke), the element the sewer may adopt (V04 §3.5.7 (f)). At
   most `maxChambers` chambers: beyond it the plan's outer rooms are dropped from the outline inward. One L1 batch, one L2
   `corridorUnion` batch, on the next unused part.

   5a. **Stepped monuments (g item 110 (c); J-V4.84).** Where the culture row carries `monument`, each host kind it names
   takes one monument: `temple` the temple chamber nearest the centre, `hall` the hall nearest the centre, `tomb` the
   mausoleum of (5); ties by address. V02's `layTiers` (V02 §3.15.1) lays it with:
   * `frame` — the chamber's rectangle on `axisDeg`: `u` along its **front**, the side facing the nearest main-street
     (`axis`) gallery, else the side facing the centre, ties by the lower side index; `v` inward; the origin the front's
     corner that makes (`u`, `v`) right-handed, moved inward by `monument.clearM` along both;
   * `region` — `W` the front's length and `D` the depth, each less 2 × `monument.clearM`;
   * `scale` 1; `cells` V02's `stepped-monument` row at the anchor `monument.band`; `heights` V11's `stepped-shrine` row
     (V11 §3.6.4), not the great temple's nave row; `pitch` M11's one pitch rule at the site's climate row; `parapetCm` 0
     and `plinthCm` 0 (a ruin keeps neither); no `anchor`;
   * `draws` — ST1's list, drawn by V04 through V02's `drawTiers` in ST1's order (V02 §3.15.1) on `sewer`, keyed by (the ruin system, the chamber's
     plan coordinate, `'tiers/0'`) for the first pass and (…, `'tiers/1'`) for the per-tier pass; an author's
     `monument.count` replaces `n`, every other draw unchanged. ST6's failed check is an attempt on the same keys with the
     attempt index, as V02 states.

   **Failure and truncation, stated once (V04-ICONF-5).** An ST0-infeasible region is retried once with `clearM` halved;
   still infeasible, the chamber keeps its form with no monument and `W_RUIN_MONUMENT_SKIPPED` is reported — nothing is
   discarded (§5). A `TierFailure` returned by `layTiers` truncates the monument to the tiers laid. **The ceiling cut:**
   with `top` the laid stack's highest point above the chamber's floor (V02 §3.15.2's envelope), the chamber's ceiling
   becomes `min(burialCm − 100, max(its kind's ceiling, top + monument.ceilingClearCm))`; the tiers, stairs and shrine whose
   top would rise above that ceiling less `ceilingClearCm` are removed from the top down. Both truncations — V02's and the
   ceiling cut — are counted in `lay.truncated`, so the top stays under the ceiling (the ground above pressed on it);
   `standing` is the decay's reading only ((6)). The result is `Chamber.monument` (item 250), decayed by (6). Its standing
   tiers and its mound are obstacles for U-CITY (V04 §3.6). With no `monument` cell the ruin draws nothing on the
   `'tiers/*'` keys and is byte-identical (T-V4-68).

   5b. **Columns, modelled (J-V4.92).** In every `hall` and `temple` chamber, in its rectangle's frame, the column grid of
   pitch `columnPitchM` centred on the rectangle, every node at least `columnPitchM ÷ 2` inside its walls and outside its
   monument's tier-0 ring grown by 100 cm; each line of nodes along `u` is one colonnade row, indexed from the front. The
   nodes of the rows (6) keeps are `Chamber.columns`, derived, never stored, exported by `@plat/under` as
   `ruinColumns(chamberId)`: V03 §3.12 (6) reads them as fixed rings, the ruin program draws them as its 60 cm dots (V04
   §5.2), C3's walkways keep 60 cm clear of each, and glTF and VTT export them. The drawn dots are the modelled points
   (T-V4-44).
6. **Decay (the persistence order).** Each element's persistence class — `hall` for halls and temples, `cistern`, `gate`,
   `axis` for the main streets, `street` for the rest, `wall` for vaults, `room` for rooms, courts and tombs; `colonnade`
   for the columns' rows — survives when one uniform on `sewer` keyed by the element falls below
   `S(class) = min(1, survival[class]^(ageYears ÷ ageRefYears) + coverKeep × burialCm ÷ 1 000)`. A non-survivor stays as a
   record with `closure: 'collapsed'`, cause `decay` — g item 109 (c)'s "collapsed or filled", the base having no filled
   state. Collapsed elements count in V12's plan measures and never in capacity or access. **A colonnade row** survives
   when one uniform keyed by (the chamber's key, `'colonnade'`, the row's index) falls below `S(colonnade)`; a fallen row's
   nodes leave `Chamber.columns`, and a collapsed chamber has none. **A monument decays from the top:** tier k stands while
   one uniform keyed by (the chamber's key, `'tiers/decay'`, k) falls below `S(hall) × monument.tierDecay^k`; `standing`
   is the first k that fails, and every tier from it up falls with its stair; the shrine stands only when every tier
   stands and its own uniform (index n) passes. The fallen tiers lie as a rubble mound on the first fallen tier's ring,
   `moundCm = moundShare × Σ` the fallen tiers' heights, capped at the ceiling less 50 cm. A host chamber created
   `collapsed` keeps its monument with `standing` 0: a mound in the rubble.
7. **Distinct from the town above (g item 109 (c)).** After the decay, V12's paired measures (`ruin.blockArea`,
   `grid.r4`, `grid.axis`) are computed for the ruin's plan and for the district fabric over its outline (V01's
   `fabricSignature(outline)`, V01 §3.16); where they fail V12's `paired` margins, the next culture row in the draw's order
   is tried (up to `distinct.retries`), then the axis is turned by `distinct.rotateDeg` (skipped when `sharedAxis`); if all
   fail the last attempt stands and `W_RUIN_SIMILAR` is reported (`attempts`, `similar`).
8. **Its surface.** The marker — the port's ruin decoration, or the ruined landmark's building — stays on the ground and
   is the `ruin_entrance` candidate of U-ACCESS (e); `RuinRecord.marker` names it.
9. **`ruinCentre(doc)`** returns the record's centre (else null): the magical node sits at the ruin's heart, the position
   V08's `magical_node` site reads (g item 109 (f); DR30). **The report line.** For each `magical_node` deposit whose site
   stands at `ruinCentre()`, the tail's T6, once S13a has named the ruin, writes the generation report's line
   `ruin.node: { deposit: Id; ruin: Id /* the ruin system */; name: NameRef /* RuinRecord.name, a reference, so a renamed
   ruin reads its new name */ }` (§5.4), derived with the report and gone with the deposit or the ruin. V05's why ("the
   node beneath the old ruins", V05 §3.3, §4.5) and V10's read it. V05's node term stays positionless (J-V5.41): no island
   rule reads this volume's arena, and this line is all a sky reader takes from it (J-V4.102).
10. **Identity.** A ruin element's creation key is (the ruin system, its plan coordinate), so a regeneration that keeps
    the plan keeps every key; the record is kept whole while its source stands (V04 §2.6).

A ruin exists whole whether or not anyone can reach it: its excavation reading (V04 §3.5.7 (g)) starts `unexcavated`.

**3.5.1 Catacombs — under cemeteries only, counted, ranked, never shrinking (g item 109 (k); J-V4.16, J-V4.55).** TOWN
and above.
1. **Candidates: burial faces only.** (a) The `churchyard` part of a church or temple plot (§4.9's churchyard row) — the
   church building is never the source; (b) every `cemetery`-class use (V11 §3.5, its `extramural` row letting it lie
   outside the walls), the dossier's `Parish burial grounds`, `Burial ground`, `Burial grounds and charnel house` and
   `Graveyard` records seated as that class; (c) each burial ground of a `Cemetery network` record, one beyond each gate
   on a through route (item 108's COUNT row, V11); (d) a monastery's `burial` yard part; (e) at CITY and METROPOLIS only,
   after every burial face, a worked-out quarry working (V04 §3.5.3) within `ossuaryReachM` of `R`: the **quarry
   ossuary**, which adopts the working by `reuse` (V04 §3.5.7 (f)). A burial face on a sky island is skipped (V05 §1.3,
   §3.5 (3)).
2. **Rank by the burials each face serves.** At growth stage k of K, `burialShare_k(f)` is the share of that stage's
   households whose nearest open burial face, by the street solver's `street` distance from their plot (§4.7), is `f`
   (ties by address), over the faces standing at stage k. The face's need `A(f)` is II.7.6's ossuary law with that share
   as its burial share (item 560), a plague epoch's burials × `plagueNeedFactor`; at an epoch the span's calamity deaths
   (`opts.deaths`, (5)) are added as burials by the epoch's `burialShare` — each added to the need, never to the cap (6).
   Candidates rank by `A` descending, then the face's area, then address. A quarry ossuary's need is the burials of the
   faces left without a complex after the burial-face pass plus the unmet need of the stagnant complexes (7); one whose
   need is under `growthFloorM2` is not adopted.
3. **Count.** One at TOWN; at CITY and METROPOLIS `min(cap, 1 + ⌊churches ÷ churchesPer⌋)` (`CatacombRule.count`, V11
   §3.23.8). `churches` counts the buildings of classes `cathedral`, `great_temple`, `church` and `v.parish_church`
   (V07's village class, V07 §2.4, V11 §2.9) on the ground and on every live sky island, each once (V05 §3.5 (3); the one
   exception to V04 §1.2's sky rule — a count, never a candidate: (1) skips every sky face), and each monastery's church;
   a collective dossier record counts each member once (item 108 (c)); V06 re-reads the count at each epoch (V06 §3.18.2
   (1)). The churches set how many; only burial faces hold them. Candidates are tried in rank order until the count is
   reached, each passing II.7.6's dry-ground gate (not `damp` or `flood`), its age gate (`minAgeYears` by the face's growth
   stage, `HistoryHint`, the replay's record or the log) and its source chance — a fixed-key draw (§6.1; V04 §3.12) keyed
   by (`catacomb`, the face's lineage), compared against `CatacombRule.chance` at the rung of each derivation (so a face
   refused at TOWN is re-compared against CITY's chance when the rung rises). A face that fails is skipped and not counted,
   so not every town has one. Every existing complex counts, closed ones included; a created complex's existence is never
   drawn again.
4. **Layout.** A `CatacombComplex` per formed complex. Its anchor is the face's centroid (a quarry ossuary's, the
   working's), its axis the face's OBB long axis; grid nodes at `gridM` pitch along that axis. A breadth-first growth from
   the anchor node, in order (grid distance, then (i, j)), admits node `n` when: it lies within `reachM` of the anchor
   (else `reach`); its ground point is neither `damp` nor `flood` (`wet`); it is no tombstone; it lies under no
   `wall_strip` band (`wallStrip`); it passes the shallow-element rule and lies within reach of no foundation obstacle (V04
   §3.5.0: basements, raised-way piers, retaining-wall footings; `foundation`); it lies in no other system's element of
   this part at another depth band (`system`); and, with `A` the admitted set, the level floor holds:
   `max_{m ∈ A∪{n}} h(m) − floor(A∪{n}) ≤ depthM.max`, with `floor(X) = min_{m ∈ X}(h(m) − coverM − headroom)`
   (`depth`). Each rejection's reason is kept in `blockedBy`. A node inside a ruin `tomb` chamber in plan at a shared depth
   reuses it: the tomb's area counts toward the complex and takes `reusedBy` (V04 §3.5.7 (f)). Growth stops when the
   galleries' floor area plus the chambers' reaches the target. Every grid edge between two admitted nodes is a gallery;
   every admitted node where `(i + j) mod 4 = 0` and three or more galleries meet takes an `ossuary` chamber, a square of
   half the pitch. One complex, one floor (`floorCm`). Complexes are laid in rank order; a node within one pitch of an
   earlier complex joins it (its floor) when that floor is admissible there, else is not admitted — two complexes meet or
   keep apart, never cross (II.7.6). A catacomb growing into another system's element at a shared depth meets it through
   U-ACCESS (a)'s overlap link — a typed opening, never a crossing (V04 §3.13 (e)).
5. **The largest under the largest cemetery (J-V4.95).** Every complex but the **primary** is capped at `rankCap` × the
   primary's extent. The primary is a reading, never stored: the complex whose source serves the most burials — the
   greatest need `A` of (2) (a quarry ossuary's its need), ties by rank, so rank 1 at generation — at every derivation; at
   an epoch, V06's derived primary, the same reading held by its hysteresis (V06 §3.18.2 (3a)), passed in `opts.primary`.
   The cap only blocks growth (`blockedBy: 'rank'`) and never removes anything, so the largest catacomb lies under the
   cemetery serving the most burials — in a big city usually the main cemetery outside the walls, which overtakes an older
   churchyard's complex over epochs (g item 109 (k) (3)). **The signature:** `CatacombGrowth.grow(doc, epoch, opts?:
   { capM2: Record<Id, number>; primary: Id; deaths?: number })` — with `opts` (V06's `under.extend`), `capM2[id]` bounds
   each complex's growth at this call (V06's per-epoch cap, V06 §3.18.2 (3)), `primary` names the primary and `deaths`
   carries the span's calamity deaths (V06's sum of `CalamityRef.deaths` over the epoch's events, V06 §2.4, §3.18.2 (3);
   V08 §3.6; absent 0), which (2) adds to the need and never to the cap; without `opts`, the cap is (6)'s — none at
   generation and at a re-derivation (each complex to its need), the per-epoch cap with `years` the stage's span at a
   growth stage of the grow-by-stage path —, the primary is the reading above and no deaths are added.
6. **The ratchet (g item 109 (k) (4)).** Generation lays each complex to its need (the generation stands for the
   settlement's whole past; the per-epoch cap is an epoch rule) and sets `extentFloorM2`. Every later derivation — a growth
   stage (`extend`), an epoch, a dossier event, a replay, a Re-derive from any stage, a New random, a migration — only
   adds: every existing element is kept, closed ones included; the target is `max(the current extent, the recomputed
   need)`; at an epoch the growth is at most `max(the current extent × ((1 + growthCapShare)^years − 1), growthFloorM2)`,
   `years` the epoch's span and `growthCapShare` V11 §3.23.8's share per year (J-VI.6) — or V06's `opts.capM2[id]` where it
   passes one, (5); growth continues from the existing frontier in (grid distance, (i, j)) order; `extentFloorM2` becomes
   the greater of itself and the laid extent. A kept element that a later town puts under a basement ring is converted
   `collapsed`, cause `foundation`, and stays in the extent. A New random on the catacomb system re-derives its lights and
   labels only; its dry run says "catacombs keep their extent". **Below TOWN.** At a village rung an existing complex (V04
   §3.1's maintenance mode) never grows: `blockedBy` gains `'rung'`, it is stagnant, and its `fill` rises by (7) with the
   burials of its standing faces; its extent never falls below `extentFloorM2`; a rise back to TOWN lifts the block at the
   next derivation.
7. **Stagnation, a reading (g item 109 (k) (5)).** A complex is stagnant when its need exceeds its laid capacity and its
   frontier has no admissible node (or the rank cap blocks it). Its `fill` is then raised to
   `max(fill, clamp((need − laid) ÷ its ossuary chambers' area, 0, 1))` — ossuaries only fill; the bones never leave — and
   §8.2's ossuary program draws it as denser niches (V04 §5.2). When a block lifts (V01's `blockLifted` notice, a plot
   above demolished, the source reopened) growth resumes at the next derivation by least disruption.
8. **A closed face (stated here once; V06 cites it).** A face is **closed** when its cemetery use is closed or ruined;
   when its face is retyped to another land use; when the dossier removes its record; for a churchyard, when its
   churchyard part is removed or its plot holds no live church-family use (the church's use closed, or moved off the
   plot); for a quarry ossuary, when no face feeding it is open. Its complex is then frozen — no growth and no change of
   `fill` (`blockedBy: 'sourceClosed'`) — and stays, every element kept. A church-family use arriving on a closed
   churchyard's plot **reopens the same face**, its lineage kept, and the complex resumes at the next derivation from its
   frontier by least disruption. A new cemetery may begin a new complex only where the count allows.
9. **The entrance (item 560).** A `stair` `AccessPoint`, source `system`, cause `{ rule: 'system', system }`,
   `knownTo: 'local'`, at the grid node nearest the face's centroid, its ground host the face; its head inside the burial
   ground's chapel where one stands on the face and its class's crypt column is `never` (V11's `cemetery` chapel slot), in
   a cellar, the nave or a store by V03's `AccessHostRow` (catacomb, any cause but `crypt`) — never a crypt — else in the
   open face, drawn with the stair symbol. **A church-class building** (its class's crypt column `always` or `basement`,
   V03 §3.7.4) never hosts a catacomb access of source `system` (V03 §3.7.2 (6)): a churchyard's catacomb is entered from
   the churchyard, and through its church only by the crypt join (V04 §3.5.7 (c)); a quarry ossuary by its working's shaft
   and a stair at the working's surface head.
10. **Dossier forcing.** A catacomb record forces one complex, within the count, on the first burial face in rank order
    that passes the dry-ground gate: its chance 1, its age gate waived and reported. Where the roster holds no burial face
    at all, item 386's row seats the record's surface as a `cemetery` use at S10b (extramural soft, V11 §3.5) before the
    tail, so the tail never writes the ground. Below TOWN the TOWN gate stands: the record becomes one `ossuary` chamber of
    the `dug` system under its burial face — a charnel vault, its area the need capped at `charnelMaxM2`, reached by a
    `stair` from the face or its chapel — reported `W_CATACOMB_BELOW_TOWN`; never a church crypt and never a gallery
    system.
11. **Crypts are the church's.** V04 lays nothing with a church as its source; F4 and C4 never seat a crypt as an
    underground plot; a crypt record binds to its church building, which V03 §3.7.4 makes `always` (V04 §2.3a). The
    cathedral always has a crypt, the largest in the settlement — V03's bar, cited here.

**3.5.2 Caverns (J-V4.17; g item 109 (a), (j) (1)).** At every rung; the extent is the site's. *Existence:* the geology
flag (S1, with g item 109 (a) (1)'s relief rows) and its row chance (soluble 0.7, volcanic 0.4), × `mineBoost` (capped at
`mineCap`) where the site **holds mining** — the share of the frame's land cells with `SiteResources.ore ≥ 0.5` reaches
`miningCellShare` (V11 §3.23.1), or the roster holds a mining class, or the dossier carries a mining deposit (V04 §1b.5);
one uniform on `sewer` keyed by (`cavern`, the town root). **The fissure variant:** where the flag is `ordinary` and the
relief class is `hilly` or `mountainous`, a `cavern` system of `variant: 'fissure'` (item 248) exists with
`fissureChance`, × the same boost, capped. *Soluble rock:* the D8 paths over the frame's `rock`, `high` and `mid` cells
whose catchment reaches `caveCatchHa`, in descending accumulation, until the extent cap `maxRockShare` of the frame's rock
area or the length cap `caveKmPerKm2` × the frame's area in km² is reached; each path's gallery follows it with a lateral
meander of amplitude `meanderM` and wavelength `meanderWaveM` (value noise on `sewer` keyed by the path's start cell); its
floor is `max(WT(v) − 50, h(v) − depthM.max)`, capped at `h(v) − coverM − headroom`, and it carries `water` where the
floor lies at or below the water table WT (§2.6). A confluence under local relief (the height range within
`reliefWindowM`) of at least `hallReliefM` takes a `hall`: an ellipse of area
`min(hallMaxM2, hallBaseM2 + hallPerReliefM2 × relief)` m², axis ratio `hallAxisRatio` along the downstream path, eight
vertices each moved radially by ± `hallJitter` (a draw per vertex); **its floor lies `hallDryCm` above the local water
table**, joined to each gallery by a `stair` step (a gallery segment graded at `digMaxGrade`), the cave stream crossing the
hall as a `water` channel along the downstream path. *Volcanic rock:* `1 + ⌊tubeMax × u⌋` tubes (one uniform, capped at
`tubeMax`), started from the highest rock cells at least `tubeSpacingM` apart, following steepest descent for up to
`tubeMaxM` or the cap, floor at `h − tubeDepthCm`, a `breakdown` chamber every `tubeChamberM`. *Fissures:*
`1 + ⌊fissureMax × u⌋` fissures (capped at `fissureMax`), each started from a rock cell of the frame's top decile of slope
(the steepest first, at least `tubeSpacingM` apart), following the steepest line along the fall of the slope for up to
`fissureMaxM`; galleries of width drawn in `fissureWidthCm` and headroom in `fissureHeadroomCm` (a draw per gallery), each
floor at `h − coverM − headroom` capped by `depthM`; one or two `breakdown` chambers of at most `fissureChamberM2`; never
a `hall`. *Openings are candidates, not certainties* (g item 109 (j) (3) (ii)): where a gallery's crown cover falls below
`mouthCoverCm` the gallery is a `mouth` candidate there; a breakdown chamber within `skylightDepthM` of the surface a
`skylight` candidate; a soluble chamber whose cover is under `sinkholeCoverM`, under ground that is no footprint, a
`sinkhole` candidate — each drawn by U-ACCESS (e); a failed mouth candidate ends its gallery sealed by talus. Every cavern
and fissure element within reach of a foundation obstacle takes V04 §3.5.0's foundation rule.

**3.5.3 Quarries, mine voids and chain mines (J-V4.18).** *Volume* (II.7.6, J-A65.45): `V = wasteFactor ×
(Σ_stone buildings perimeter × (eaveCm − baseCm) × 60 cm + Σ enceintes length × thicknessCm × heightCm + Σ towers 0.4 π r² h)`.
*Cells:* the frame's cells with `SiteResources.stone ≥ 0.5` within `reachM`, ordered by (inside `R` first, stone value
descending, distance to the centre, cell index), taken until their volume reaches `V`. **The surface quarry** is §4.9's
`quarry` land use without a `source`; a landfall's rubble working — a `quarry` with a `source` (V05 §3.16 (4), item 253)
— is never the surface quarry, a stone working or a volume source (the sky layer is never read, V04 §1.2). **The surface
split** (II.7.6's "left to the hinterland's surface quarry"): a taken cell whose terrain class is `high` — the outcrop at
the surface, its cover under `minCoverM` — contributes its volume to §4.9's surface quarry and is laid nowhere
underground; the other taken cells, at `headroom × (1 − pillarShare)` per unit area, are grouped into **workings**
(4-connected components, a component over `workingMaxHa` split along its OBB's short axis). **A working's floor** is
`min_cells(h) − max(minCoverM + headroom, depthM.min)` in centimetres, a cell whose floor would lie deeper than
`depthM.max` dropped. *Layout:* each working is one `workings` chamber, its outline the working's cell union simplified at
`minEdge`; the room-and-pillar field inside it is **drawn, not modelled** — §8.2's `quarry` program lays square pillars
of side `√pillarShare × roomM` at pitch `roomM` along the chamber's OBB axis (J-V4.18); a `shaft` (public, source
`system`) opens on the nearest ground point within 30 m of the working's centre that is no footprint, its `access_point`
vertex on the chamber's wall nearest below it; the workings are joined by `haulage` galleries along a minimum spanning
tree over their centroids (Euclidean, Prim's, ties by index), the nearest working to the town joined to its outcrop by an
`adit` gallery ending in a `mouth` where the stone cell lies outside `R`. *Mine voids:* the same on
`SiteResources.ore ≥ 0.5` cells, volume `oreM3PerMine` per mining class instance, in the same `quarry` part. *Chain mines
(item 104 (h) (1), item 105):* each ACTIVE supply chain whose resource node is of kind `subterranean` reuses the nearest
mine or quarry working within `reachM`, or makes one — a working at the hinterland edge on the nearest
`SiteResources.ore` or `stone` cell — of volume `oreM3PerMine` × the chain's `chainVolume` factor, its surface head the
map feature item 104 (h) (1) places; the working is the chain's resource node for the supply web (item 105). The
undercity settles a workings chamber as any chamber (V04 §3.6): S10 cuts its floor, the drawn pillars yielding to its
plots.

*Worked out (J-V4.98).* `workedOut(working, scope?)` is a reading, derived at every derivation and never stored: a mine
working is worked out while its mining use is closed or gone; a chain mine while its chain is not ACTIVE; a stone working
— which serves the town's building stone and has no use — is worked out unless it is in the running epoch's
`scope.elements` (laid or extended by this epoch), so at generation, which stands for the town's past, every stone
working is worked out. Readers: the quarry-ossuary candidates (V04 §3.5.1 (1) (e)), V06's candidate list (V06 §3.18.2
(1)), V10.

*New workings at an epoch.* `QuarryWorkings.extend(doc, scope, epoch)` (TOWN and above; V06's `under.extend`, after the
catacombs' growth) lays, one commit per kind: (1) **stone** — the stone the epoch built, `ΔV = wasteFactor ×` (the volume
law's terms over the stone buildings of `scope.built` and the enceinte stretches and towers the epoch's fortification
step built), taken as new cells in the volume law's order, skipping every cell under an existing working or the surface
quarry's face, with the surface split, grouped into new workings; (2) **mines** — one working of `oreM3PerMine` per
mining-class use whose `opened` epoch is this one, on the ore cells in the same order; (3) **chain mines** — each ACTIVE
chain with a subterranean resource node and no working, as above. Every existing working is kept and never re-derived.
Each new working takes its `workings` chamber, its `shaft` and one `haulage` gallery to the nearest existing working of
its kind by centroid (ties by index; none for the first), its ordinal the system's next unused (so an epoch renumbers
nothing, V04 §2.5), and joins `EpochScope.elements`; its commit runs U-ACCESS (a)'s overlap test and, for a mine working,
the mine breach. Where the cells within `reachM` run out before `ΔV` is met, the rest comes from the surface quarry, the
unmet m³ reported. No draw is made (V04 §3.12).
