
### 3.6 U-CITY — the undercity (II.7.5's rules, as steps)

Existence, the size law, the allocation, the shadow rules, the outskirts and the light are II.7.5's. The layout constants
are `UnderLayoutRule`'s (V04 §2.3).
1. **C0 — existence.** U-CITY re-reads U-FORECAST's P6 verdict `{ exists, n, H }` and never re-draws; the four
   requirements are checked on the built voids. Under `require` at least one neighbourhood forms (V04 §3.5.6 lays its void
   where none is counted), and the first neighbourhood takes the `Underground city` record's assignment: its
   `District.identity.dossier` is the record's binding key and its name the record's name, a protected dossier name,
   never a naming-slice draw. Under `forbid` U-CITY does not run.
2. **C1 — the voids, read-only first.** For each built system part whose kind the preset admits
   (`UndercityRule.presets`; the `historical` preset admits `ruin`, item 558), its clusters are V04 §3.5.7 (g) (1)'s, the
   arch vaults and the terminal cistern excepted — they stand at grade or under a conduit house and no undercity takes
   them. A cavern, ruin or buried former cluster is a candidate only while its excavation reading is `accessible` (the
   undercity never excavates a sealed or lost void, J-V4.54). A cluster's capacity is its floor area — chambers plus the
   bands of galleries that carry no `water` (a `water` gallery counts 0, is never a walkway, and still joins clusters and
   serves as a water lifeline) — ÷ `m2PerHousehold`. Before any commit, each candidate passes a **read-only lifeline
   test**: L1's candidate search for access, air and water (V04 §3.7 L1) and C2's carve feasibility, run against the parts
   as they stand after U-ACCESS; a cluster that fails is not a candidate, so no neighbourhood is ever dropped after it is
   committed. Candidates are ranked by `capacity × RuinRule.sourceWeight[kind]`; where a ruin and another kind both
   qualify, the first pick is one draw on `sewer` keyed by (the settlement, `undercityRank`) weighted by those products
   (so V12's `ruinChosen` share is `w_ruin ÷ Σ w`), the rest in descending product, ties by lowest address; one
   neighbourhood each, while fewer than `n` are formed and the cluster holds at least `minHouseholds`; where fewer
   clusters qualify than `n` and the largest holds at least `2 × householdsPerNeighbourhood`, it is split in two by its
   chamber graph — seeds the two chambers farthest apart by graph distance, every node to the nearer seed, ties by address
   (J-V4.24; no draw). `H` is shared among the neighbourhoods in proportion to capacity, each capped at its own. **None
   formed** (never under `require`): no undercity; its households go to the underground pseudo-district's free capacity —
   its sewer-front plots, then F6's void dwellings — and only the remainder to T8 (V04 §3.7a), reported (F-VOID). Under
   `require`, where the one counted void the forecast relied on proves sealed, the required neighbourhood takes it with a
   lifeline stair of cause `dossier` (the dossier outranks the chance).
3. **C2 — the main chamber (J-V4.22).** The core (`District.core`, item 212) is, in order: a ruin `court`, `temple` or
   `hall` chamber of at least `mainChamberM2` (an ancient forum is the first plaza candidate, g item 109 (e)); else the
   cluster's largest chamber of at least `mainChamberM2`; else one is **carved** by U-CITY's carve post-pass on the host
   part: from the cluster node nearest its area centroid (ties by address) a 16-gon of radius
   `√(mainChamberM2 ÷ π) + carveMarginM` is inserted as a `chamber_wall` chain of kind `market`, its floor the seed's, its
   ceiling by V04 §3.5.0's table. A ruin chamber holding a monument is a core candidate where its floor less the
   monument's tier-0 ring and mound reaches `mainChamberM2`; the monument then stands inside the plaza disc, which the
   ring's stall pins and walkways keep clear of (an undercity market round an ancient pyramid). The carve lays nothing
   within reach of a foundation obstacle (V04 §3.5.0). Each host gallery crossing the 16-gon is split at the wall by §6.3's
   SPLIT rule; its inside parts take `existence: 'cleared'`, cause `rule:undercity-carve`, and are kept, never deleted (in
   a catacomb its grid of galleries — the undercity hollowed its own square). A New random on the host system re-runs the
   carve for every neighbourhood whose core it held, in the same transaction set; a New random on the neighbourhood
   `resurrect`s the cleared parts (§2.8) and re-carves. The carve's feasibility — its admitted area, inside the part's depth
   band and clear of every other cluster's elements, reaching `carveMinShare` of `mainChamberM2` — is part of C1's
   read-only test.
4. **C3 — streets (J-V4.23).** The core's plaza is the disc of radius `√(plazaShare × area ÷ π)` about its centroid,
   ringed by a closed walkway (a `Gallery` with `walkway`, width `walkwayCm`, wall 0): plots front the ring's outer kerb and
   the disc inside it stays open. In every chamber of at least `walkwayMinChamberM2`, the openings — the vertices where
   galleries meet its wall, and its access points — and one hub (the ring's nearest point for the core, the centroid
   otherwise) are joined by a minimum spanning tree (Prim's, ties by index) whose edges are shortest paths inside the
   chamber polygon (a visibility graph on its vertices, ties by index; a monument's standing rings and mound, a foundation
   pillar and each column point grown to 60 cm are holes in the polygon), each edge a walkway. Every gallery of the cluster
   that joins two chambers of the neighbourhood and carries no `water` takes `walkway` (II.7.5's adopted streets). One L1
   batch inserts them; one L2 `corridorUnion` batch bands them.
5. **C4 — plots and buildings.** S10 on the neighbourhood's blocks with frontages on walkway and gallery kerbs and on
   chamber walls (II.7.5); the surface test of V04 §3.5.0's shallow-element rule applied through F-UNDER's samples where
   the ceiling lies within the host row's `surfaceClearCm` of the ground, those blocks recording their ground cells as F3
   does; no plot on a floor at or below the water table (a cavern hall's floor lies `hallDryCm` above it, V04 §3.5.2); no
   plot on a monument's standing tiers or mound or on a foundation pillar (obstacles cut out of the floor), while a plot may
   hold column points, which V03 §3.12 (6) plans round. Every entrance S11 derives here takes `Entrance.faces: 'tunnel'`
   (item 239). S10b places the neighbourhood's share of the allocation, its relation families measured by `underDistance`
   (V04 §3.6a), the black market as a hard `near: core` row fronting the ring — bound to a `Black market bazaar` record
   where the dossier holds one (V04 §2.3a); ladder members, SIZE steps and clandestine modes as F4 states; no crypt is ever
   seated below (V04 §3.5.1 (11)). S11 with `UndercityRule.arrangements`, heights ceiling-capped (II.7.5); the ring's stall
   pins by §4.7's market pins at the secondary-market scale.
6. **C5 — the district.** `District { kind: 'undercity', parent, core, faces }` with II.7.5 (2)'s parent rule, computed as
   the first entry of V01's `districtsAbove(part, core)` (V01 §3.16); its prosperity field as II.7.5 states (the parent's
   field with its daylight term, J-V4.51); `outskirts` is written by U-LINK L6 (item 212).
7. **C6 — lights.** V04 §3.9's undercity rows.

At an epoch the undercity is re-read, never re-formed, by `UndercityEpoch.reread` (V04 §3.10); a neighbourhood whose host
cluster turns `lost` vacates by II.7.5's rules (V04 §3.5.7 (g) (4)).

### 3.6a The cross-arena distance (items 103, 105, 107; II.7.5 (3); J-V4.66)

1. **`underDistance(member, target)`** for a member on an underground part is the shortest path over the member's part's
   open walkways, galleries, chamber floors and tunnels — and on through open links into other parts — to an open access
   point or lifeline whose `knownTo` the member's use admits (a public use: `public` and `local` accesses; a clandestine
   use: its own `knownTo` and wider), plus the ground's `street`-mode distance (§4.7's solver) from that access's
   `ground.at` to the target; a target that is itself underground takes the underground path where it is shorter. It is
   II.7.5 (3)'s "along its walkways to its nearest lifeline and on from the lifeline's ground point", built. A member whose
   part holds no reachable admitted access (its cluster `lost`, its every access `bricked`) has `underDistance` ∞: it is
   no provider and no consumer of any relation until access returns, and its coverage counts it unserved (T-V4-60).
2. The relation families SUPPLY, CLIENTELE and COMPETITION, item 105's links (each consumer bound to its nearest
   provider) and item 107's comfort distance read `underDistance` for underground members.
3. EMITS and SENSITIVE-TO act within an arena only — rock absorbs smell, smoke and noise — except water pollution, which
   reaches the ground at the network's outfalls (a sewer-front tannery's counts at its run's outfall), and disorder, which
   reaches the parent district.
4. ALIGNMENT reads plan distance, so a guild seat under the garrison still repels (item 103 (b)'s example).
5. Coverage (item 103): each undercity neighbourhood and the underground pseudo-district (the sewer-front neighbourhoods
   and F6's void dwellings) is a catchment of its own for the everyday-service classes whose V11 row admits the arena.
6. The siting statistics of item 103 (e) run on underground members against a random-placement baseline (T-V4-60).

### 3.7 U-LINK — the connection stage (II.7.6's rules, as steps)

1. **L1 — lifelines, realised (II.7.5 (4); J-V4.25).** For each undercity neighbourhood, in address order, L1 makes what
   C1's read-only test found: *access* — the cluster is accessible by construction; where its nearest access is another
   part's element, L1 writes that **lifeline link** itself (kind `open`, or `secret` by `lifelineSecretShare`, a draw on
   `sewer` keyed by the district; its `via` by U-ACCESS (b)'s floor rule; cause `lifeline`), and where a basement access is
   the cheapest access L1 creates it with source `lifeline`, cause `lifeline`, and L3 skips that building; else the
   shallowest element's ground point above, on ground part 0 — public ground or a yard (V04 §1.2), or outside ground (a
   point under a raised deck on the way beneath, never inside a raised-way pier's footprint) — within `lifelineReachM`: a
   `stair` where the drop is at most `stairMaxDepthCm`, else a `shaft`, source `lifeline`, `knownTo` `secret` by the same
   draw else `local`; *air* — a `grating` of role `air` on public ground above a walkway or chamber, else a well pin's
   point, else a ruin decoration's, else a `shaft` through a building whose footprint lies over the cluster (its chimney),
   one per neighbourhood and one more per `airPerM2` of floor; *water* — a `water` gallery or a cistern of the cluster or
   one linked to it within `linkReachM` (U-ACCESS's links exist by now), else a well pin within 30 m of the cluster in plan
   (a `well` access), else a river-bank `mouth` of a cavern of the cluster; *a route outside* — satisfied when the graph
   reaches a `mouth` or an outfall, never required. A realisation that cannot place a vertex the test found runs U-DIG's
   lifeline closure from the district ring, its pieces keyed by (the district's lineage, the piece ordinal), its target
   never an unexcavated or lost cluster. No neighbourhood is dropped here.
2. **L2 — links between parts** are U-ACCESS (b)'s (V04 §3.5.7), run before the undercity.
3. **L3 — basement accesses (item 64).** II.7.3's *Basement accesses* (1)–(5), building by building on ground part 0
   (never a sky-layer building, V04 §1.2) in address order, cause `{ rule: 'basement' }`, for every target but a cavern,
   ruin or buried former element (U-ACCESS (d)'s dig draw decides those), a catacomb element from a church-class building
   (U-ACCESS (c)'s join is its only way to a catacomb) and an arch vault (at grade, reached by the street), skipping a
   building L1 gave a lifeline access. It runs here, after U-FRONT and U-CITY, because its targets include sewer
   dwellings' and undercity buildings' walls and undercity walkways (II.7.3 (1)).
4. **L4 — lifeline closures** run inside L1 (above).
5. **L5 — subterranean dossier institutions (item 386).** An institution with `facets.subterranean` that S10b placed on a
   surface plot (no underground allocation could take it) has a basement — S10b's hard row admits only plots whose
   terrain gate allows one, and S11 forces it by the requested-mode ladder (item 243) — and takes the first rung that
   succeeds: (1) a basement access to the nearest element within `accessReachM`; (2) U-DIG's connector to an element within
   `digReachM`; (3) U-DIG's minimal `dug` system to an outlet; (4) always: a `cellar` chamber of the `dug` system directly
   beneath its basement (floor at the `dug` row's upper depth, `cellar` 6 m², `knownTo` the institution's, no connector),
   reached by a `hatch` — where the water table lies above that floor the chamber is created `flooded` and
   `W_SUBTERRANEAN_WET` reported, and the fact still has its underground. An `Underground network` record takes the **dug
   network** instead (V04 §2.3a): from its building the passage to the `beyondWalls` outlet first, then up to
   `networkCellars` cellars of uses in its district by dug passages, one `storage` chamber, all `knownTo: 'secret'`, its
   `forbiddenResources` infinite in U-DIG's cost. Every L5 piece is keyed by (the institution's binding key, the piece
   ordinal) on `sewer`, source `system` with the building among the dug system's `sources`, cause `dossier`, held while the
   institution is live; the rung used is reported (T-V4-34). **When the dossier removes or closes the institution** (an
   epoch's `dossier` phase, an absorb, or a dossier event before a re-derivation): its pieces are **released**, never
   removed (item 11) — kept as `dug` elements with their cause, the building dropped from the dug system's `sources`, no
   longer held and no longer re-derived with an owner; the accesses headed in its building follow V04 §3.10's host
   closures (a use turned to a class whose access row is 0: `bricked`); U-FADE ages the released pieces at the
   underground's rate; the clusters they joined re-read their excavation in the same batch. A dossier that restores the
   institution finds its pieces again by the same binding key and holds them anew; an institution relocated by the
   dossier re-derives its pieces from its new building, the released ones kept (J-V4.124; T-V4-78).
6. **L6 — outskirts.** From each link endpoint on part 0 that joins a neighbourhood's cluster, a walk along open part-0
   runs up to `outskirtsReachM`; the U-FRONT blocks whose frontage runs it reaches are `District.outskirts` (II.7.5). L6
   re-runs after any part-0 edit within `outskirtsReachM` of a lifeline link (V04 §2.6).
7. **L7 — close.** `Features.underground` recomputed (II.7.4).

### 3.7a T8 — U-RECONCILE, the tail's only writer of ground uses and households (J-V4.59)

The last eager tail stage. Its inputs are what the tail could not seat below after absorption (V04 §3.4 F0, V04 §3.6 C1,
F6): surplus households, and institutions no underground plot took (an `underground`-mode concealed use; an
`undercity: 'preferred'` or `underground: 'preferred'` class). *Households* go by S10b's reconcile (§4.7) to the ground's
vacant residential buildings of the poorest band — first in the parent district of the neighbourhood they were meant
for, else the district above their run, else the town's poorest district — then to occupied poorest-band buildings up to
each building's capacity (§4.7's generation fill), in address order; a remainder (practically nil after `forecastSafety`
and the absorption) is reported in `requestedVsBuilt` and still counted in the population. *Institutions* take item 63's
chain (§4.7 *Concealed institutions* (2): `underground` → `front` → `hidden`) or their class row's ground placement, each
by S10b's reconcile on a vacant ground plot. T8 writes uses and household counts only: no plot, face, footprint or
building is created, moved or removed, so the ground arena's geometry and digests stay those of the first map and T12-1's
"0 writes on the ground `PlatKey`" holds for the underground stage. The ground's model digest is finalised after T8
(V04 §3.1). With nothing to reconcile T8 writes nothing and reports zero. Every T8 write is reported; T-V4-3 bars its rate
(at most 2 % of CITY seeds at the default presets).

### 3.8 U-DIG — the pieces an access generates (II.7.6's seven steps, as algorithms)

The pieces and their order are II.7.6's; the closure mechanics are §7.3 item 9's. Added here:
* **Target search.** Each part's static index is queried for elements within `accessReachM` of the basement ring (a
  direct access, no connector), then within `digReachM`, in II.7.6's order; ties by plan distance, then address. An arch
  vault is never a target; from a church-class building a catacomb element is a target only as a hand crypt join (V04
  §4.1).
* **The connector (J-V4.27).** A local grid of `digGridCm` cells over the bounding box of the basement ring and the
  target point grown by 20 m (at most 400 × 400 cells; larger is a refusal naming the bound). Step cost = step length ×
  `rockCost` of the terrain class under the cell (`damp` and `flood` infinite); infinite within `foundationClearM` of
  every other building's footprint and basement ring, within reach of every foundation obstacle (V04 §3.5.0) and of every
  dug passage's band, under a water face, and where the water table lies above the passage floor. A\* with the octile
  heuristic × the least cost, ties by (f, g, cell index). The path's cell centres are simplified (Visvalingam at
  `minEdge`) and snapped. Its floor falls linearly from the basement floor to the target's floor, clamped to the `dug`
  row's depth range, above the water table by 30 cm and below the network's `coverCm` (II.7.2); a remaining drop beyond
  `digMaxGrade` is taken by the end link's `stair` (`via`). A route longer than `2 × digReachM` is a refusal with the
  reason (II.7.6). A basement ring already touching its target needs no connector; a path that simplifies to a single
  cell is a direct access.
* **The minimal system.** Where nothing lies within `digReachM`, the outlet row's first kind with a candidate within
  `2 × digReachM` — another cellar of the same `Use.owner`, a void, the nearest river or canal bank, the shore, the
  nearest point beyond the outermost enceinte's outer strip — ends the passage: a `mouth` on outside ground, or a `door`
  into the other cellar. With no outlet of any kind within reach the closure's rung fails with its reason and the next
  rung (§7.3 item 9) is taken.
* **Keys at generation and at an edit.** An edit's pieces are keyed by the event's `seq` (§7.3 item 9 (b)); a lifeline's
  pieces at generation by (the district's lineage, the piece ordinal); L5's by (the institution's binding key, the piece
  ordinal); each on `sewer` with the same key. Generated pieces re-derive with their owner (the neighbourhood's New random;
  the institution's relocation); edit-made pieces are held (§7.3 item 9 (c)); a `regenerate` on the `dug` system
  re-derives only its generated pieces, its dry run listing the held ones as kept (item 561).
* **Voids and bans.** The target search never picks an element of an `unexcavated` or `lost` cluster unless the edit
  names it, or the access is the grouped access of §7.3 item 9 (d) (item 372): an `addUndergroundAccess { building }` with
  no `target`, grouped before a dwelling declaration on an `unexcavated` or `lost` cluster (V09 §4.17.4's forms), targets
  that cluster's element nearest in plan to the building's footprint (ties by address). Either is the user's excavation
  (cause `edit`), its dry run listing the clusters it opens, and the declaration's closure then finds the cluster
  reachable and adds no access. The grouped access keeps every other piece of its own closure (the basement by §2.6's
  requested-mode ladder, the connector where the target lies beyond `accessReachM` of the basement ring, the plan
  reservation); the rule holds for a building the dwelling closure's first rung qualifies (below; the base's "one of those
  buildings"), and an untargeted access from any other building takes the ordinary search, the declaration's closure then
  making its own way in. A record's `forbiddenResources` make `damp` and `flood` cells infinite in the connector's cost.
* **The dwelling closure's way in (§7.3 item 9 (d), item 372; V09 §4.17.4; J-V4.119).** A dwelling declaration on an
  `unexcavated` or `lost` cluster — a `quarter` or `drawBoundary` on a void part, a `placeAt` of a dwelling class on a void
  plot, a clerk's proposal of either — is a closure, and its first rung calls U-DIG. The buildings **above the cluster**
  are the ground buildings (ground part 0; never a raised-way pier) that V04 §3.5.7 (d)'s geometry admits over one of its
  elements — a footprint overlapping the element in plan, or a basement ring within `DigRule.planReachM` of it — each
  measured to the cluster's element nearest in plan to its footprint (ties by address); one **qualifies** when its dig
  floor (V04 §3.5.7 (d)) lies within `DigRule.maxM` above that element's top. They are ordered by that plan distance, then
  address, and the first digs: its target that element (a `reused` piece of the closure record); its pieces U-DIG's
  connector, only where the element lies beyond `accessReachM` of the basement ring (or, without a basement, of the
  footprint), the access and the plan reservation — no basement piece, the rung taking the building as it stands so that
  its dig floor stays the one it qualified by, and no minimal system; the access of V04 §3.5.7 (d)'s kinds (a
  `cellar_stair` where the building has a basement and the drop lies within `stairDropCm`, else a `shaft`, a `well` where
  the plot's well pin stands over the element), its head by V03's access head as (d)'s, source `dig`, cause
  `{ rule: 'edit', system: the cluster's system, event: the declaration's seq }`, with `bend.under.accessDug`. A building
  whose connector U-DIG refuses (the `2 × digReachM` bound above, or no finite path) is passed over for the next, listed in
  the dry run with its reason, and the rung fails only when none remains. The dry run lists in
  `PreviewResult.alternatives` the qualifying buildings after the one that dug, in the same order, at most three (the
  base's), without digging them in the dry run; a re-preview of the grouped access from one whose connector U-DIG then
  refuses is refused with its reason (§7.3 item 9 (e)). The base's second and third rungs — a public opening at the
  nearest of V04 §3.5.7 (e)'s candidates with its chance waived, else a `collapsed_shaft` at the base's point of public
  ground over the cluster — are made by U-ACCESS (e)'s opening maker as `placeAccess`'s public-opening form makes them
  (V04 §4.1): source `opening`, role `access`, cause `{ rule: 'edit', system, event }`, `knownTo` by (e)'s rule, V01's
  opening pass in the same commit (V04 §3.10); the fourth, the bend onto ground part 0, is the base's and adds no
  underground piece. The pieces are keyed as an edit's (above) by the declaration's `seq`, recorded in its closure record,
  held while it is live and removed by its undo (§7.3 item 9 (c)); the excavation reading opens the cluster in the same
  commit, and the declaration's own pieces follow in the same event (§7.3 item 9 (a)'s order). In a skeleton prefix the
  closure runs where U-CITY C0 reads the declaration on a cluster still `unexcavated` after T2b (V04 §4.7), a grouped
  access before it in the prefix running there first rather than among T5's closures (J-V4.120).
* **The edit-triggered lifeline closure (§7.3 item 9 (d)).** After the dry run of a `drawBoundary` or `split` on an
  undercity district, C1's read-only lifeline test runs for each resulting district; one that fails calls the lifeline
  generators from its ring, keyed by its lineage and the event's `seq`, the pieces shown as fates on the tools T5 and T9.
