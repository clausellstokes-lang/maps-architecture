### 3.12 Terrain and water over epochs (item 25)

The base owns the kinds, their repair and the trigger rows' chances (§4.11 *Terrain and water change slowly*); this
section fixes which bodies and faces are candidates and what each fired op changes. The `terrainWater` phase visits
candidates in address order; each fires at most once per body per epoch.
* **`siltReach`** — (a) a harbour basin with no live harbour use for `silt.idleYears`; (b) a reach within
  `silt.downstreamM` below a course change or a canal off-take made since the reach's last silting; (c) an oxbow (below).
  The share is the row's; a basin that falls below the harbour draught closes its harbour use (the use phase reads it next
  epoch).
* **`reclaim`** — a `marsh` `LandUse` face or a foreshore margin that touches a live plot or lies within 50 m of one, when
  S5d's shortfall record of the last growth is positive and the face's district band is `modest` or above (§4.11). The
  region is the whole face when it is at most `reclaim.maxHa`, else its part within `reclaim.bufferM` of built land,
  clipped to `reclaim.maxHa` from the built side. A reclamation that changes a shoreline an enceinte's closure relies on is
  recorded in the epoch's scope (`surfaceChanged`), and the next epoch's fortification phase re-evaluates that closure
  (V06 §3.7.6 (3)); `under.access` tests the changed faces for public openings the same epoch (V06 §3.18.1).
* **`shiftCourse`** — a reach whose sinuosity between two control knots exceeds `river.cutoffSinuosity`: the **neck** is
  the knot pair with the smallest straight distance per metre of course between them, at most 120 m apart; the cut-off
  re-traces the reach as the straight chord between them, and the old loop becomes an **oxbow** — a still water body of
  kind `oxbow` whose face is the abandoned channel (BCR-V6-6), cut off from the river, keeping its crossings as they are
  (its bridges stand over still water until the strike pool's `deck` candidate clears them once the oxbow has silted to marsh
  and no deck plot on them is occupied, V06 §3.19.5 (3)); the changed banks enter the epoch's scope as for a reclamation.
  **An aqueduct's source**: a `siltReach` or `shiftCourse` that silts a spring's reach or moves a river away from an
  aqueduct's intake is read by the `raisedWays` step the same epoch — the aqueduct runs dry at once (V06 §3.19.2 (2)). An **avulsion** — a reach whose flood cells exceed the
  row's share — re-traces the reach between the two knots along the least-cost path through the lowest ground of its
  flood band (deviation ≤ 300 m), and the abandoned channel becomes a `marsh` face. Both run as S1's water operator on the
  changed reach (§7.3 (b)), so their footprint, fates and locality are the carve's.
* **An oxbow silts to marsh**: its `oxbow.marshAfterYears`, drawn once at its formation on `stream(EVO_TERRAIN, oxbow)`,
  makes it a `siltReach` candidate whose op shrinks its shore by the row's share; when its area falls below 0.1 ha its
  water face is removed and the land typed `marsh` (BCR-V6-6).
* **`digCanal`** — only when a live use of a `canal` class (the roster's or a dossier element) has no canal water body
  within its plot's reach: the engine traces the canal on the S3 lattice from the nearest river reach or harbour to the
  use's plot with `existingFabricCost` (§4.3; plot lines cheap, footprints dear), at most `canal.maxM`; no path, no canal —
  reported. The dig is `carve` (§4.11).


**3.12.1 Springs and oases over epochs (item 111 (b)).** Last act of the `terrainWater` phase, after the trigger rows,
in its batch. Every live `WaterBody` of the ground arena that carries `spring` is visited in address order (a sky
island's water is volume 5's and is never visited).
1. **The epoch's drought.** The drought on the settlement over the epoch's span is the largest severity `s` among: each
   `EpochInputs.events` entry of kind `drought` whose span — its week plus its `years` (drawn once from `drought.years`
   on `stream(EVO_HAZARD, settlement)` keyed with its week when the entry carries none) — overlaps the span, its
   `severity` (drawn from `drought.severity` the same way when absent); each `calamity` entry the calamity table maps to
   its `drought` row, at its band's severity; each standing `ConditionOverlay` whose archetype the port maps to
   `drought` (§2.7; V08), at its `severity`; and, standalone under hazard mode `full` on a site whose climate row is arid,
   the spontaneous `drought` row's draw (`1 − (1 − perYear)^years` on `stream(EVO_HAZARD, settlement)`, its severity
   and years drawn as above). `y` is the number of years of the span the droughts cover (their union), `≤ years`.
2. **The flow.** A spring's new flow is `max(spring.minFlow, flow − s × spring.dropPerYear × y)` under a drought, then
   `min(1, flow' + spring.recoverPerYear × (years − y))` for the dry-free rest of the span; with no drought only the
   recovery applies. Arithmetic is `dmath`'s, rounded to 1/1024. Where the new value differs, one `setAttr` of
   `WaterBody.spring.flow` (author `engine`, cause `rule:spring`); a locked or pinned water body, or one whose `spring.flow`
   carries an edit mark (a hand `setAttr`), is never written and is reported held (`springs.held`, the report's count).
   No draw depends on the spring, so the step is exact and replayable.
3. **Failure and return.** A spring at or below `spring.failFlow` after the step is **failed** — a reading of `flow`,
   stored nowhere: it is chronicled `springFailed` in the epoch it first crosses down and `springRestored` in the epoch
   it crosses back up; its irrigated gardens step by V06 §3.5.3 (4); an aqueduct whose source is the spring's body runs
   dry by V06 §3.19.2 (2) (its source lost) while it is failed and is repaired by §3.19.2 (5)'s rule when it returns.
4. **What follows by itself.** The water pull on the travel-cost field (§4.2 S3 (6)) and the prosperity water term
   (§4.5) weight each spring by its `flow` (item 111; §2.3), so from the next `field` phase the quarters round a weakened
   spring lose prosperity past §4.5's hysteresis and drift down the wealth ladder by its lag (V06 §3.4 (7), §3.8.4), a new
   stage's boundary leans less toward it (V06 §3.6), and S10b's placements and the use phase's re-scores read the lower
   pull — "the quarters around it may decline" — and they recover the same way when the flow returns. Households leave
   only by the population target and the departure matching (V06 §3.5.1); the spring moves no household itself.
5. **Neutral.** With `spring.dropPerYear` 0 and no drought, or with the climate row's water-pull weights at 0 (item 111's
   neutral cells), the step writes nothing and changes nothing (T-V6-70's no-op clause).
