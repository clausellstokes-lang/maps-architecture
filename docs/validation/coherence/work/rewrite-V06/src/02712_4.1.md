### 4.1 Advancing time (`advance`) — T1's time card, the clerk and the CLI

* **Where.** The basic panel's T1 `intend` gains a **time card** (BCR-V6-11): "Advance … years", a shape chip (grow by a
  share, reach a population, decline, hold, follow the timeline — mapped to the trajectory by V06 §2.3's table), an
  optional span, cadence, threat and era row behind "more", a hazards chip (none, minor, full; absent on a dossier-bound
  document), and "start small" at creation (V06 §3.3.3). The clerk's tool API gains `advance`
  (§11.2), and the CLI `plat advance <doc> --years N --to P | --rate r [--cadence w] [--hazards m]`.
* **A standalone neighbourhood document has no clock** (V01 J-V1.36; J-V6.102; the ruled behaviour, CANON-C02). On such a
  document — one district's close-up opened alone, with no S4–S5 growth route — the time card and "start small" are
  absent, the clerk's and the CLI's `advance` bend to none with the line "a neighbourhood alone has no history yet — open
  it in its town to advance time" (item 101 R2; V09's `bend.*` message row and V10's bend row carry it), a skeleton
  file's `history` block is bent to none with that line, and no history replay runs at its generation; `forkAt`, the
  timeline bar over its existing events and the history panel still work. T-V6-23 tests the bend.
* **What it appends.** One `group` (§7.4) of `epoch` events, one per cadence step (V06 §3.3.2), each folded before the
  next is built and each carrying `EpochInputs.advance` (BCR-V6-9); the trajectory rides on the first. The app runs it as
  a cancellable background job in the generation worker with a progress bar over the epochs; **Stop** ends the job after
  the running epoch, and the epochs already folded stand as a shorter group (J-V6.2's refusal on a dossier-bound document
  holds: its host owns time).
* **Preview** (item 101 R3, R14). Before commit, the card and the clerk run the whole request on a **scratch fork in the
  background** (the generation worker, lower priority than the picture), with its progress, and show the **end state**
  against the present (then-and-now, V06 §3.15.3) with a **count badge** — households and buildings gained or lost, the
  walled share, and the raised ways built (by kind) — so a hundred-year advance previews its hundredth year, not its first;
  the history panel lists their `raisedWay` lines. The clerk offers three variations —
  the logistic, linear and front-loaded shapes of the same change (V06 §2.3) — and the card one; accept appends the chosen
  one's epochs to the document (item 101's three-variations rule applied to time). Above `advance.maxEpochs` the card
  bends the cadence and says so (V06 §3.3.2). On a dossier-bound document the card is replaced by its alternative, "Make a
  what-if copy and advance it" (a detached `forkAt` at the head, V06 §4.4).
* **Undo** (§7.4). The group is one undo step while its last epoch is the latest live event: the undo **restores the
  checkpoint taken before the group's first epoch** (the base takes it) and truncates the group in an unshared document —
  so undoing a hundred-epoch group needs neither a hundred undo records nor §7.4's 64-event / 20 MB window. Per-epoch undo
  records are kept for the group's latest epoch only; after the group commits, its epochs' checkpoints are dropped except
  every `history.checkpointStride`-th, which become the history store's keyframes (V06 §3.15.2). An epoch that carried a
  dossier event is never an undo target (§7.4), so a dossier-bound history is undone only by the host.
  **After a save and a reload** the checkpoint and the history store are gone (memory only): the group is still one undo
  step while its last epoch is the latest live event, and its undo rebuilds the state before the group by §7.4's replay
  from the nearest checkpoint or keyframe before the group's first epoch — the verification replay after the load
  rebuilds keyframes — or from the base, and says it is slow ("undoing this history replays the town to year N first");
  the result equals the unsaved undo's digest for digest. **In a shared document or one attached to a host bridge** the
  undo is §7.4's appended `undo` event naming the group's first epoch (`of`), never a truncation: the fold skips the group
  from its checkpoint (or by that replay), and a later `redo` re-applies it. T-V6-23 tests both.
