## Part 1 — a term that leaves law 1 (8) no reading

### PRB7E-GEN-01 — BLOCKER · (a) — *anything across a strip* takes in the raised way's own reservation

**Law or term.** *Anything across a strip*: "whatever is laid on the ground within it, and wet ground: a wall's band or
wall strips, a run that stands, a retaining wall's line, a way's corridor, water, a flood cell or marsh, a square or
plaza, a reservation, a plot or building, a grove, another raised way's support, ramp, stair or foot" (adj l. 308–312;
PRB7D-TIM-03's refinement, adj l. 2692); law 1 (8): "a `vault` span is read `open` while anything lies across its strip
and `vault` while nothing does" (adj l. 363); *a span's strip*, "the deck's plan between its two piers' inner faces" (adj
l. 290–292); §2.7's support step (6)'s lead as (M6) words it (base l. 2565–2567; adj l. 1151–1156).

**Case.** Every CITY or METROPOLIS generation, regeneration and later commit with a span the span rule rules `vault` —
an S4 viaduct's (`span.vaultShare`, V11 l. 3975) or an S6 aqueduct's —, from the span rule's run on (base l. 2729–2748).

**Claim.** (6)'s lead makes "the under-deck strip of each `vault` span — but one the crossing table's *beneath* reads
`open` — … a **reservation** on ground part 0" (base l. 2565–2567 with (M6), adj l. 1152–1153), and the span's strip lies
inside that stretch of the under-deck strip. The term lists "a reservation" with no exception (it says "another raised
way's support, ramp, stair or foot", but not another's reservation), so the raised way's own reservation is "laid on the
ground within" its span's strip. Run as written: a span read `vault` has a reservation across its strip, so law 1 (8)
reads it `open`; read `open`, (M6) takes its strip out of the reservation, nothing lies across, and law 1 (8) reads it
`vault`. No reading satisfies law 1 (8), and E-D13's row ("a `vault` span with anything across its own strip read
`open`, one with nothing across read `vault`", adj l. 1967–1968) returns `E_INVARIANT` at every stage end from the span
rule's run on every map with a `vault` span — unless a builder breaks the loop with an exclusion no text states. The
refinement that added "a reservation" for an S7b claim or a gate market's square laid on a strip while it read `open`
(PRB7D-TIM-03) opened it.

**Amended words (Part L).** Terms, *Anything across a strip*: "**Anything across a strip** is whatever is laid on the
ground within it, and wet ground:" → "**Anything across a strip** is whatever is laid on the ground within it, and wet
ground — never the raised way's own reservation (its supports, its ends and its `vault` spans' under-deck strips, §2.7's
support step (6)), which follows the span's reading and is nothing across —:".

### PRB7E-GEN-05 — MAJOR · (a) — the way a generated road deck carries has no extent

**Law or term.** *Meeting*: a raised way "with its supports, ramps, stairs, feet, spans, arch vaults and the way it
carries (by hand, the declared way to its stroke's end)" (adj l. 269–271); *held*: a lock holds a raised way "with its
parts (… its supports, spans and arch vaults)" (adj l. 316–318); *Carried way*: "a carried way standing before the stage
(a locked road deck's at a regeneration, J147, or a creation prefix's) taking its gate from that stage's gate law at its
crossing beyond the foot (at stage 1 a forced pick of §4.2 S3 (4), at a stage k ≥ 2 a pin of S5a), the way never moved"
(adj l. 405–407); *beyond the foot* ("a road deck's carried way, past its foot"). PRB7D-GEN-07's closure (adj l. 2727),
as J147 amended it, holds for a hand deck and fails for a generated one.

**Case.** Regenerate (keep) or New random of a CITY whose S4 viaduct — a stretch of a `through` route "priced in this
route solve" (base l. 5802–5804) — the user placement-locked; T110-4 (c) draws it ("a road deck, one of the two
placement-locked", adj l. 1687).

**Claim.** A raised way is a `Way` whose deck lives in its own part, its two ends joined to ground vertices (base
l. 2525–2528); a generated viaduct's route goes on over ground part 0 beyond each foot. Part L gives the carried way's
extent only by hand. Builder 1: the generated deck carries the route it was priced on, held with it ("a carried way
standing before the stage"), so the regenerated stage-1 ring takes a forced pick of S3 (4) where that route's ground
course crosses it, one of the `N₁` picks re-placed, and the route never moves. Builder 2: it carries nothing a lock holds
(*held*'s parts) — S3 (5) and S4 re-solve the routes, which may or may not ride the deck (§4.3's route rule, PRB7C-GEN-07,
adj l. 2592), and the gates are S3 (7)'s own. Builder 3: the ground ways from its feet to the nearest junctions stand
with it. Different picks, gates and arterials on every regeneration of a map with a locked generated viaduct. And at

## Verdict

Run as one resolver on the generation and regeneration path, the refined laws decide most meetings uniquely: one order
for law 2, a total tie-break, the band and the strips' width by their derivation, the chamber's timing, the union of
several raised ways, the unwalled stage, the creation prefix's order and every meeting of two locked structures come out
one way, and no op needs a row. They are not yet complete and determinate. One blocker remains: as written, *anything
across a strip* counts the raised way's own reservation, so law 1 (8) has no reading for any `vault` span (GEN-01). Nine
majors remain: the stored order law 1 (3) and the check read disagrees with *Newer* for every line one step fixes and a
later one lays and for every step a stage repeats (GEN-02); *beyond the foot* is a point the laws read as a region
(GEN-03); a regeneration gives a kept, unlocked structure no order (GEN-04) and a locked generated deck no carried way
(GEN-05); law 2 (b) leaves open which parts its search may pass (GEN-06) and what follows a gate fixed to a moat deck
(GEN-07); law 2 (c)'s rung has no reach of its own (GEN-08); the check's clause (10) has no placement leaf for a held
part and no undo record or "named" event on these paths (GEN-09, an invariant unchecked); and a fixed place off its wall's
line has no zone width (GEN-10). None crosses an owner rule — J144, J147, items 110 (b) and (f), 114 and 101 R2 stand as
Part L reads them —, and each closes by one sentence in Part L's terms or laws, by class (one exclusion, one dating rule
with an execution-order step, one point-and-region pair, one regeneration rule, one carried-way extent, one passable set,
one moat-deck clause, one reach, one placement definition, one width rule). Two minors correct examples to the laws
(GEN-11, GEN-12). In all, 10 majors (1 a blocker) remain in the generation and regeneration lens; the laws should be
amended before the family's repair seats write against them.
