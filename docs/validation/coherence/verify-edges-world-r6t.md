## 3. New issues

### VEW-r6-01 — MAJOR · CATALOGUE-GAP (C-wall-raised) · owner pipeline

**A deck crossing that S5a's pull moves onto the arterial's ground course.** At a stage k ≥ 2, S5b routes on the stage's
*picked* boundary, and S5a then lays the trace (§4.1's S5 row, l. 5049: "S5b L1: the main roads, picked on the stage's
boundary … S5a L1: the trace with every arterial crossing on ground part 0 as a gate"). S5a *Trace* (2) (l. 5946–5948)
pulls the boundary "to defensible ground within a 40 m search band (local ridges, slope breaks …)", with the gates
pinned where arterials cross the boundary — "an arterial crossing the boundary on a raised deck pins no gate there".
The pull treats only "a standing raised way's ramps, stairs and feet and an aqueduct's standing supports" as
impassable. So where the picked boundary crosses an S4 or earlier-S5b viaduct's deck within 40 m of a foot, the pull
can move the crossing past that foot onto the arterial's ground course. This is likely, because the foot stands on the
valley's rim, which is the high ground the pull seeks. The pulled trace then crosses that arterial on ground part 0
with no pin and no gate vertex. S5b already ran and counted that arterial out of `c` (l. 5996–5997: "a crossing on a
raised deck no gate"), so `N_k − c` primaries were spawned without it.

The texts now disagree:
* §4.1 makes the crossing a gate ("every arterial crossing on ground part 0 as a gate"), which gives `N_k + 1` gates.
* §4.8 *Gates* says "exactly N, by construction" (l. 7729), and F-WALL's B-CITY bar says "main gates = N exactly; 0
  non-gate crossings" (l. 9203).
* F-WALL's ladder reads the crossing as "a non-gate way reaches the trace" and would "divert excess arterials to the
  nearest gate as lanes". That would downgrade an S4 arterial that §4.4 says is "never trimmed" (l. 6055).
* *beneath*'s "(a way S5a pins no gate for meets the trace as anywhere on ground part 0)" (l. 2627) does not say which of
  these applies to an arterial.

One builder ships `N_k + 1` gates, another diverts the arterial, a third keeps the pull off the ground course. T110-2
(3)'s own fixture is this case: "a CITY c14 fixture whose S4 viaduct over a valley is crossed by stage 2's moated ring
… the arterial on the deck pinning no gate" (l. 18555–18561). It asserts no gate count against the pull. The same holds
at every epoch stage (S5b runs "for every epoch stage", V06 §3.7.1).

**Required change.** §4.4 S5a *Trace* (2), after "an arterial crossing the boundary on a raised deck pins no gate there,"
add: "and the pull keeps that crossing beneath its deck — that arterial's ground course beyond each foot of the deck,
within the search band, impassable to the pull as the deck's ramps, stairs and feet are — so the pulled trace crosses
each arterial as the picked boundary did: on ground part 0 at its pin, or under its deck (§2.7's crossing table,
*beneath*), and the stage's gates are its pins (`N_k`, §4.4 S5b),". The second end is scope's: in §13.2 T110-2 (3),
after "the arterial on the deck pinning no gate and S5b's count read on ground part 0", add "— and a variant whose
picked boundary crosses that deck within the pull's search band of the valley's rim, the pulled trace crossing it under
the deck and never on its ground course beyond the foot, the stage's gates exactly `N_k`, 0 non-gate crossings".

### VEW-r6-02 — MAJOR · CONFLICT / EDGE-UNDEFINED (C-wall-raised × C-raised-lock × V04) · owner model

repair-r6-V04 filed this as its one open hand-off, "left open for a ruling; nothing written for it" (assemble-r6 §2.1).

### VEW-r6-03 — MAJOR · CATALOGUE-GAP (C-wall-raised × barrier closure, item 106) · owner model

**A barrier anchor, or a weld vertex, inside a raised way's under-deck strip.** *beneath* moves every tower out of the
strip: "*No gate and no tower in the under-deck strip* — one the wall's law would place there moves to the nearest
point clear of it: a tower along the trace (S7's towers beside a trace an aqueduct at S6's end crossed among them,
*over the band*)" (l. 2645–2648). The barrier-closure rule puts a tower at each anchor:
* §4.4 S5a (1): "each stretch's ends anchored on its barrier … with a tower at each anchor (§4.8)" (l. 5930), item 106
  (a)'s own words.
* §4.8 *Towers*: "and at every barrier anchor (item 106 (a))" (l. 7744).
* `Enceinte.anchors`: "typed end anchors of the open stretches, a tower at each (§4.8)" (l. 2255).
* `PreviewResult.anchors`: "each anchor tower's point" (l. 16381).
* T-V6-47's bar: "its stretches end on tower anchors" (V06 l. 4170).

An anchor is the stretch's end, so a tower moved "along the trace" leaves its anchor bare. *over the band* keeps an
aqueduct's strip clear of "the trace's gates and towers" (l. 2600). At generation, though, an aqueduct at S6's end is
solved before S7 lays any tower, and the rule moves only "a tower its spacing would place in the strip" (l. 2601–2603).

The case is common on gorge and river-neck sites, where one feature is both the closing barrier and the viaduct's
reason. A town on a spur closes its gorge side by the barrier (anchors on the gorge's rim), and its S4 viaduct crosses
that gorge where the road leaves. The viaduct's under-deck strip covers the rim where stage 1's trace, laid later at
S5a, anchors. One builder moves the anchor's tower inward, against item 106 (a)'s words and the bars. Another keeps it
under the deck. A third re-anchors the stretch.

**Required change.**
* In *beneath*, after "a tower along the trace", add: "— never a barrier anchor's or a weld vertex's (item 106 (a), (f);
  §4.8 *Towers*). A trace laid after a raised way takes no anchor or weld vertex inside its under-deck strip: the
  `EnceinteStrategy` anchors the stretch at the nearest point of its barrier edge (or enceinte) clear of the strip, and
  a stretch laid by neither is bent locally there (by hand a fate in `bend.wall.raisedCrossing`'s line), so every anchor
  keeps its tower —".
* In *over the band*, "its under-deck strip keeps clear of the trace's gates and towers" → "its under-deck strip keeps
  clear of the trace's gates and towers, and at generation, before S7 lays them, of its barrier anchors and weld
  vertices".
* The second end is scope's: §13.2 T110-2 (3) adds "a gorge-side CITY whose S4 viaduct crosses the gorge at the rim
  where stage 1's open stretch anchors — the stretch anchored on the rim clear of the under-deck strip, its anchor tower
  standing; and (1)'s aqueduct laid at S6's end with its course near a ring anchor — its strip clear of the anchor".

### VEW-r6-04 — MAJOR · CONFLICT (C-wall-raised × C-raised-lock at an epoch) · owner V06

**An epoch's founding across a drawn or locked wall.** V06 §3.19 (5) (V06 l. 2815–2822) solves a founded course with
"Locked, pinned and declared entities, dossier-listed buildings, every other raised way's supports, enceinte towers and
gates … **impassable**, filtered before any draw". In the same sentence it says "a course meeting a standing curtain
meets it by §2.7's crossing table — an aqueduct *over the band*". The crossing table's row W2 (a hand `drawWall`) sends
a raised way created later to W1·C1, which is *over the band* "or V06's founding", with "nothing of the wall … written"
(l. 2603, 2686–2690).

Every drawn wall is "locked by default" and every declared entity is held (§7.2, l. 10810–10820). So on any document
whose user drew or locked a ring, two readings apply:
* V06 makes the ring impassable to an epoch aqueduct, which then never finds a course. Its terminal lies "inside the
  outermost enceinte" (V06 §3.19 (6)), so it waits (`raised.waited`) at every epoch.
* The table crosses that ring on a span, writing nothing of it.

The same list makes a drawn ground way impassable to a founded deck that would pass over it, while §7.4's raised-way row
lets a deck pass over a locked way ("a raised way over a locked way keeps its span `open`", l. 11447). This is common: a
user who draws the walls and runs epochs at CITY never gets an aqueduct from one builder, and gets one from another.
VDC6-01 (determinism) is the hand-edit sibling at §7.1's lock row. This one is the epoch path, a different home.

**Required change.** V06 §3.19 (5): "Locked, pinned and declared entities, dossier-listed buildings, every other raised
way's supports, enceinte towers and gates, and V05's `skyAccessObstacles` (V05 §2.3b) are **impassable**" → "Locked,
pinned and declared entities, dossier-listed buildings, every other raised way's supports, enceinte towers and gates,
and V05's `skyAccessObstacles` (V05 §2.3b) are **impassable** to the course's supports, ramps, stairs and feet and to its
clearing ((7)). A course passes over a locked, pinned or declared ground way on a span, as over any way (item 244's
clearance), and meets a locked, pinned or declared wall by §2.7's crossing table, as an unlocked one (an aqueduct *over
the band*, a road deck *down before the wall*), nothing of either written". The second end is scope's: §13.2 T110-2
(1)'s founding clause adds "and the same founding across a ring drawn by hand (locked by its drawing, §7.2), *over the
band*, no wait", with V06's T-V6-73 founding clause adding that run.
