## Part 1 — law 2's resolver by hand

### PRB7E-LCK-01 — MAJOR (a) — law 2 (a)'s reach, written as a direction, admits no local re-lay, which law 3 ranks and every hand bend makes

**Law or term.** Law 2 (a)'s reach, refined for PRB7D-LCK-02 (adj l. 411–419); *the reach* of a meeting (adj l. 325–326);
law 3's keys (1) and (2) (adj l. 443–452); the bent-group sentence's (i)–(iii) (adj l. 985–995); row W14 and veto 18
(adj l. 1110, 585–587); E-D2 (adj l. 1877–1882) and T110-2 (1) (base l. 18547–18548); E-S4's drawn-along-beneath run
(adj l. 1575–1578).

**Case.** Three common hand gestures. (1) A hand `drawWall` drawn along beneath a standing aqueduct through two of its
supports (the bent group's (ii); E-S4 runs it). (2) A hand T4 aqueduct stroke whose straight course puts a ring tower's
footprint under its strip, a lawful course a few metres aside (E-D2; T110-2 (1)'s `bend.wall.aqueductArch`). (3) A hand
`setAttr` re-grading a stretch of a drawn wall `light` → `full` whose wider band would meet a pier (W14).

**Claim.** Law 2 (a): "The newer structure is laid lawfully first, by its own derivation and within its own reach — each
part it changes moved along its own line or tie only to the meeting's nearest clear points, within the bound its own law
gives it (…) and never past its line's ends (…): … an op given its shape — a stroke, a moved vertex or grip, a
re-section, a placed or slid gate, a drawn course, … — takes the lawful shape law 3 picks nearest it; (a) fails where
none lies within that reach" (adj l. 411–419). Read as a **direction** — each changed part moves along its own line or
along a tie — the admissible changes are a gate, postern or tower along its wall's line, a fixed place along its tie, a
crossing along the deck line it crosses, a station along its deck and an end drawn back along its own line. None of the
three cases' lawful shapes is of that kind: (1) replaces the part along beneath "by that side's edge between its entry
and exit points" (adj l. 992–993), its points moving across the strip, not along the wall's line; (2) moves the
aqueduct's course sideways off the tower; (3) is "re-traced locally where it would not" cross lawfully (W14, adj
l. 1110; veto 18, adj l. 585–587). Each finds no shape "within that reach", so (a) fails: (b) moves the tower in case (2)
(today), finds nothing movable on a raised way in (1) and (3), and (c) refuses, `refuse.raised.meeting`. Read as a
**bound** — each change no further than the meeting's nearest clear points — the edge path, the bent course and the local
re-trace are admitted and law 3 chooses among them, as law 3 itself expects: its key (1) ranks "one that keeps the op's
own course or trace, or bends it only locally, before one that re-lays it (which an op does only where it names that
course or trace — a stroke, a re-shape, a drawn course)", and its key (2) measures "a re-laid line or course by the
largest distance of the new from the old" (adj l. 445–452) — bends and re-lays the direction reading never admits. The
bound list itself names searches ("the pull's search band", "a siting's candidates"), which move nothing along a line.
The examples and tests take the bound reading — the bent group's (i)–(iii), W14, veto 18, E-D2's "its course bent clear
of that wall's towers and gates", T110-2 (1)'s bent stroke, E-S4's "each such part moved to the strip's edge on its entry
side" — but the laws govern the examples, and the laws' own clauses point both ways. Two builds on the commonest hand
paths: refused, or bent; in case (2) also two maps (the tower moved by (b), or the stroke bent). Not a re-raise: the
direction words are PRB7D-LCK-02's refinement, which bounded a given shape's reach and in doing so constrained its
direction. *J148*: with (b)'s tower moves withdrawn, the direction reading refuses every hand aqueduct whose straight
stroke meets a tower — the commonest aqueduct gesture.

**Amended words (Part L).** Law 2 (a): "within its own reach — each part it changes moved along its own line or tie only
to the meeting's nearest clear points, within the bound its own law gives it" → "within its own reach — each thing it
moves moved along its own line or tie (a gate, postern or tower along its wall's line, a fixed place along its tie, a
crossing along the line it crosses, a station along its deck, an end back along its own line), and each stretch of its
own line or course it changes re-laid only between the nearest points clear of the strips, zones and ends it meets on
either side (a local re-lay: the bent group's moves, a re-section's local re-trace, a drawn course bent clear of a gate
or tower), every change only as far as the meeting's nearest clear points and within the bound its own law gives it";
and law 3's key (1): "or bends it only locally" → "or re-lays it only locally (law 2 (a))".

## Verdict

Within the locks and hand edits lens the laws are not yet complete and determinate: **six majors remain, no blocker,
and three minors.** The pass-4 refinements closed the classes they were written for — law 2's order, law 3's total
order, (G2)'s one encoding, an undo exact or refused for a re-created meeting, the stored order of lines, one commit —
and none of pass 4's locks findings is re-raised. But three refinements opened new ambiguities: (a)'s reach, worded as a

list takes in a span's own reservation and the building seated in its chamber, so law 1 (8) has no fixed point and law 1

their undo, one of them against item 110 (b) (PRB7E-LCK-04); its *Carried way* bend through a gate the wall has lies
outside (a)'s reach (PRB7E-LCK-03); and law 1 (9)'s lock order splits on a lock made live again by an undo or a redo
(PRB7E-LCK-05). Each closes with the one sentence given; none reopens J144 or J147; the three minors are an END and
examples corrected to the laws (E-D13 not conformed to J147; a seventh correction for examples that hold a lock to
placement alone; the held-building example's strip and its bar on the raised way's own edit).
