## Part 1 — the order of law 2: (a), (b), (c)

### PRB7D-LCK-01 — MAJOR (a) — law 2 bends the newer first; correction 1 and veto 6 move the standing wall's gate or tower first

**Law or term.** Law 2 (a)–(b) (adj l. 316–324); Part L's correction 1 (adj l. 372–374); the restated veto list's item 6
(adj l. 407–410); J146's method (PROGRESS l. 205).

**Case.** A hand aqueduct (T4 `road`, Raised, class `aqueduct`) drawn across a standing wall — generated, drawn or locked —
whose straight course would put one of the wall's towers (or a gate) under its strip, a lawful course a few metres aside.
The same for a hand re-shape of an aqueduct toward a tower (C13) and, on the generation lens's path, S6's aqueduct beside
a gate.

**Claim.** Law 2 orders the resolution: "(a) The newer structure is laid lawfully first … an op given its shape — a stroke
… — takes the lawful shape law 3 picks nearest it. (b) Failing that, the standing structure's shape changes inside the
meeting's reach (G1) …: its gates …, posterns and towers move along its line" (adj l. 316–323). So the stroke is bent
clear of the tower and the wall is untouched. Part L's own correction 1 reverses it for every example: "Wherever an example
has a newer structure **bent**, dropped, refused or waiting because a standing wall's gate, postern or tower (not a fixed
place) lies where law 1 bars it, law 2 (b) **first** moves that gate, postern or tower along its wall inside the meeting's
reach, and the example's outcome follows only where (b) finds no lawful point" (adj l. 372–374). Veto 6 says the same as
the program's behaviour, the bend being the vetoable alternative: "a raised way built later moves a standing wall's gate or
tower along the wall rather than give way. *Veto option:* such a drawing refused, and such a raised way bent or not built"
(adj l. 407–410). The examples correction 1 rewrites are exactly this case: E-D2's "its course bent clear of that wall's
towers and gates" (adj l. 1690; live base l. 10486) and T110-2 (1)'s "a hand aqueduct drawn across a standing wall, its
course bent clear of a tower, with `bend.wall.aqueductArch`" (base l. 18547–18548; E-S1, adj l. 1342–1343). Builder A
follows law 2 — the stroke bent (`bend.wall.aqueductArch`), the wall unchanged, nothing to confirm; builder B follows
correction 1 and veto 6 — the tower moved along the wall (a held wall's fate *reshaped*, `towerMoved`), the stroke
straight. Two maps and two fate lists on a common hand path. The method the owner chose orders it as law 2 does: "the op
creating or changing the meeting reshapes its own structure first; failing that, the standing structure's local shape
only" (J146, PROGRESS l. 205).

### PRB7D-LCK-02 — MAJOR (a) — a given shape has no "own reach", so (a) never fails by hand — or fails where the examples say

**Law or term.** Law 2 (a)'s "within its own reach (the pull's search band, the support step's slide bound, the strip's
edges, its siting's candidates)" (adj l. 316–318); law 3 (adj l. 340–350); the fixed-place sentence (adj l. 862–865);
E-S12 (adj l. 1509–1511).

**Case.** A hand `drawWall` whose end snaps to a shore point inside a standing viaduct's fixed-place zone, the nearest
clear shore point lying beyond `wall.barrierReachM` (a viaduct along the shore). Likewise a hand aqueduct stroke whose
nearest lawful course lies far off the drawn line.

**Claim.** Law 2 (a) bounds the newer's lawful shape "within its own reach", but every reach it names belongs to a search
(the pull's band, the slide bound, the strip's edges, the siting's candidates); for "an op given its shape" it says only
"takes the lawful shape law 3 picks nearest it", and law 3 has no bound. Read so, the anchor moves along the shore to the
nearest clear point however far, a stroke always has a lawful shape, and (b) and (c) never run for a stroke. The examples
bound it: "with no such point within the trace's reach (its search band; `wall.barrierReachM` for an anchor), the trace
takes (G3) (… `refuse.raised.meeting` by hand)" (adj l. 862–865), and E-S12 runs this very case as a refusal: "a hand

`refuse.raised.meeting`" (adj l. 1510–1511). Builder A moves the anchor 300 m along the shore (law 3), builder B refuses
(the bound). The same split decides whether law 2 (b) is ever reached by hand (PRB7D-LCK-01).

**Amended words (Part L).** Law 2 (a), after "an op given its shape — … a warp's result — takes the lawful shape law 3

### PRB7D-LCK-03 — MAJOR (a) — law 3's no-effect clause sends the op to (c) past law 2 (b)

Within the locks and hand edits lens the laws are not yet complete and determinate. Law 2's order is stated three ways —
