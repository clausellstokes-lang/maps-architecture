## The items

**992.** **A mill has a power source — water, wind or muscle — and only the water mill is bound to water.** The grain mill is one institution with three lawful forms: the **water mill** (as designed: a hard need of running water with fall, its leat or race, its weir and pond), the **windmill** (wind-powered; a form in its own right, no longer only the fallback of a settlement without a mill seat), and the **muscle mill** (worked by hand or by animals — the owner's "manual" — "in a field with work livestock"). The sentence "a watermill on water" stays true of the water mill and stops being true of "a mill". *The session's reading of the owner's last clause, vetoable:* the three sources are alternatives, and "in a field with work livestock" is the muscle mill's siting condition as the watercourse is the water mill's.

**993.** **The two siting conditions the owner's word opens — the session's proposals, vetoable.** (1) *The windmill.* Today its hard need is `highGround` (terrain class `high`). A windmill needs exposure, which high ground gives but open flat ground gives too; the design already carries wind (`Terrain.wind`, `windOf(doc)`, `ContextVector.upwind`; volume 13's climate raster). PROPOSED: the windmill's hard need is *exposure* — high ground, or open ground with nothing taller upwind within a stated distance — so a mill may stand on a plain. (2) *The muscle mill.* "In a field with work livestock" needs a notion the design lacks. PROPOSED, the smallest form: a holding or field system carries a flag for work stock (plough teams, draught animals) derived from the settlement's agrarian character and the host's data where it has any; the muscle mill's hard need is such a holding, and its form is a mill house with a walking circle at the yard or the field's edge (a hand mill where the holding is small). A muscle mill inside a town's walls (a maltster's or a brewer's horse mill) is not opened by the owner's words and is not proposed.
