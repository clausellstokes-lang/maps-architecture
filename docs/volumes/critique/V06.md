## 0 Verdict in plain words

Volume 6 is the most complete design of time in this program, and its core should stay. A town can be given a future,
year by year, from the same seed. When it grows it fills its own empty houses and ruins before it spreads. When it shrinks
the exposed edges empty first, houses lose their roofs and are robbed for stone, and plots turn to gardens. Old walls
become ring roads over decades, and a shrunken town may build a shorter wall. Shops close where their customers left and
open where people now live. A time bar lets a user scrub through a century and see what stood where in any year. The
engine is careful about the things the owner cares most about: nothing is ever re-rolled, anything a user holds is left
alone, and a SettlementForge town has only the disasters its dossier reports.

It cannot be built as it stands, for three kinds of reasons.

First, one of its base changes would break something that already works. Before this engine exists, the base's simple
"add a growth stage" path is what runs a SettlementForge campaign's yearly steps. The volume rewrites that path as four
phases and drops the step that applies the dossier's changes. After that change, dossier events that arrive with a
yearly step would be lost. That breaks the owner's rule that the dossier is the one truth.

Second, the owner's rules filed after the volume landed (items 105–109, including the refinements the owner approved at
~19:55) have no mechanism here, and the volume is where their "over time" half lives:
* Caves and ancient ruins must become reachable as a town grows and builds over them, or as its sewers grow into them.
  People may live in them only once there is a way in.
* Catacombs lie only under cemeteries, and in a big city also under an old quarry turned into a bone store. They must
  grow slowly and never shrink, and when they cannot grow they fill up instead. Churches get crypts instead. The
  cathedral always has the largest crypt, even after the bishop's seat moves to another church.
* Supply links must re-bind to the nearest supplier when shops open and close.
* Everyday services must spread out rather than bunch together.
* SettlementForge's "5–10 blacksmiths" must become that many buildings, and the count must rise and fall with the
  population.
* New walls must use cliffs and the sea, and must never cut a town off from its harbour.

Worse, two of the volume's own rules already break these owner rules. Its shop-counting formula would open extra
blacksmiths beside the dossier's "blacksmiths" record, which is double counting. And its shorter-wall rule could wall a
shrinking port off from its quay.

Third, when a town crosses from TOWN to CITY in the middle of a history, nothing turns its sewers on. The owner said
sewers are a necessity for cities. The same crossing between the village and town products is also blocked: the volume
clamps every history at 1 500 people, so a village cannot grow into a town.

The rest are real but ordinary repairs:
* Households moved by fires, falling islands or sealed caves need a typed hand-over between the steps of an epoch.
* The growth rules quietly change the base's "fill first" order, and they need their base change requests.
* The shade from new buildings does not update exactly when houses are still being built.
* The "why did this move" answers vanish after a reload or a rebase.
* Scrubbing the time bar can show a road that was not there yet.
* Several tests cannot pass as written. One of them sets the threat level to exactly the value at which the rule keeps
  every wall.
* The metropolis budgets are missing.

None of this needs the owner. The one owner question (when to build it) stands, with a larger price: about 23–31
person-weeks rather than 17.5–23.5.

## 2 Findings

### 2.1 Blockers

**C6-01 — blocker — buildability and owner fidelity (item 20; BCR-V6-1; T-V6-1). The base change that turns the grow
path into phases drops the dossier step.**

The problem is in BCR-V6-1's new text and in V6 §3.1 (2), which both say: "`'grow-by-stage'` registers the phases
`demand`, `field`, `siting` and `growth` and reproduces the path above byte for byte". The runner "for each `EpochStep` in
order" runs "the strategy's phase for that step" (V6 §3.1 (4)), so a step with no phase runs nothing.

The base's epoch does much more than those four phases (§7.4's epoch row; §4.11 *The epoch's order*):
* before S2, it reads the rung and the dials;
* it applies `EpochInputs.dossier` at step (e);
* it re-checks every rejection by the lapse rule at (h);
* it runs the character step at (i);
* it starts and retires Works at (j).

§4.11's *Test (M4)* binds "the Works', the clock's and the character step's" hook tests to M4, and V08 §3.6 relies on
exactly this: "before it lands, a catch-up epoch runs the base's built growth path and hooks".

If BCR-V6-1 is applied as written, a dossier-bound document without the engine silently drops every dossier event an
epoch carries (item 20: the dossier is the sole truth). It also never lapses a rejection, never renames a district and
never shows a Works. T-V6-1 then cannot hold, because M4's goldens contain those steps.

*Required change:*
(1) BCR-V6-1's sentence becomes: "`'grow-by-stage'` registers the phases `prelude`, `demand`, `field` (without due marks
and without the building mask), `dossier`, `siting`, `growth`, `lapse`, `character` and `works` — the base's steps before
S2, S2, (a), (e), (f), (g), (h), (i) and (j) — and reproduces the path above byte for byte."
(2) The base's phases are built once, in `@plat/edit`. The `'history'` strategy reuses the same phase objects for the
steps V6 §3.2 marks "base" (`prelude`'s base part, `demand`, `dossier`, `lapse`, `character`), so both strategies share
one code path.
(3) State the runner's rule for a step with no phase: nothing runs. Extend T-V6-26 so that every registered strategy has
a phase for every step the base's order (a)–(j) names.
(4) T-V6-1 gains a dossier-bound fixture: a catch-up epoch carrying two dossier events under `'grow-by-stage'` applies
both, with a digest equal to the M4 golden of the same log.
(5) J-V6.1 gains the rule.

*Required change:* add a new V6 §3.18.2, "Catacombs and crypts over epochs". It runs inside `under.extend` at
`afterGrowth` (order 10, C6-02 (1)), and the crypt rank ((8) 3) runs at the end of the batch. The base-side rules are
V04's, V02's and V11's: the candidates, the need, the `CatacombComplex` record, the crypt ladder and its size row
(C4-49 and C4-50 as revised under J111; V2C-18 (b1)–(b4)). V6 calls them and owns only their epoch half.

(1) **Candidates and the count, at each epoch.**
* After `dossier` and `siting`, the candidates are ranked in 109 (k) (3)'s order.
  * First come the cemeteries, by the burials each has served. This is II.7.6's need measure over the population
    history, so it is cumulative: an old ground keeps its rank until a newer one has buried more. At METROPOLIS the
    Cemetery network's grounds (V08) come before the others.
  * Then, at CITY and METROPOLIS only, a quarry working that V04 marks worked-out, as an ossuary.
* A cemetery is one of four kinds:
  * a churchyard (the churchyard part of a church, temple or cathedral plot, §4.9's row);
  * a burial-ground use;
  * a monastery's burial face;
  * an extramural cemetery.
* These are never candidates: a church building, a crypt, a religious precinct without burial ground, and a sky-arena
  plot (C5-02 (3a)).
* The allowed count is re-read from the rung at the epoch's target and the church count (109 (k) (3)'s rows). With a
  dossier, the count comes from its church records and ranges (item 108's members).
* A candidate that the count admits beyond the existing complexes draws its source chance once, keyed (source lineage,
  `catacomb`) on `sewer`. The lineage is the cemetery's:
  * for a churchyard, its plot's churchyard part;
  * for a burial ground, its use;
  * for a quarry ossuary, the working.

  The draw is never repeated. A refusal is final for that source, and a new source draws once when it first qualifies.
* The count bounds **foundations**, not survivals (J-V6.52, J-V6.64). No complex is founded while the standing complexes
  fill the count, and a fall in churches or in rung removes none. A CITY that declines to TOWN with two complexes keeps
  both, reported `catacombsAboveCount`.

(2) **Age.**
* A cemetery's age at an epoch is (the epoch's week − the week its burials began) ÷ 52, plus its generation age. The
  week its burials began is its use's `opened.epoch`, or, for a churchyard, the church use's. The generation age is
  II.7.6's stage age at creation, or the port's `HistoryHint.ageYears`.
* A quarry ossuary's age is the age of the oldest cemetery whose bones it takes (C4-49's feed). The gate measures old
  bones, not the quarry.

(3) **Growth per epoch.**
* The need is recomputed from the population history: the epochs' targets.
* The target extent is max(current extent, need).
* The added area is ≤ max(extent × ((1 + `catacomb.growthCapPerYear`)^years − 1), `growthFloorM2`), through `dmath.pow`.
  The cap is therefore keyed to the epoch's span and does not depend on the cadence. `growthCapPerYear` is 0.01 (own,
  calibratable). A plague condition's burials enter the need, never the cap.

(3a) **The largest complex across epochs.**
* The **primary** complex at an epoch is the one whose cemetery has served the most burials, ties by source address. It
  takes the larger burial weight of C4-49's share rule.
* Every other complex grows only while its extent stays below `catacomb.rankShare` (C4-49's 0.9) × the primary's.
* That rank cap stops growth and never reduces an extent, because of the ratchet. A held complex is stagnant with
  `blockedBy: 'rank'`, and its unmet need goes to its fill.
* When a newer cemetery overtakes, it becomes the primary. In a growing city this is usually the main cemetery outside
  the walls. It then grows by its full cap each epoch until it is the largest (J-V6.65).

(4) **The timeline invariant, which V6 owns.** Along every log, after every batch, each complex's `extentFloorM2` (C4-50)
never decreases.
* It binds: every epoch; every dossier event; Regenerate (keep settings); a Re-derive from any stage; New random on the
  catacomb system; an upgrade's replay; a migration. Each of these re-derives the complex from its floor, never below it.
* It does not bind:

**C6-04 — blocker — owner fidelity (item 105 (a)–(j); V6 §3.9.5–§3.9.6, J-V6.25). The supply web over epochs is absent,
and two of V6's rules contradict it.**

The volume has no item 105 content, and two of its own mechanisms conflict with the item.

**(i) The opening order.** V6 §3.9.6 opens deficits "in flow order — the topological order of the classes' `SupplyEdge`s,
producers before consumers, a cycle broken by class id". Item 105 (f) orders placement differently: "Anchors first …;

**(iii) Missing parts.** These have no epoch rule:

The visiting order stays address order. J-V6.25 is restated: the result does not depend on visiting order except

(2) **Openings in 105 (f)'s order**, replacing V6 §3.9.6's "flow order":
1. anchors, fixed;
2. field-driving classes;
3. source-oriented classes, by depth;
4. balanced classes;
5. market-oriented classes.
