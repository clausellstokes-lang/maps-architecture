## 0 Verdict in plain words

Volume 1 is a thorough and mostly buildable design. Its core ideas are sound and should stay. Each district picks a
street layout from what it will hold and where it lies: most keep the organic lanes, and some get a main street, a
square, rings, a quayside or a street round their church. Blocks become houses, a great house, a square, a green or a
paved yard by quotas that follow the district's population and wealth. Opening a district only adds wells, trees and
fountains and never changes the town, and each layout card is an undoable edit of one district.

Two things in it break the owner's rules and must change before it is built.

1. Several edits are refused where the owner's authoring rule says the tool must bend them and say so in one line.
   Turning a block into a square when the neighbours are full, typing a value past its range, and drawing a lone
   neighbourhood too small are all refused today. The owner's rule allows a refusal only for a dossier fact or a lock.
2. The layout is chosen after the district's households have already been counted against its land. A square or ring
   layout then takes land those households were promised, and no rule moves them to another district. Some people would
   be left without a home, which breaks the rule that population decides the town.

The rest are repairs. The volume was written before the owner's rules of 28 September (items 102–109), so it has none
of their neighbourhood parts:
- sunlight and shade,
- the siting rulebook's "one per neighbourhood" coverage,
- supply chains and ladder quarters owning their buildings,
- walls that end on cliffs and marsh,
- cave mouths, sinkholes and ruin entrances as named places in the close-up,
- catacomb doors as named places too. The owner has now put catacombs only under cemeteries, while a church, and always
  the cathedral, keeps a crypt instead. The usual site is a cemetery outside the walls, which no close-up can open today,
  and a rebuilt district could move a catacomb's churchyard away from it.

Several steps also read things that do not exist yet when they run. A church's front door is read before any church is
placed. Street names read squares that are named only after the streets. The floating-island hand-over that volume 5
built for this volume goes unread. The volume duplicates squares the base already makes, and it can create market
squares that break the market rules.

Against watabou's Neighbourhood, the volume reaches most capabilities and improves several. It still over-plants wells
and fountains by an order of magnitude, spaces roof hatching in screen pixels so that the stroke count changes with zoom,
and does not name a district after its layout as Neighbourhood does. None of the fixes needs a new owner decision beyond
the one the volume already asks (Q-V1-1).

Counts: 2 blockers, 36 majors, 25 minors (63 findings, V1C-01 … V1C-63).

## 2 Blockers

### V1C-01 — blocker — owner fidelity (item 101 R2), casual-user UX — four dry runs refuse where the owner's rule says bend

**Where:** V1-4.1's "dry-run refusals" column, V1-3.13 F-UNIT, T-V1-20.
**Defect:** item 101 R2 (owner rule): "The tool bends a declaration to the nearest lawful one and says so in one gentle
line; it refuses only what item 20 forbids (dossier facts) and what a lock protects". The volume refuses four other things:
(a) a block's content edit when "the district's other blocks cannot house B's households at their bands" — refused with
closest options; (b) a `fabricParams` value "outside the row's `range`"; (c) `pinGate` "outside [0, 2]"; (d) a lone
neighbourhood whose outline is "below one block's area", refused at its dry run (F-UNIT, and T-V1-20 bars the refusal).
A block-content edit to `manor` in a district with no top-band household is also undefined (it would fail S10a).
**Failure scenario:** a casual user drags the "square" chip onto a block in a full poor quarter and gets a refusal
dialog, which R2 forbids.
**Required change:** (a) the content edit's dry run bends. It re-homes the displaced households by S10a's re-homing order
(§4.5 (5)): first to the district's other blocks with the join maximum lowered one step (denser plots), then to the first
district in S7b's order whose capacity holds them. It names the move in one line ("14 households move to the
Weavers' quarter"). A `manor` edit with no top-band household in reach bends to the largest residential class the band
range admits ("typed a large house: no household of the rich band lives here"). Refusal stays only for a locked,
pinned or dossier-backed plot on B, with `rejectSite` offered. (b) Values clamp to the row's `range` with the one-line
bend. (c) `pinGate` clamps to [0, 2]. (d) An outline below `4 × eLo²` is scaled up about its centroid to that area, and
the bend is named. Rewrite T-V1-18 and T-V1-20 accordingly, and add T-V1-40: in B-EDIT's random sequences, 0 refusals
other than dossier-owned and lock cases, and every bend named in one line.

### V1C-02 — blocker — buildability, owner fidelity (population decides; §7.7 item 2) — the template's land is taken after S7b has counted households against it

**Where:** V1-3.1's stage order (S7b → S7c), V1-3.4.5 (the plaza), V1-3.4.4 (ring courses), V1-3.4.3/3.4.6 (spines,
fans), V1-3.5.2 (`squaresM2`), V1-3.5.9 and BCR-4 (b), BCR-8, T-V1-12.
**Defect:**
- S7b (1) allocates each district's households by its capacity before S7c chooses the fabric. BCR-4 (b) subtracts only
  the dispatch's expected land: `Q_sq + Q_gr`, the yard expectation and a manor's lost capacity.
- A template then takes built land nobody planned for:
  - the plaza's `A`, clamped up to `maxShareOfFace ×` the template face and × `plazaScale` up to 2;
  - every seed corridor at `w_tpl`: the main spine, the cross pair, the through streets, up to six ring courses, the fans;
  - a void square's corner claims, which `squaresM2` does not even count — so S9b types a full `Q_sq` of square blocks
    as well.
- The bent-cut regime's plot share (§4.2 S2's `plotShare`) does not cover this land.
- S10b's reconcile "never reaches into another district" (§4.7), and λ_b + 0.05 (V1-3.5.9's fallback) is one town-wide
  constant that cannot follow a per-district loss.
- So the households a `square`, `crossroads`, `bridgehead` or `ring` district cannot hold go to F-SITING. They stay
  unplaced, and the population is no longer whole: the owner's "the actual size of the town … is determined by
  population", §7.7 item 2, and T-V1-12's own bar.
- S7c cannot simply move before S7b, because χ̃ is computed from S7b's allocation.

**Required change:**
1. Add to `TemplateRow` a land estimator: `templateLandM2(D, row)` = the plaza's `A` (V1-3.4.5 (a)) + Σ over seeds of
   the expected course length × `w_tpl`'s band top. Spine: `faceObb.majorM`. Ring: Σ 2π r_k over the radius law's
   expected series inside `inscribedRadiusM`. Fan: portals × half `faceObb.minorM`. A void square: its `A`. `bent-cut`
   is 0.
2. Place S7c **inside** S7b (1). S7b's greedy pass runs first; S7c draws each district's fabric on χ̃ over that greedy
   allocation; then S7b's existing bounded reconcile (≤ 3 rounds) subtracts `templateLandM2` from the district's capacity
   and moves the excess households to other districts by its declared order. S7c becomes a callback S7b calls between
   its greedy pass and its reconcile, not a stage after it. Rewrite BCR-3 (a) and BCR-4 (b) to say so.
3. `squaresM2` (V1-3.5.2) counts a void square's `A` and a template plaza's area, and `Q_sq` is floored at 0 after both.
4. A template whose requirement fails after the reconcile (its face changed by a moved claim) bends by F-TEMPLATE; the
   draw is keyed, never re-drawn.
5. T-V1-12 is reported per template class: 0 households unplaced in each class on B-CITY, and the population within its
   band with every template share forced to 100 % in turn (a stress fixture).
6. Pre-decided fallback, if χ̃ on the greedy pass misses T-V1-2's bar against χ̃ on the reconciled allocation: S7c
   keeps its greedy-pass choice and the reconcile still re-homes.
