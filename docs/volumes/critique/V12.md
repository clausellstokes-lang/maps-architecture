## 0 Verdict in plain words

Volume 12 is the measuring room of the whole program: how we measure the other generators and our own towns with the
same ruler, how a number that is out of line gets tuned (by changing a table value, never the code), how the owner's
eye is recorded at each milestone, and how the historical maps will later adjust the defaults without replacing what we
built. Its big choices are right and should stay: one implementation of every measurement run on every source; numbers,
and only numbers, crossing into the product through a checked file; seeds fixed by formula before anything is seen;
tuning as a pre-registered search with a held-back test set and a declared shift; the owner's sittings on one sealed,
resumable page; and the historical reconciliation as a separate layer that never overwrites the base. Every change
request it makes to the base quotes the base correctly (checked by script: 13 headings and 22 quotations, each found
exactly once).

Three things stop it from being built as written.

1. **Its main test for "a leaning, not a guarantee" passes pure chance.** The owner's siting rules are proven by
   comparing each town with the same town shuffled at random. The volume's pass rule works on smooth numbers, but most of
   these bars count things ("how many tanneries are downstream"). With few members and a skewed share, the rule passes a
   town with no pull at all 58 % to 98 % of the time (executed, V12C-01). The base's own campaign for "New random" already
   solved this with a tie-breaking rank; the volume must use that, plus an exact pooled count test for shares.
2. **The owner's ~20:05 ruling on crypts and catacombs is only one-fifth here.** The volume measures that a catacomb never
   shrinks, but only across epochs. The owner's words and item 109 (k) (4) say "every derivation", so regeneration,
   re-derivation, New random and upgrade replay must be covered too. It has no crypt at all: the cathedral's crypt and the
   crypt rows have no calibration entries. There is no measurement of stagnation (bones reused, ossuaries filling), no
   check of growth against its cap, and no place for the sibling critiques' bars that already point at this volume
   (V12C-02).
3. **The historical reconciliation could quietly undo an owner rule.** A single historical example "proves possibility",
   and the volume lets such an example lift a zero chance to 2 % in the historical layer. That layer may become the
   default. Historical catacombs under a cathedral exist, so the process would either add a church catacomb or file the
   owner's rule as a defect of the generator. Owner rules must be exempt and go back to the owner as a question
   (V12C-03).

Beyond these, seventeen major findings make parts of it guesswork or unwired. The historical layer's corpus rows gate the
default town that the layer is forbidden to change. The bar map misreads one g item and omits two. The volume's pass rules
contradict the ones V11 and V04 already state for the same bars. Nothing stops dozens of nightly bars from triggering
fallbacks on bad luck. The metric catalogue gives no band floors or tolerances, and there is no mapping from our street
classes to the measured ones. The sitting records and milestone records cannot hold what the new panels produce, and the
panel's answer files would be read from outside the product folder. Several M-1 measurements have no metric. The "no
number dangles" check misses most calibrate-later constants. A tuning run can break a SettlementForge fixture without
noticing. The historical layer has no slot in the parameter stack. The corpus process leaves its reachability search
and frame registration undefined. The dossier fixtures the bars use are not traced, the metropolis budgets are missing,
and the app volumes that read the calibration evidence were never told. Thirteen minor findings follow. Every finding
below states the change that fixes it.

## 2 Blockers

### V12C-01 — blocker — buildability / owner fidelity (item 103 (e) "provable as correlations"; J-V12.12), coherence with §13.2's faithfulness campaign — the correlation rule passes chance on discrete statistics

**Defect.** V12 §3.6 step 5 scores each town by z = s × (S_obs − mean S_k) ÷ sd S_k over K = 20 permutations, with
"sd = 0 gives z = 0 when S_obs equals the mean, else ± 10". The bar passes when z > 0 in ≥ 70 % of 50 towns and the
median z ≥ 0.2 (l. 769–777). The calibration (l. 774–777, `v12_nullrule.py`) plants **normal** statistics only. Most
statistics the rule serves are **shares of few members**: `shareWithin`, `downstreamShare`, `downwindShare`,
`prestigeShare`, `servedShare`, `flowOrder` (l. 368–377). A town's S_obs then takes a handful of values, and many S_k tie
with it. When most eligible plots already satisfy the indicator, S_obs sits at the top of its range and is above the
permutation mean in most towns, with no pull at all. Executed
(`docs/validation/bench/critique_v12_nullrule_discrete.py` → `.out`, 400 bars of 50 towns, members placed uniformly among
200 eligible plots):

| base share q, members m | V12 rule passes under the null | randomised-tie rank, sign clause only (u > 0.5 in ≥ 70 % of towns), passes under the null |
|---|---|---|
| 0.7, 1 | 0.580 | 0.000 |
| 0.85, 1 | 0.980 | 0.013 |
| 0.85, 2 | 0.618 | 0.000 |
| 0.9, 3 | 0.718 | 0.005 |
| 0.5, 10 | 0.000 | 0.003 |

The converse also holds: at q = 0.3 and m = 1, a real pull (a member that would miss the indicator lands on it with
probability 0.3) passes only 0.3 % of the time (`critique_v12_nullrule_rank_power.out`). So the rule both "proves" pulls that do not exist and misses
ones that do, depending on an accident of the indicator's base share. The base met exactly this problem in the O9 (c)
faithfulness campaign (§13.2): it ranks with "a uniform draw from 0 … t over the t references equal to it … so r is
exactly uniform … however discrete the statistic", and records that "without the randomised tie-break a perfect
regenerator fails every campaign" on discrete statistics. V12 cites that campaign as its model (§1's hook table, l. 95)
and then drops its tie-break. `minMembers` also has no default (l. 386).

**Required change.**
1. Replace V12 §3.6 step 5 and the "Across towns" rule. Per town, compute the randomised-tie rank
   u = (#{S_k < S_obs} + U × (#{S_k = S_obs} + 1)) ÷ (K + 1). U is drawn from `TestStream('null/<spec>/<rung>/<water>/<i>/tie')`,
   and S is negated for `expect: 'lower'`. Across towns, the **rank rule** passes when the share of counted towns with
   u > 0.5 is ≥ 0.70 and the median u is ≥ 0.60. Executed (`critique_v12_nullrule_rank_power.out`, 400 bars): with no
   effect it passes 0.000–0.005 on the normal and all six discrete fixtures. On normal statistics it passes 0.412 / 0.927
   / 0.993 at planted shifts of 0.5 / 0.8 / 1.0 sd, where V12's rule passes 0.470 / 0.932 / 0.988. Delete the "± 10" clause
   and `medianZ`, and report median u.
2. **Share statistics that count members on indicator plots** (`shareWithin`, `downstreamShare`, `downwindShare`,
   `prestigeShare`) use a pooled exact test instead. Per town, the null count is hypergeometric: m members drawn from the
   E eligible plots of the scope unit, G of which satisfy the indicator. The pooled null distribution is the convolution
   of the towns' distributions, and the bar passes when P(pooled count ≥ observed) ≤ 0.01. Executed
   (`critique_v12_pooled_exact.py` → `.out`, 1 000 bars): size 0.002–0.015, where the simulation draws observed members
   with replacement, which is slightly wider than the null. Power at a 0.3 pull is 0.705 (q 0.3, m 1), 0.384 (0.5, 1),
   0.905 (0.5, 3) and 1.000 (0.3, 10), against the rank rule's 0.302, 0.170, 0.535 and 1.000. The convolution runs in
   integer-indexed doubles in town order (deterministic, V12 §3.13). `servedShare` and `flowOrder` are not
   member-on-plot counts, so they stay on the rank rule.
3. **Headroom lint at registration.** Refuse a share spec whose pooled null share over its first 50 towns exceeds 0.85
   (below 0.02 for `lower`). Executed: at q 0.85 no rule shows a pull of 0.3 (power ≤ 0.09). The spec's owner restates
   the indicator, for example with a tighter radius, or states the complement.
4. Set `minMembers` defaults: 1 for the pooled route, 3 for the rank route.
5. Carry these into the neighbouring text. `NullModelSpec.pass` becomes `{ kind: 'rank'; townShare: 0.70; medianU: 0.60 } |
   { kind: 'pooledExact'; alpha: 0.01; minShift?: number } | { kind: 'margin'; value }`. J-V12.12 is rewritten. BCR-V12-12's
   correlation row states the rank and pooled rules. T-V12-14 gains the discrete fixtures (m ∈ {1, 2, 3, 10},
   q ∈ {0.3, 0.5, 0.85}; ≤ 1 % of 200 null bars pass for each) and the headroom refusal. The receipts above join V12 §12's
   CONFIRMED list, and `v12_nullrule.py` is kept as history. T71-3 keeps its `margin` rule (its margin is §13.7's).

### V12C-04 — major — coherence (V12 §3.10.3 R1 against R2) — corpus target rows gate a default the corpus may not move

**Defect.** R1: "A corpus value lands in the reconciled preset `historical:<frame>` … and never in a table's shipped
default … the default town changes only if the owner adopts the layer". R2: "Corpus target rows join §13.5 and gate the

by a corpus run, and corpus runs may only write the layer. From M-CAL on, every milestone's parity exit (§13.3,

other kind of run can reach them either: a `reference` run's targets are its own reference's baselines, and a `fallback`
run moves only the register entry it names (V12 §2.5's `source`).

### V12C-21 — minor — coherence (§17.1 (8)) — the statistical methods V12 adds are not on the published-algorithms list
