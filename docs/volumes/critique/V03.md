## 0 Verdict in plain words

Volume 3 is a strong, well-grounded design: programme first, the stair and chimneys placed before any wall, one cut
primitive, restrictive re-rolls, every edit undoable, and exports to tabletop tools. It is not yet buildable without
guessing, and in two common cases its default mechanism breaks an owner rule. (1) When two households share a house
split by storey, the upper family can only reach its own door by walking through the lower family's hall. (2) When a
secret way down to the sewer opens in a cellar, the whole cellar is made secret, so it is either reachable from the
public stair (the secret leaks) or unreachable (the plan fails). Beyond those, the volume was written before file g and
before volume 2's composer and volume 11's registry settled, so it does not read their outputs: volume 2's façade bays and
dormer rows, its composer room roles `gatehall` and `towerRoom`, its wing roles; volume 11's 179 programme keys, its
specialisations by band and its workplace `staff` column; item 105's split works-and-shop buildings; item 108's ladder
buildings; custom institutions. Several steps also cannot be implemented as written (a shared chimney placed on a wall
that the cut has not made yet; a straight cut across a bent piece; a hall-and-parlour pattern in a wing that holds no
hall; a programme smaller than its shell; storeys at different floor heights). The fixes are all local to the volume
plus a handful of base change requests; none needs an owner decision beyond the one the volume already asks.

File g's item 109 (the owner's caverns, ruins and catacombs, ~19:15–19:55, with the refinements he approved at ~19:55)
reaches this volume where a way down, a crypt or a dwelling meets a building, and brings a third and a fourth blocker.
(3) Any way down that is not item 64's cellar lottery is never drawn in the floor plan (V3C-59). This covers the user's
own "add an underground access" edit, a neighbourhood's cellar stair, the catacomb's stair in a cemetery's chapel, the
door from a church's crypt into a catacomb that grew beneath it, and the owner's new rule that a house built over a cave
or a ruin may have dug its way in. A hovel over a cave has no cellar to hold its shaft, and a deep stair does not fit
(V3C-60). (4) The owner's ruling of ~20:05, "cathedral also gets crypts but cemetaries get catacombs", is not met
(V3C-61). The cathedral may end up with no crypt, and nothing makes its crypt the largest. The revision in progress forces
a crypt on every church, where the owner says the other churches "may" have one. And the crypt exists only inside a floor
plan that nobody may have opened, so the catacomb's join and the size rule cannot read it. The new dwellings in caves and ruins, like
the sewer dwellings before them, have no floor-plan rules (V3C-62); a cellar edit can seal a void unannounced (V3C-63);
and nothing explains a way down (V3C-64).

Counts (as amended for J111, section 10): 4 blockers, 39 majors, 21 minors (64 findings, V3C-01 … V3C-64). Every finding carries an exact required change.

## 2 Blockers

### V3C-01 — blocker — owner fidelity, buildability — a household reaches its door only through another household's room

**Claim.** Item 28 (f) (owner: "a corridor or stair hall inserted to give each [family] its own door") and T28-13 ("houses
every household … with its own door off a corridor or stair hall") require every unit to be reachable from the street
through circulation. V03 guarantees only the door itself: O5 checks that a unit's entry opens onto circulation, and O2
forbids edges between two units' rooms, but nothing makes the main entrance's room circulation or joins the entrance to
the stair core through circulation. With the shipped rows this fails in the commonest multi-household case: the
`comfortable` row (`dwelling`, [1, 2] households, "divide the shell by storey … each with its own door off the stair
hall") with the `house` class's ground patterns `hall_and_parlour 2, cross_passage 1`; A5 (b) puts unit 1 on the ground
storey, E1 makes the main entrance's room the hub `hall` — unit 1's own room — and the core's bay opens (O2: "onto its
adjacent circulation room, else its largest neighbour") into that hall. Unit 2's door is on the upper landing, so O5 is
satisfied, yet the upper family walks through the lower family's hall. The same holds for a live-in household above a
shop or forge and for `lodgings` units whose lobby does not reach the core.

**Required change.** (1) Add §3.2 A6 *Shared access*: when a building houses ≥ 2 units, or any unit lies wholly on a
storey other than the main entrance's, the programme gains one circulation `ProgramRoom` of role `stair hall` anchored
to the main entrance (`anchor.entrance` = the main ordinal) on its storey. (2) In §3.5, before the storey's pattern runs,
cut that room as a `strip` of `corridorCm` from the main entrance's inward point to the core's bay (along the wing side
the entrance lies on, then along the bay's side; a `splitFace` of 2–3 vertices), or — where the core's bay touches the
entrance room's piece — a stair-hall piece of the role's min area around both; the pattern then cuts the rest. (3) S3:
for such buildings every placement rule is scored by the sum of its own score and the bay's distance from the main
entrance's inward point, so the core lands near the entrance. (4) Add bar T-V3-16: every unit's entry door is reached
from the main entrance, or from a secondary entrance of the same unit, through circulation rooms and stair cores only —
never through a room of another unit or a service room of the use (checked on the door graph, B-PLAN and the ladder
fixtures of T28-13). (5) BCR (new BCR-V3-11) to §5.3's interior-ladder row: after "never opens through another unit's
room" add "and is reached from the main entrance through circulation rooms and stair cores only (V03 §3.2 A6)".

### V3C-02 — blocker — owner fidelity, coherence — a secret basement access makes the whole cellar concealed

**Claim.** §3.7: an item-64 access's "room the `cellar` holding them, flagged `concealed` when the access's `knownTo` is
`secret`". A basement usually has one cellar (with nothing else allocated it "is one `cellar` (nave)"), and the basement
is joined by the core or a trapdoor from the public ground storey. The cellar therefore becomes a concealed room joined
to public circulation — which O2 forbids ("between a concealed room and any public room"), so the cellar is left
unreached (tier 1, T28-2 fails) — or, if joined, it violates item 63 (owner: concealed rooms "reached through a service
or yard door or a concealed door") and §5.3's row ("reachable only from its concealed entrance or its front's service
side, never through the public rooms"). Item 64's multiplier (6 × for a concealed use) makes this the default for every
clandestine building with a cellar. The whole public cellar also vanishes from public views.

**Required change.** Rewrite §3.7's access paragraph: each access of source `basement` whose `knownTo` is `secret`
reserves, before the basement's cut, its own concealed room — a fixed ring of role `back room` (area min of the role
× the ladder's `sizeFactor`, holding the access's bay or hatch plus `landingCm` in front of it), against the basement wall
nearest the element; that room is `concealed`; the public cellar keeps scope `service` and no concealed flag; the room is
joined by one opening to the cellar (or to the concealed suite where the building has one on the basement storey), and
an opening is *concealed* exactly when a room on either side is concealed (a derived rule, no new field: drawn, exported
and answered only for an admitting key). A `local` or `public` access stays in the cellar as written. Define every
basement room's scope (cellar, store, undercroft `service`; crypt `service`; strongroom of a concealed use `service` +
`concealed`). Extend T-V3-10: on the item-64 fixtures with a secret access, the public cellar is reachable from the core
or trapdoor, the access room only through its concealed opening, and T63-3 holds.
