## 0 Verdict in plain words

Volume 7 is a careful and mostly buildable design of the village product, and its main choices should stay: the
village is its own registered product on the town's engine; it copies the Village Generator's order (the "crowding" map
first, then the fence, then the roads grown like walkers); it keeps the exact population a dossier gives by racing the
houses block by block; it puts the fields, meadow, commons and woods around the village sized to the people who work
them; its palisade uses rivers, cliffs and marsh as the owner asked; and a village that grows past 1 500 people hands
over to the town generator with its old core kept.

Three things stop it from being built as written, and a fourth breaks an owner rule more quietly.

1. **The owner's newest rule is missing.** On 28 September the owner said caverns and ruins can lie under any
   settlement, "a village or a Hamlet could be sitting on top of a ruins or caverns", reachable only once someone digs
   down or finds an opening. The volume still says a village has no underground at all, so nothing in it lays a cavern
   or a ruin, draws the chance of an access, or keeps dwellings out until an access exists (V7C-01).
2. **The village skips a town stage without taking over all of its jobs.** Besides placing the market and the first
   wall, the skipped stage draws the map's border, builds the grid the road router uses, lists the places roads must
   reach and digs the ponds. Nothing in the village does these, so the road stage waits for an input no stage makes
   (V7C-02).
3. **Some village steps add roads, piers or water after the houses are laid, which the engine forbids.** The
   piers, the extra roads for a village that ran out of house plots, and the pond in the green must move earlier
   (V7C-03).
4. **Several things jump at the size boundaries instead of growing smoothly with the head count**, which the
   owner's definition requires: a 399-person hamlet and a 400-person village differ in whether they must have a church,
   a smithy and a green (V7C-04, a major).

The rest are gaps a builder would otherwise have to guess: where the outlying farmers are counted, how the smallest
thorps meet their housing range, which dossier records become which village buildings (a graveyard would become a
second church), how the village looks at build depth next to the Village Generator's drawings, what happens to a
town's sewers and catacombs when it shrinks into a village, whether a village church has a crypt and which village
ground a future town's catacomb may lie under now that the owner has put catacombs under cemeteries only and crypts under
churches (V7C-12), and a dozen smaller items. None needs the owner, except
that the owner may want to see V7C-01's reading of "a hamlet sitting on ruins" before it is built.

### V7C-05 — major — buildability, dossier fidelity (DR7's bar) — the outlying farmsteads' households are counted twice or nowhere

**Defect.** §3.2 (3) computes the dwellings `D` from all households `H` and houses "`farm.outlyingShare[form]` of [the
farm households] at outlying farmsteads"; §3.8 allocates the same `D` to the district's blocks and races them; §3.9 (5)
counts the outlying farmsteads again as "`farm.outlyingShare[form] ×` the farm households ÷ `householdsPerFarmstead`".
Nothing subtracts the outlying households from the village's `D`, so they are housed twice (a `dispersed` form, share
1.0, would build every farm household in the village *and* on its farm). The farm-household count itself is not stated:
the base's farm table gives `farmsteads = round(arableHaPerCapita · P ÷ fieldHaPerFarmstead)` (§4.9), which the volume
never ties to `H`. And DR7's bar ("the residential building count inside the housing record's range") does not say
whether outlying farmsteads count — at a `dispersed` thorp every dwelling is outlying. `householdsPerFarmstead` is cited
as "§2.2" (l. 1028) but is the base's `FarmTable` field.

**Required change.** In §3.2 (3) state: farm households `H_f = min(H, round(arableHaPerCapita · P ÷ fieldHaPerFarmstead)
× householdsPerFarmstead)` (the base's farm table, village rows); outlying households `H_o = round(outlyingShare[form] ·
H_f)`; outlying farmsteads `D_o = ⌈H_o ÷ householdsPerFarmstead⌉`; village dwellings `D_v = D − D_o` with `D` as now.
§3.8's quotas and race use `D_v`; §3.9 (5) places `D_o`. DR7's residential count is `D_v + D_o` (both are dwellings of
the settlement), and DR7's re-solve (next finding) runs on that total. Correct the `householdsPerFarmstead` pointer to
§4.9's `FarmTable`. Add T-V7-2's clause: `D_v + D_o = D` and every household is housed once.

### V7C-06 — major — buildability, dossier fidelity (DR7) — the smallest thorps cannot meet their housing range

**Defect.** DR7 (l. 1132–1135): `D = clamp(Σ round(H_b ÷ (1 + share2_b)), range.min, range.max)`, "the doubled share
re-solved per band to meet it", and "when `P < range.min · 2` the household size falls instead (`H = max(H,
range.min)`)". For SettlementForge's smallest thorps (the soak's minimum is 10 people, `dossier-outputs.md` §3.4) with
the thorp record `Dwellings (4-16)`: `H = round(10 ÷ 4.5) = 2`, `P = 10 ≥ 8`, so the household-size rule does not fire,
the clamp raises `D` to 4, and doubling can only *lower* `D`. Two households own four dwellings — two empty houses — or
the implementer guesses. The trigger should be the household count, not `2 · range.min`.

**Required change.** Replace the rule by: `H₀ = round(P ÷ meanSize)`; if `H₀ < range.min`, `H = range.min` (the mean
household size falls to `P ÷ range.min`, never below 1.5 persons — below that, the surplus dwellings are built vacant
and reported, a village in decline being the historical reading); else `H = H₀` and doubling is re-solved band by band
from the poorest up, at most two households a dwelling, only to bring `D` down to `range.max`. State the members' sum
to `P` exactly after the size change (largest remainder over households). Add the 10-person thorp to T-V7-2's fixtures.

### 12.2 Majors

**V7C-37 — major — buildability and realism (item 110 (a): "the generator raises one where a block, a plot or a ward
is levelled on ground steeper than `terrace.slopeMin` … and along a terraced district's edge when the relief row asks

At a village rung, each of item 110 (a)'s three units is the wrong size:
* A **plot** is one toft-and-croft plot (l. 1277–1279). Its toft is 8–80 m deep and its croft up to 300 m (§2.2,
  l. 433–436).
* A **block** is a whole network face of the district (VS6: "no block is cut. Every face of the district is a block",
  l. 1257).
* The **ward** is the one `village` district (l. 1243).

So an implementer who applies (a) as written has three bad outcomes:
* a prosperous yeoman's croft is levelled as a 200 m terrace;
* the face between two lanes is flattened;
* with the district-edge source, a hill village's perimeter becomes a continuous wall line. The item says that line is
  not an `Enceinte`, but it reads as one.

The 23:11 note's "a toft run on a slope taking the wall along its downslope edge" puts the wall on a frontage run, which
is not a unit the base levels, and its step and datum are undefined. The base already carries the fall under a building:
a footprint's skirt up to `skirtMaxCm` (300 cm), else stepped wings (l. 1392–1395).

The longhouse's hard rule (l. 587), "byre end on the downhill side (hard: the building's axis follows the fall line when
slope > 3 %)", reads the very slope that a levelled platform removes. On a platform the rule never fires, and the
longhouse loses the drainage it was built down the slope for.
