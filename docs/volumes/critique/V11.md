## 0 Verdict in plain words

Volume 11 is the rule book of the whole generator: every table, what each column means, and the actual rows — the
ladder of building kinds from hovel to cathedral, what each kind of building gives off and cannot bear, which trades
pull together and which push apart, the goods that move between workshops, the translation of all 280 of
SettlementForge's institution kinds onto our buildings, the district kinds, the quarter names, the walls on cliffs and
shores, the eras and materials, and the naming grammars. Its big choices are right and should stay: one typed register
of every table; rows written in a human-friendly source form and compiled to the base's cell format; a rulebook built
from short per-building profiles and a dozen general rules rather than a 280 × 280 grid of pairs; every new tendency
marked as our extension so the "play it like MFCG / FTG" presets still behave like the originals; and a full,
script-checked mapping of every SettlementForge kind.

Three things stop it from being built as written.

1. **The owner's newest underground rules are missing.** On the evening of 28 September the owner asked for caverns that
   follow mountains and mines, ruins of an older civilisation under any settlement, and access that appears as the town
   grows. At about 20:05 he settled the burial rule: churches get crypts, the cathedral always and with the largest one,
   and catacombs lie only under cemeteries (and, in a city, under a worked-out quarry), growing but never shrinking. The
   volume was finished before those rules arrived, so none of the numbers they need — how likely a ruin is, what an
   ancient street plan looks like, what survives longest, how likely a cellar breaks into a cave, how many catacombs a
   city may have and how fast they grow, how large a crypt is — are written anywhere (V11C-01, V11C-02). Worse, no
   building in the volume has a crypt at all, so the cathedral cannot have the one the owner says it always has
   (V11C-02).
2. **The rows fail the volume's own checks.** The compiler is told to stop the build when, for example, a building both
   gives off a smell and cannot bear one, or when a one-off landmark is told to cluster. Four food trades and ten landmark
   classes break those checks as written, most rows do not say which profile family they belong to, and one district kind
   name is used twice. The build would stop on the first run (V11C-03).
3. **Many rules are prose, not data.** A large share of the per-building rules have no weight, several point at things
   the solver cannot measure ("the lee of the walls", "its trade's members", "the river's fall"), and the goods web has
   workshops waiting for goods nobody makes (a weaver's yarn, a tanner's bark, a thatcher's reed) and shops making goods
   nobody is told to buy — so the owner's "supply chains want to be closer" cannot work for textiles, rope or thatch, and
   bakers and butchers have nothing to lean toward (V11C-04 – V11C-06).

The rest are fixable gaps: catchment units for "one per district / one per walking radius" that are never defined, a
district-quarter chain whose "defining" records name no class, SettlementForge words the tables do not know
(`operational`, `running`, `backbone`), a raster cost ten times the base's budget, and a handful of base rules the volume
contradicts without asking. None of them changes the architecture; every one has an exact change below.

#### V11C-37 — major — buildability (item 110 (b): "where the city's water need exceeds what its wells, river and cisterns supply inside the walls (the base's cistern rule reads the same distances)") — the need ratio has no units, no demand and no supply

**Defect.** Every sibling names the test and none gives numbers:

  frontage and cisterns by the base's distances)";

V11's `ClimateRow` (l. 414–417) has no rainfall. No row gives water per head, well yield or a well density. The test also
runs at the end of S6, before any well pin, cistern or household is placed (C4-110-6 and I110R-2: "reads only eager

reason cannot be computed, and whether a city gets one would be an implementer's guess.

**Required change.** V11 §3.24.6 `water` (`WaterNeedRow`, one row; registry row 85). Its readers are the aqueduct reason
(the base's S6 test, V06's pass) and V09's aqueduct refusal text.

* **Demand.** `D = pop_in × farShare × perHeadL ÷ 1000` (m³ a day). `pop_in` is S2's population inside the enceinte.
  `farShare` is the S6 density-weighted share of the intramural area lying beyond the base's `cisternDistM` (read from
  II.7.6's row, never restated) from a river or lake face, since frontage serves the rest. `perHeadL` is 20 litres a
  day, for drinking, cooking and washing.
* **Supply.** `S = walledHa × wellsPerHa(geology) × wellYieldM3 + walledHa × 10 000 × roofShare × captureShare(biome) ×
  rainMm ÷ 1000 ÷ 365`.
  * `wellsPerHa` is keyed by S1's geology row: alluvial 1.0, default 0.6, soluble 0.3, volcanic 0.3.
  * `wellYieldM3` is 4 m³ a day.
  * `roofShare` is 0.4.
  * `captureShare` is keyed by biome: arid 0.3, maritime 0.1, every other biome 0.05, following the practice of cistern
    towns in dry climates.
  * `rainMm` is `ClimateRow.rainMmPerYear`, a new V11 §2.8 column. Its values are temperate-deciduous 750,
    temperate-coniferous 700, mixed 700, maritime 1 100, arid 200 and cold 500.
* **The ratio.** `needRatio = D ÷ S`; `S = 0` counts as met, and the candidate lives. The aqueduct is a candidate when
  `needRatio > aqueduct.needRatioMin` (1.0) and a higher source lies within `sourceReachKm`.
* **Worked check (PLAUSIBLE, to be measured).** A temperate alluvial CITY of 15 000 in 100 ha with a far share of 0.6
  gives D 180 and S 240 + 41, a ratio of 0.64, so no aqueduct. An arid METROPOLIS of 60 000 in 300 ha with a far share of
  0.7 gives D 840 and S 360 + 197, a ratio of 1.5, so a candidate. The rule thus favours dry, karst and large cities, as
  the historical pattern does.

  is below 0.5, or the share of temperate alluvial CITY seeds with one is above 0.2, `perHeadL` moves by 5 litres once

### 11.6 Wiring, lifecycle and determinism rows

* **Wiring.** V11C-44 (2)'s W49–W57 give each new piece its producer, consumers, base hook and dossier row. No new
  piece is an orphan:
  * every table has a reader stage and a `says` sentence;
  * every column has a compile check (T-V11-32);
  * every edit has its `editTable` row (V11C-42);
  * every entity it feeds is drawn, labelled and exported by its owner (the base, V02, V09) and named by V11C-46.
* **Lifecycle.** V11C-42's table covers create, read, persist, regenerate, undo and migrate for each row set. The one
  interaction the owner named is that a removed raised way stays removed. It is the base's held delete, and no V11 row
  can undo it.
* **Determinism.** V11 holds no stream (V11 §3.2.4). Each draw its rows feed belongs to the reading stage under the key
  V11C-42 lists. The caps are counted in fixed orders: V06 C6-37 (5)'s kind order and strength for raised ways, and
  roster sequence for specialisations. So a regeneration with the same tables is byte-identical (T-V11-10 extends to
  the new tables).
