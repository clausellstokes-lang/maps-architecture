## 0 Verdict in plain words

Volume 9 is a careful design of the editor's screens: the ghost-before-commit pipeline, the tool rail, the plain-words
message catalogue, autosave with crash recovery, the history panel and the first-five-minutes test are all worked out to
a level an implementer could build. It fails the bar in four places. First, it was written in parallel with volumes 10
(the assistant) and 11 (the content rows), and the three now disagree about shared pieces: two different shapes for the
"why is this here" answer, two different contracts for the assistant's drawer, and two different places to store the
owner's siting, supply-link and works-and-shop declarations. An implementer holding all three would have to guess.
Second, the owner's newest underground rules (item 109: ruins, hidden caves the game master can see but the townsfolk
cannot, how a way down came about, no dwellings without a way in, catacombs that lie only under cemeteries and never shrink, while churches keep crypts and the cathedral always has the
largest) have no screens at all.
Third, several things the owner's rules and the base promise have no button: placing the castle, painting farmland,
a tree brush, lifting a "not here" rejection, rotating the map, the ruler, notes and pins, the people search and family
tree. Fourth, a few mechanisms would misbehave as written: dragging one corner of a quarter would redraw its whole edge,
a confirmed "rebuild" is missing from the crash journal, "view as a player" would stop autosave, and the known-city test
asks the assistant's version to be byte-identical to a hand-drawn one, which cannot happen. Each finding below says
exactly what to change.

**V9C-08 — major — owner fidelity (item 109 (k) (1)–(6) as revised ~20:05, J111). Catacombs, crypts, the count,
growth and stagnation have no screen, and nothing bends a catacomb drawn in the wrong place.**

> Revised 2026-09-28 ~21:15 for the owner's ~20:05 switch (J111): "switch it then, cathedral also gets crypts but
> cemetaries get catacombs". The 20:25 text followed the ~19:40 wording. It stopped a catacomb when "its church is ruined"
> and bent a second TOWN catacomb into "a crypt under the chosen church". Both are superseded by the text below.

**The rule.** Item 109 (k) as revised says four things.
* Where: catacombs lie "ONLY under CEMETERIES — a churchyard, a separate burial ground, a monastery's burial ground, or a
  cemetery outside the walls". In a CITY or METROPOLIS, "a worked-out quarry … turned into an ossuary" also qualifies.
* Crypts: "CHURCHES GET CRYPTS: the cathedral always has a crypt, the largest crypt in the settlement, and every other
  church may have one by the base's crypted-class rows — a burial vault in the church's own basement, never a catacomb
  system". Where a catacomb grows beneath a church, "its crypt may join the catacomb by a link".
* How many: at most ONE at TOWN, and more at CITY and METROPOLIS by the number of churches (`catacomb.churchesPer`,
  capped). "The LARGEST catacomb is the largest cemetery's".
* Growth: the extent is a ratchet that "may only add", with growth per epoch "capped at `catacomb.growthCapShare`".
  Blocked growth is `stagnant`, "recorded as the ossuaries' fill rising and drawn as denser ossuaries, never as loss".
  "A catacomb whose cemetery is closed stops growing and stays".

Critique-V4's J111 fold gives these their records:
* burial faces ranked by the burials they serve, the count, the 0.9 rank cap and the crypt join (C4-49);
* `CatacombComplex { extentFloorM2, fill, blockedBy, lastGrowth, source, rank }` and the author's delete (C4-50, J-V4.56);
* `overCount` on a complex kept past the count (C4-59 (1));
* the access cause `crypt` (C4-48 (4)).

**The defect.** V09 has no screen for any of it. There is no catacomb panel and no crypt on a church's panel, and a user
cannot see that a catacomb has stagnated or why. Nothing maps a user who starts a catacomb under a church, a second one at
TOWN, a quarry ossuary below CITY, or any catacomb below TOWN. Under R2 each of these is a declaration to bend to the
nearest lawful one, not to refuse: none is a dossier fact or a lock. The V9 lane's 20:55 summary spoke of "refusals" for
a catacomb not under a cemetery and for more than one at TOWN; this finding supersedes that word.

*Required change:*
(1) **The catacomb's panel.** It shows the `UndergroundSystem` of kind `catacomb` and reads its `CatacombComplex`.
* Its name is V04's label (C4-49 (8)): "the Catacombs of {cemetery}", a churchyard's by its church, or "the Ossuary of
  {quarter}".
* "Under {burial face}", with **Show** selecting that face. The source kind is given in words: churchyard, burial ground,
  "the cemetery outside {gate}", "the monastery's burial ground", "an old quarry turned ossuary", or "from an older version
  of the town" for `legacy`.
* The rank: "The largest catacomb in {town}" for rank 1, else "{n} of {count}". The count rule is given in words: "{town}
  is a city with {churches} churches, so it may have {count} catacombs", or "A town has one catacomb".
* When `overCount` is set: "Kept from when the town was larger; no new catacomb starts until the count allows"
  (`info.catacomb.overCount`).
(2) **The "Over time" tab.**
* The extent (`extentFloorM2`, in m²), with a line chart of it per epoch that never falls.
* This epoch's growth (`lastGrowth.addedM2`) against its cap.
* The state, read from `blockedBy`: **growing** when it is empty; **stopped** when it holds `sourceClosed`; **stagnant**
  otherwise. A stopped catacomb reads "{cemetery} is closed; its catacomb stays and no longer grows"
  (`info.catacomb.stopped`). A stagnant one lists each `blockedBy` entry in words:
  * `reach`: "It has reached as far as its diggers go";
  * `wet`: "The ground beyond is too wet";
  * `system`: "Another tunnel is in the way";
  * `foundation`: "Deep foundations are in the way";
  * `wallStrip`: "The town wall's footing is in the way";
  * `depth`: "The rock lies too deep";
