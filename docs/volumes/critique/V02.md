## 0 Verdict in plain words

Volume 2 is a strong design for the buildings of the map. It turns Fantasy Manor's idea (a building is a path of roofed
ranges with blocks, towers, dormers and a porch) into a generator that fits each plot. It faces the street, stays in
metres, keeps what fits when a house becomes an inn, and gives every building one exact roof plan that the drawing, the
light and the 3-D exports all share. The mechanisms are mostly written as real steps with constants and tests.

It cannot yet be built without guessing, for four reasons.

1. **It speaks a different class language from the content volume.** V02's tables have rows for 18 names. The content
   volume (V11) defines 179 town and city classes, 44 generic ones and the village classes, built from 34 "template
   families". Only 15 class names match. Everything else falls to a `default` row that is never written.
2. **Two volumes define the same heights table in two incompatible ways.** They differ in keys, fields, values and in
   where the roof pitch comes from.
3. **V02's building sizes do not fit V11's size budgets.** For example, a rich manor's smallest courtyard plan is about
   495 m², but V11 budgets 260 m² for the whole compound. V02's own size step cannot shrink a courtyard plan, so two of its
   own acceptance tests cannot both pass.
4. **Rooms are fixed at the wrong wealth level.** The hall, the chapel and the other rooms the composer makes follow the
   building's built wealth, not the wealth of the people living there. They are then held fixed through every
   re-division. So a manor that falls on hard times keeps a 60–190 m² hall that can never be split for the families who
   move in, which breaks the owner's rule that "the interior has no floor" (item 28 (f)).

The volume also landed before the owner's rules of 2026-09-28 (file g, items 102–109). It has nothing yet on:

- the building side of sunlight: which parts cast shade over the years, and whether rich houses turn toward the sun;
- custom institutions;
- businesses split into a works and a shop;
- the institution ladders;
- digging down into caverns and ruins;
- crypts and catacombs.

Several smaller mechanisms also need an implementer to guess:

- **Whether a block may have a pyramid roof.** The volume gives blocks pyramid roofs, which the adopted item 59 ruled out.
- **Where the hall goes in a courtyard house.** The courtyard plans have no "main" range to hold it.
- **Which wing a door or a room belongs to.** Stored doors, rooms and stairs name wings by list position, and the list
  changes with every edit.
- **Where a back range goes on a gable-fronted row house.** The rule reads the ridge before the ridge is decided.
- **Dome and "plain" roofs.** The single roof plan has no rule for them.

All of these fixes stay within V02, plus a few base change requests and cross-volume notes. None needs a new owner
decision. The one owner question already in the volume (should SettlementForge be able to dictate a building's shape?)
stands, with its recommendation "not now".

Counts: **4 blockers, 20 majors, 22 minors** (46 findings, V2C-01 … V2C-46). Every finding carries an exact required change.

## 2 Blockers

### V2C-01 — blocker — buildability, coherence, owner fidelity (item 19, "all the buildings across prosperity bands") — the composition key does not resolve for the content volume's classes

**Claim.**

*Which row a building reads.* V2 §3.2 step 2 resolves a building's composition row in this order:
- the template's `composition` column (BCR-V2-09);
- then `<class>/<band>`;
- then `default/<band>`.

*What exists.*
- **V2 §3.10 writes rows for 18 prefixes.** Of these, 15 are V11 class ids (see §1). `hall`, `temple`, `keep`, `mill` and
  `tower` are not V11 classes: V11 has `guildhall`, `town_hall`, `watermill`, `windmill`, `tower_house`, `watchtower`,
  `mage_tower`, and so on.
- **The `default/<band>` row has no content anywhere** ("A cell the table below does not name reads the `default/<band>`
  row's", V2 §3.10). So for about 164 of V11's 179 authored town and city classes, for all 44 `generic-<family>` classes
  and for every village class (V07 §2.4), every cell of the row is undefined. Examples: bakery, smithy, tannery,
  guildhall, barracks, cathedral, market hall, bath-house, and the custom archetypes' `mage_tower`, `archive` and
  `town_hall`.
- **V11's `TemplateFamilyRow` carries no `composition` field.** The compiled templates therefore never name a row.

*The rows ignore the arrangement.*
- **`town_house`.** V02's `town_house` row is mostly `row` (".8 / .6 / .4"). V11 compiles `town_house` as
  `dwelling-compound`, arrangement `compound` (V11 §3.6.1).
- **`house`.** V02's `house` row draws `L`, `T`, `H` and `U`. V11 compiles `house` as `dwelling-row`, arrangement `row`,
  so M0 (a) passes its quad through and the row's families are never read.

*The slot roles do not line up.*
- **V02 has outbuilding rows for six roles only:** `stables`, `barn`, `store`, `brewhouse`, `kitchen` and `workshop`.
- **V11's structure lists need about thirty building roles.** Among them: `shop-house`, `forge`, `journeymen`, `works`,
  `shed`, `hut`, `office`, `kiln`, `bath hall`, `furnace`, `nave`, `tower`, `porch`, `nave and choir`, `chapter house`,
  `cloister ring`, `ranges`, `gatehouse`, `main range`, `block`, `armoury`, `post`, `annex`, `farmhouse`, `byre`,
  `mill house`, `wheel or sails`, `works shed`, `stable` and `dwelling (staff)`.

*The band anchors differ.* V11 authors four anchor rows (poor, modest, prosperous, rich; squalid reads poor, comfortable
interpolates). V02 authors three (poor, comfortable, rich) and derives modest and prosperous as midpoints. So the size
budget and the massing bend at different bands.

*The result.* The owner's "diversity from a giant manor to a small hovel … Or at least rules associated with that
determination" and "they should scale appropriate in size and structure … this goes for all of the buildings across
prosperity bands" are met for about a tenth of the classes.

**Required change.**

(a) **The resolution chain**, stated once in V2 §3.2 step 2.
- *Main slot:*
  1. `DesignationTemplateRow.composition`;
  2. `<class>/<band>`, the class override rows;
  3. `<templateFamily>/<band>`, where `templateFamily` is `BuildingClassRow.templateFamily` (V11 BCR-V11-1);
  4. `default/<band>`.
- *Any other slot:*
  1. `structures[i].composition`;
  2. `<role>/<band>`, with `role` normalised to a composition role key by one table, `composition.roleKeys` (for
     example `stable` and `stables` → `stables`; `shed` and `sheds` → `shed`);
  3. `outbuilding/<band>`.
- *Cross-volume note.* V11's sentence "templateFamily … not read by any stage" must change to "read by S11's composition
  resolution (V02)". Put it in §13's fix list as a cross-volume item.

(b) **Rows keyed by V11's 34 template families.** Write in V2 §3.10 one row per family. Every family maps onto the V02
shape vocabulary: `dwelling-row` and `lodging-row` → `row`; `dwelling-detached` → the present `cottage` row;
`dwelling-compound` → the present `manor` row; `shopfront`, `workshop-row` and `tavern-row` → `row` with a back range;
`workshop-yard`, `works-noxious`, `yard`, `kiln-yard`, `stable-yard`, `landing-yard` and `farm` → `single`/`L`/`U` rows;
`inn-compound` → the present `inn` row; `hall`, `court-institution` and `market-hall` → `single`/`T`/`H`/`rect` rows;
`church`, `chapel`, `cathedral`, `cloister` and `castle` → `template`; `tower` and `watch-house` → `towerHouse` or
`single`; `bath-house`, `barracks`, `mill` and `extractive` → their own rows. Also write one row for each building
structure role listed above. The present class rows stay only as overrides of their family's row.

(c) **Anchors.** Author every row at V11's four anchors (poor, modest, prosperous, rich), with V11's derivation for
squalid and comfortable. Revise J-V2.30 to "one band-anchoring rule for every template-like table".

(d) **The default rows.** Write the full `default` and `outbuilding` rows:
- `default`: `single` 1; wing width 5–6.5 m; length 8–14 m; no blocks or towers; back range .2; hip share .3; dormers
  per 10 m 0 / 0.3 / 0.6 / 0.8; porch none .8, hood .2; hearths 1.
- `outbuilding`: the present outbuilding row.

(e) **An arrangement law, checked when the tables load.** A row read by a `row` arrangement may carry only the family
`row`. A row read by `compound`, `detached`, `hovel` or `institution` may never carry `row`. A row read by `cluster` or
`tents` carries only `shelter`.

(f) **A new totality test, T-V2-33**, in the PR tier. Every class of the compiled roster (town, city, generic and village)
at every band, and every building structure role of every template, resolves to a row above `default`. The exceptions
are the `generic-*` classes, which are allowed `default`. Every resolved row satisfies (e).

### V2C-02 — blocker — coherence, wiring — two incompatible `HeightsRow` definitions; the roof pitch has two sources; the climate's roof form and the tower and spire columns are unread

**Claim.** Two volumes each declare the row that `DesignationTemplateRow.heights` names, and they disagree:

| | V2 §2.3 | V11 §2.8 `HeightsRow` |
|---|---|---|
| keyed by | class (hovel, cottage, …, tower) | template family |
| fields | `groundStoreyCm`, `storeyCm`, `attic`, `parapetCm` | `storeyCm {ground, upper}`, `roofPitch`, `towerStoreys?`, `spireFactor?` |
| values | e.g. manor 340 / 300 cm | e.g. `dwelling-compound` 380 / 320 cm |

The two also give the roof pitch from different places:
- **V2 M11** says `pitch` is the `ClimateTable` row's default pitch (§4.9), "never a material's".
- **V11** gives every family its own `roofPitch`, and a `ClimateRow.roof` of its own.

Four inputs are then left hanging:
1. **The climate's roof form.** M12 never reads `ClimateRow.roof.form`, so an arid town gets gabled and hipped roofs
   although its climate row says `flat`.
2. **`towerStoreys`.** V11's column, for example church towers 3–5 × 450 cm and cathedral towers 5–8 × 600 cm, is unread.
   M11 raises a tower only by `towerStoreyDelta` (1–2) over its host.
3. **`spireFactor`.** No stage reads it, so no church has a spire.
4. **`attic`.** V02's `attic` column, which gates dormers (V2 §3.7), has no source in V11's row.

**Required change.**

(a) **Delete V2 §2.3's `HeightsRow` interface and V2 §3.10's heights defaults.** Read V11's row. Add a base change request
(to §10.2, in V11's typing) for two columns, `attic: boolean` and `parapetCm: Cm`, with V02 supplying their defaults per
family:
- `attic` is true for every dwelling, shop, workshop, inn, hall and warehouse family, and false for `hovel`, `cluster`,
  `church`, `chapel`, `cathedral`, `extractive` and `kiln-yard`;
- `parapetCm` is 60 for `tower` and `castle`, and 0 otherwise.

(b) **M11 reads `storeyCm.ground` and `storeyCm.upper`.**

(c) **One pitch rule**, stated in M11: `pitch = HeightsRow.roofPitch × (ClimateRow.roof.pitch ÷ pitchRef)`. Here
`pitchRef` is the pitch of the `temperate-deciduous` row, and the result is clamped to [0.3, 1.8] (own).

(d) **The climate's roof form.** Where the site's climate row has `roof.form = 'flat'`, every range, block and back range
takes `flat` with the family's parapet, and no dormers. `hipShare` applies only between gable and hip.

(e) **Towers of the families that carry `towerStoreys`** (church, cathedral, tower, castle) take their floors from that
range, drawn on the `massing` stream at creation index 5, at that family's upper storey height.

(f) **Spires.** A tower whose family carries `spireFactor` takes a `cone` roof (round) or a `pyramid` roof (square or
octagonal) whose rise is `spireFactor × (eaveCm − baseCm)`, stored as the `RoofSpec.pitch` that gives that rise.

(g) **Tests.** Update T-V2-7 accordingly and add a fixture for an arid town and one for a cathedral.

(h) **Decision.** Record the choice as a new J-V2 row: chose V11's family-keyed row with V02's two columns over V02's
class-keyed row, because one content owner and one key avoid a second source of truth.

### V2C-03 — blocker — buildability, coherence — the default rows cannot meet the size budget they are tested against, and the budget itself is incompletely defined

**Claim.** M10 needs a target band, and that band is incompletely defined in three ways:
1. **p10 and p90 are not authored.** M10 reads "the template's `footprintM2` p10 and p90". The base types
   `footprintM2: [p10, p50, p90]` (§10.2), but V11 authors only p50 (V11 §2.8 `TemplateFamilyRow.footprintM2`, "p50 by
   poor, modest, prosperous, rich"), and its compiler states no rule for p10 or p90.
2. **The budget is the plot's, not the slot's.** M10 applies the whole plot's budget to each slot. It ignores
   `structures[i].footprintShare`, so a compound's hall is measured against the budget of hall, kitchen, stable and
   stores together.
3. **Many families are larger than the budget.**
   - *A rich manor.* Its `rect` court is at least `max(2w + courtMinM, 3w)` = 24 m a side at w = 7.5 m and `courtMinM` =
     9 m. That is ≥ 495 m², against V11's `dwelling-compound` rich p50 of 260 m² for the whole compound.
   - *A rich house.* Its `H` is at least 1.4w × w + 2 × (1.5 m + w) × w — ≈ 140 m² at the row's least width 6 m, ≈ 175 m² at its middle width — against a `dwelling-row` or
     `dwelling-detached` rich p50 of 95 or 110 m².

**Why M10 cannot repair this.** M10's repair scales the drawn lengths and re-runs M5 "with the same draws". But M5's
clamps (`Lf ≥ max(2w + courtMinM, 3w)`, `Lh ≥ 1.4w`) push the scaled lengths back up. M10 then drops blocks and towers and
"accepts and reports". So almost every rich court massing overshoots. T-V2-30 (≥ 95 % within [0.9 p10, 1.1 p90]) and
T-V2-29 (rich manors ≥ 60 % in {`rect`, `plotRing`, `U`, `H`}) cannot both pass.

**The owner's direction** decides which way to reconcile: "a very prosperous neighborhood would have fewer buildings but
bigger buildings bigger yards bigger courts".

**Required change.**

(a) **M10's target for slot i:**
`[p10, p90] × footprintShare_i ÷ count_i × buildingScale × sizeFactor`.
- `count_i` is the drawn count of that role.
- `sizeFactor` is the SIZE ladder step's factor (V11 `LadderStepRow.sizeFactor`, item 108), 1 otherwise.
- Where the compiled row has p10 = p50 = p90, use `[sizeBandLo × p50, sizeBandHi × p50]` with `sizeBandLo` = 0.75 and
  `sizeBandHi` = 1.35. Both are constants of `composition.json`, own and calibratable.
- Put in the fix list, as a cross-volume item, that V11's compiler should emit p10 and p90 by the same two ratios.

(b) **A budget test inside M3's feasibility.** A family is infeasible when its least footprint exceeds `1.1 × hi`. Its
least footprint is computed at `w₀`, the row's least lengths and `courtMinM`, in closed form per family (the formulas
belong in M3). M4 then draws only among families that fit.

(c) **M10's shrink.** M10 re-runs M5 with each clamp's lower bound lowered to the M3 minimum. When it still overshoots, it
falls to the next smaller family along the reverse growth lattice (`rect` → `U` → `L` → `single`; `H` → `T` → `single`)
before any "accept and report".

(d) **Reconcile the default rows with the budgets.** Tabulate each family row's median footprint at each anchor. For the
owner's "giant manor … bigger courts", file with V11 a `manor` class override:
- rich: footprint p50 650 m² (p10 450, p90 1 100) and plot area 2 400 m²;
- prosperous: p50 420 m²;
- all values own and calibratable.

Record it as a J-V2 row and in §13's fix list. Until V11 adopts it, V02's rich manor row must drop `rect` and `plotRing`,
a pre-decided fallback stated in the row.

(e) **Run T-V2-30 on B-MASS in the PR tier** against the compiled V11 templates, so the conflict shows before M6a.

### V2C-04 — blocker — owner fidelity (item 28 (f): "same building geometry but not the interior") — the composer's rooms are fixed at the built band and block subdivision

**Claim.**
- **The rooms are read at the built band.** V2 §3.2 step 3 reads every cell of the composition row at the manifested
  band, "never at the occupants' band". This includes `rooms.hall`, `hallLenShare`, `rooms.chapel` and `gateHallM`.
- **The hall is large.** V2 §3.8 emits a hall of 35–55 % of the main range's length, from eave to eave. For a rich
  manor's 22–40 m × 7.5–9.5 m main range that is about 60–190 m².
- **The plan cannot move it.** The base passes every composer's room to the plan as a FIXED ring, kept unchanged through
  every re-cut along the interior ladder (§2.6: "FIXED … the composer's rooms"). V03 E2 keeps it as one room.
- **So the manor cannot be divided.** A manor whose occupants fall to the poor or squalid band cannot divide its
  principal range into the `apartment` or `room` units the ladder requires. The owner's rule is that the interior reads
  who is there ("a mansion district that grows poor keeps its big shells while their interiors fill with families").
- **The rung never notices the change.** The `rooms` rung's recorded inputs do not include the occupants' band, so a band
  change never re-derives it.
- **The hall's size ignores the programme.** V03's hall row is 16 / 24 / 40 m², ×1.25 at rich (V3C-36 proposes
  40 / 80 / 250). A 190 m² fixed hall fits no programme row.

**Required change.**

(a) **Band.** In V2 §3.2 step 3, the `rooms` cells are read at the occupants' band (the plot's `fieldBand` and the current
field value), like the programme (J-A19.43). Every other cell stays at the manifested band.

(b) **When each room is emitted** (V2 §3.8):
- The hall is emitted only where the interior-ladder row at the occupants' band gives the form `house`, or where the class
  is an institution whose programme row names a hall: the inn's common room, a guild or town hall's hall.
- The chapel is emitted under the same rule.
- The gate hall and the tower rooms are shell and circulation rooms and stay at every band.

(c) **Hall size.** The hall's area is clamped into the class programme's `hall` row [min, max] × the ladder row's
`sizeFactor` at the occupants' band. The clamp shortens the hall's length, never its width (which runs eave to eave).

(d) **Staleness.** The `rooms` rung's recorded inputs gain the occupants' band row and the household count (BCR-V2-13).
A band change then marks the rung stale, and the plan's re-cut chain starts from the rooms the new band admits.

(e) **Test T-V2-35.** A rich-band manor at the `poor` occupants' band has no hall or chapel ring. Its plan houses at
least three units in the main range. Returning to the `rich` band restores the as-built rooms exactly (the canonical
chain).

### V2C-05 — major — owner-amendment fidelity (item 59, ADOPTED), parity — blocks get pyramid roofs; square towers cannot stand on blocks

**Claim.** Item 59's ruling ADOPTS "the block/tower distinction (already in `RoofSpec`: `hip` ≠ `pyramid`)" on the code
reading's evidence: "a hip block … whose ridge stops a quarter of its short side from each end — never a pyramid; the
pyramids are square towers standing on blocks" (docs/AMENDMENTS-2026-09-27c.md item 59; research/findings/manor.md, Fable
review B14, which CORRECTED the earlier "when square its roof is a pyramid"). V02 does the opposite: M12 "`block` by
`WingRoleRow`, `pyramid` when `|lb − db| ≤ 0.1 × lb`", and V2 §3.10's `WingRoleRow` gives blocks "roofs hip .7 / pyramid
.3 (pyramid when square)". V02's hip is an equal-pitch hip (V2 §5.2 step 2: the ridge end pulled in by half the span),
which collapses a square block to a pyramid, whereas the reference's block keeps a ridge half its short side long. And M8
(iv) keeps every tower 0.5 m from every block, so the reference's 4-facet towers standing on blocks (9 of 29 typed towers,
README-frequencies §4) have no counterpart.

**Required change.** (a) M12 and `WingRoleRow`: a `block`'s roof is always `hip`; delete the pyramid weight and the square
rule. (b) Add `WingRoleRow.hipInset` (the ridge end's inset as a share of the span; `hip` roofs only): default 0.5 (equal
pitches) for ranges, blocks 0.25 under the `FM` preset — calibrated at M6c from the captures' draw records

closest alternatives." The fold's own §4.6 cites R2 for its bends, but it also refuses five declarations that are neither

* "`removeWing` on a stair is **refused**, named, offering `tiers { stairs }` (J-V2.76)" (V2 §4.6 (3));
* "`addRange`, `addTower`, `addBlock` and `backRange` are **refused**, named, offering `family` (J-V2.73)" (V2 §4.6 (3));
* "a `family: 'stepped'` op on a deck building is refused, named" (V2 §4.6 (4));
* the `tiers` op "Only on a `stepped` building (elsewhere refused, named, offering `family: 'stepped'`)" (V2 §4.6 (1)).

J-V2.73's reason ("they would break the stack's nesting and its stair chain") holds only for a wing placed **on** a tier.
A range, a tower or a back range standing on the ground beside tier 0 breaks neither: ST6's nesting reads only wings with
`on`, and the ground wings' tiling is M13's ordinary pairing. Critique-V9's V9C-59 (4) proposes to widen volume 9's
refusal list by "a rule of a building's structure or of nature". That changes the design around the owner rule rather
than meeting it, so it cannot stand: the owner's rules outrank the volumes. The revision's two refusals fail the same test.
These are V2 §4.2 step 1's `removeWing` of the last wing, and `reshapeRange` or `removeWing` on a row quad.

**Required change** (V2 §4.2 step 1, §4.6 (1), (3), (4); §2.6's `MassingRef.stack.stairs` and `TierCells.stairs`; ST3,

1. **`tiers` on a building that is not stepped** bends to `setMassing { op: 'family', family: 'stepped' }`, followed in
   the same event by the op's values. This is V2 §3.15.5's anchored `single → stepped` on the building's `main` range,
   passing through `single` by the growth rows. It is named: "Made it a stepped building first."
2. **A sealed stair form `none`.** `MassingRef.stack.stairs` and `TierCells.stairs` gain `'none'`. It is lawful only over

#### V2C-52 — major — buildability (the roofscape), rendering — a pent skirt's `ridgeCm` does not give the pitch the roofscape draws

**Claim.** ST4 gives a pent tier the roof `hip` (rect), `pyramid` (octagon) or `cone` (round), with `ridgeCm = eaveCm +
p_k × d_{k+1}`: "the line where the skirt meets the tier above, the facets beyond it occluded" (J-V2.75). But V2 §5.2
builds a hip's facets from the eaves to the ridge **at the wing's midline** (step 2), and a pyramid's or cone's from the
ring to **one apex over its centroid** (step 3), taking z from `ridgeCm`. With `ridgeCm = eave + p_k × d`, the facet
planes rise `p_k × d` over the half span. Their pitch is therefore `p_k × d ÷ (span ÷ 2)`, not `p_k`. At the tier above's
wall, a distance `d` in, the skirt stands only `p_k × d² ÷ (span ÷ 2)` above the eave. For a 12 m tier with `d` = 1.1 m and
`p_k` = 1, that is ≈ 0.2 m instead of the 1.1 m ST4 intends. Step 4's occlusion cuts the facets in plan only, and the
casters and glTF roofs read the same wrong facets. The load checks also allow `lowerRoof: 'pent'` with `axial` or
`flights` stairs, whose front inset differs from the side insets, so there is no single `d_{k+1}`.

**Required change** (V2 §5.2 step 3, BCR-V2-15's roofscape; V2 §3.15.4's load checks; J-V2.75 amended):
1. **A pent band facet rule.** For a wing `x` with a pent roof and the wing `y` standing on it, the facets are the band
   between `x`'s ring and `y`'s ring:
   * for each side of `x`'s ring, one planar facet runs from that side at `eaveCm` to the matching side of `y`'s ring at
     `ridgeCm`, and neighbouring facets share their corner edges as hip lines;
   * a round or octagonal ring is treated per edge (a cone's band);
   * no facet is built inside `y`'s ring.
   `ridgeCm = eaveCm + p_k × d_{k+1}` then means what ST4 says, and the casters and the glTF roofs read these facets.
2. **Load checks.** `lowerRoof: 'pent'` only with `stairs: 'internal'` or `'none'`, where the insets are equal on every
   side. A pent tier with nothing standing on it (a truncated top) takes the top's roof instead.
3. T-V2-44 gains: every pent facet meets the wall of the tier above at `ridgeCm` within 1 cm. T-V2-9's tiling runs on the

### 11.3 Minors

#### V2C-60 — minor — buildability (units) — `deckLevel` subtracts a parapet a solid tier never added

**Claim.** ST1 adds `parapetCm` only to a **storeyed** tier's height "where its roof is flat". ST4 sets every tier's
`eaveCm = heightCm_k`. But `deckLevel(w)` subtracts "the family's `parapetCm` when w's roof is flat" for every wing. On a
solid flat tier of a family whose `parapetCm` > 0 (`tower` and `castle` carry 60 cm, V2 §3.10.6), the wing standing on it
therefore sinks by the parapet. T-V2-44 compares `baseCm` with the same `deckLevel`, so it cannot catch this.
**Required change.** ST4 adds `parapetCm` to every flat-roofed tier's `eaveCm`, solid or storeyed, so the eave is the
parapet's top. ST0's `h₀` and ST5's `rise_k` read `heightCm_k` without it. `deckLevel` then subtracts it exactly where it
was added. T-V2-44 gains: `deckLevel(x) = x.baseCm + heightCm_x` for every tier.

#### V2C-61 — minor — coherence (base `Storey`, `Building.heights`), wiring (volume 3) — a stack's storey numbering is left half-stated

which the base defines as "the building's max over its wings". On a terraced palace of four two-storey tiers that max is

critique-V3's `floorCm(w, s) = w.heights.baseCm + (s − s₀(w)) × storeyCm(w)` (V3C-65 (2)) assumes one height per wing.
**Required change.**
1. BCR-V2-33 amends `Storey.level`'s comment to "0 the lowest storeyed level: the ground, except on a stack whose ground
   wings are solid (volume 2's `storeyRange`)".
2. The envelope rule and BCR-V2-32's `heights` comment set `Building.heights.floors = 1 + max storeyRange[1]` over the

#### V2C-62 — minor — buildability — the flights do not meet, and a round stack's axial stair is one chord wide

**Claim.** ST5 lays flight k from the face's left end for even k and from its right end for odd k, "so each flight's foot
lies near the last one's head". It does not. Flight k − 1's head lies at `left + run`, but flight k's foot is at tier k's
right end, which is near only when the run is about the face's length. On a round or octagonal stack, the axial stair's
width is "`min(sw, the length of tier k's front side)`", and a round ring's front side is one chord. A 24-gon of 5 m
radius has a 1.3 m chord, against the row's `stairWidthM` of 3–6 m.
**Required change.**
1. ST5: flight k (k ≥ 1) starts at the point of tier k's front face nearest flight k − 1's head, clamped so the flight fits
   the face, and climbs toward the face's end that has room. ST3's front-face test adds that offset to `run_k + landingM`.
2. ST2 and ST3: on a round or octagonal stack under `axial`, each ring's front chords within `(sw + 2 × stairClearM) ÷ 2`
   of the centre line merge into one straight edge (a D-shaped front), so the stair keeps its width. ST6's regularity
   check exempts that edge.
