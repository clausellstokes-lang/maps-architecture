## 0 Verdict in plain words

Volume 5 is a careful and mostly buildable design, and its core should stay. Islands appear only in rich cities with the
fantasy setting on. They carry either a rich neighbourhood or one great institution, and the city's population stays whole.
Each island has a rocky underside whose shape really drives its shadow, both in the picture and in the shade that makes
the ground below a little poorer. People reach it by hoists and rope lines, and an island that loses its magic sinks and
falls slowly over the years.

Two things must change before it is built. First, the "shade catches up at the next epoch" rule cannot survive saving and
reopening a map: the base recomputes the prosperity field from the island's current position when a map loads, so the lag
the volume promises disappears on reload. Second, when a cathedral or palace moves up to an island, the base has already
reserved its ground site at the town's plaza, and nothing says who gets that empty site. The same gap leaves the owner's
new catacomb rule without a church to sit under. (Since corrected by the owner, ~20:05: catacombs now lie only under
cemeteries, and churches get crypts, the cathedral always the largest. What remains for an island is the cathedral's
crypt, which must sit in the island's own rock, and the island's churchyards, which must never become catacomb sources;
see the correction under C5-02.)

Several owner rules from file g reach this volume and are not yet in it: walls using natural barriers (an island's rim is
the perfect cliff), the supply web, spread and pull, institution ladders, and the catacomb and magical-node parts of item
109. Three smaller owner points also need work. A rich island almost never becomes the "set of neighbourhoods" the owner
described. An island's single, uniform wealth value cannot house both of the bands it carries. And the rule of one
apothecary per district is weakened on islands. The rest are fixes to wiring, tests and wording.

## 2 Findings

### 2.1 Blockers

**C5-01 — blocker — lifecycle and determinism (item 102 (e); J-V5.27). The shade lag does not survive a save and load.**
The volume holds each island's strip "with the field's rasters, re-derived on load and replay" (V5 §2.6's strip row). It
also promises that no island edit changes the field until the next epoch or a Re-derive from S10a (V5 §3.19 (6), §4.3,
J-V5.27). But the base does not store the fields: "an editable document recomputes them from their drivers and the stroke
list on load" (§2.9 *What the snapshot holds*; J-DET-12).

After an island is moved, raised, resized, reshaped, given a new underside or rerolled, and before the next epoch, the
session's field still reads the strip at its old basis. A reload re-derives the strip from the island as it now stands, so
the field changes on load. From then on, every re-derivation of blocks (a field brush, any edit that re-derives blocks past
τ) reads a different field than the session that never saved. The derivation records then disagree with a replay. The same
break hits SKY-F's clearance raise, whose strip "keeps its pass-2 basis until the next epoch" (V5 §3.9 (5)). A strip cannot
be re-derived from the live island, because the inputs it was computed from are not stored anywhere.

*Required change:*
(1) Add a persisted `SkyIsland.shadeBasis: { epoch: number; anchor: PointCm; elevationCm: Cm; groundRefCm: Cm;
surfaceTopCm: Cm; underside: UndersideProfile; rim: PolygonCm /* local lattice points, a copy that is no vertex — §2.2
rule 5's precedent is Works.before */ }`. Field pass 2 writes it (at generation and at every Re-derive from S10a or an
earlier stage), and so does each epoch's S6 re-evaluation. No island edit ever writes it. It sits in the model digest.
(2) `islandShadeStrip` reads `shadeBasis`, never the live island. Only the shade view's pending outline reads the live
island (V5 §3.19 (8)). V5 §2.3a's `basis` becomes the hash of `shadeBasis`.
(3) Add the lifecycle row to V5 §2.6:
* create: at pass 2;
* read: by the strips on load, on replay and at every field recomputation;
* persist: in the snapshot;
* regenerate: rewritten only at the moments above;
* undo: restored by the undo record of the event that rewrote it;
* migrate: when absent, computed from the island as stored.
(4) Add BCR-V5-1's field and a BCR to §2.9 naming `shadeBasis` as the one sky input a field recomputation on load reads.
(5) Add bar T-V5-21 (h). Move an island, save, reload, then re-derive a block with a prosperity brush. The field raster and
every derivation record must equal those of the same session without the save, byte for byte. Run the same check after
F-SKY-CLEAR.
(6) V5 §6's memory and document rows gain ≤ 2.5 KB per island (the rim copy has ≤ 256 vertices).

**C5-02 — blocker — buildability and coherence (§4.2 S3 (2); V11's `cathedral` and `palace` rows; item 109 (k)). An
institution island can leave its ground reservation empty, and the catacomb then has no source.**
S3 reserves "the temple or cathedral precinct adjacent to the main plaza by score, sized from its institution row" (§4.2
S3 (2)), and V11 sites the `palace` by "the castle's reservation rules (§4.2 S3)". S7b (5) draws an island's reason only
after S3–S7 have run, so taking the cathedral or the palace up to an island leaves its S3 reservation on the ground with no
owner. V5 §3.7 (3)(c) even scores a cathedral island toward "the precinct", which assumes that precinct stands empty.
Nothing in the volume says what fills it, so the implementer must guess. Item 109 (k) then has nothing to sit under: it
puts catacombs "only under the largest church or cathedral … the largest one is the cathedral", while the owner's item 2
says an island has "no relation with the sewer system or underground".

*Required change:*
(1) V5 §3.5 *An institution slot* gains a step, **the ground reservation's hand-over**. When the row's instance holds an S3
reservation, the reservation stays exactly as it is, with no L1 change, and passes to the next instance given by a new
`IslandInstitution.handover` column:
* `cathedral`: the roster's largest church (the greatest `church`-family instance by the class's size rank, then by
  ordinal), with its churchyard. The town's mother church stands in the old close.
* `palace`: the castle row, if the roster demands one. Otherwise the government's next seat class (§10.2's government row).
  Otherwise the reservation is re-typed `park`, as the palace gardens.
* `prison` and `tower`: none (both are sited by S10b).
The report and the clerk name the hand-over.
(2) S3 reads the creation prefix ahead. A cathedral or palace that a T5 `sky` declaration or a T6 `criteria.sky` assignment
sends to an island is not reserved on the ground. This needs a BCR to §4.2 S3 (2): "… not reserved where the creation
prefix sends its institution to a sky island (V05 §3.5)".
(3) Item 109 (k): an island's cathedral is never a catacomb source (item 2). "The largest church" means the largest church
on the ground, which is the hand-over church in the old close. Churches on islands still count toward
`catacomb.churchesPer`, the count that sets how many catacombs a CITY or METROPOLIS gets. State this in V5 §1.3, add
J-V5.43, and list it for V04's revision.
(4) Add bars. T-V5-4 gains: "no S3 reservation is left without an owner by an institution slot (100 %)". A new T-V5-26:
a CITY with a cathedral island and a catacomb puts that catacomb, the largest one, under the hand-over church.

**C5-02, post-landing correction (owner, 2026-09-28 ~20:05: "switch it then, cathedral also gets crypts but cemetaries get
catacombs"; item 109 (k) (2)–(3) as revised; written 20:59 under the resume rule, nothing else in this file changed).**
Required changes (1), (2) and (4)'s T-V5-4 clause stand: the S3 hand-over gap is unchanged by the revision. Required change
(3) and (4)'s T-V5-26 are REPLACED by (3a)–(3c) and (4′), because catacombs now lie only under cemeteries (a churchyard, a
separate burial ground, a monastery's burial ground, a cemetery outside the walls) and, at CITY and METROPOLIS, under a
worked-out quarry turned ossuary, while churches get crypts and the cathedral always has the settlement's largest.
(3a) *Island churchyards are never catacomb sources.* The base gives every church and temple plot a churchyard part (§4.9's
churchyard row), so a cathedral island's plot, and any church the fill carries, would otherwise field a cemetery. V5 §1.3
and V5 §3.5 state that a sky plot's churchyard is a surface burial ground only (item 2). It is left out of item 109 (k)
(3)'s candidate list and out of its "largest cemetery" ranking. Churches on islands still count once toward
`catacomb.churchesPer`'s count (item 108; C5-17), because the dossier's church records set that count wherever the
churches stand.
(3b) *The island cathedral's crypt.* The cathedral always has a crypt, the largest in the settlement (109 (k) (2); V11's
crypt row after its revision). On an island that crypt is a burial vault cut into the island's rock under the cathedral's
footprint. It lies inside tier 0, the rim slab (`rimThicknessM` 10 m, V5 §2.4's `underside` row), so it exists for every
underside kind, `flat` included. It is part of the building (the cellar storey of the base's cellar row, `depthCm` 220), not
an `UndergroundSystem` part and not an item 109 (j) (3b) link, and no catacomb can grow beneath it. SKY checks that the
crypt's floor lies at least `IslandRule.cryptFloorM` (1 m, own) above `elevationCm − rimThicknessCm` at every point of the
footprint. Where an `own` relief basin dips too low, the claim's site search (C5-45) scores that site out. If no site
passes, the crypt is a half-sunk room (the form V02's and V03's revisions own), listed as a fate. The wet-ground gate never
removes the cathedral's crypt (V11's revision). The ground's hand-over church in the old close keeps its own crypt by the
crypted-class rows and is never ranked above the cathedral's.
(3c) Record J-V5.43 as: "chose a vault in the island's own rock for an island cathedral's crypt over a ground crypt or no
crypt, because only that keeps both the owner's crypt rule (109 (k) (2)) and item 2's 'no relation with the underground'".
List for V04's revision that the catacomb candidate list skips sky-arena plots. List for V11's that the crypt row admits a
sky plot, as a vault within the rim slab.
(4′) T-V5-26 becomes: "on 200 CITY and METROPOLIS seeds with a cathedral island: the cathedral carries the settlement's
largest crypt, inside the island's rim slab (100 %); 0 underground parts, links or catacomb sources on any island (100 %);
every catacomb lies under a ground cemetery or a quarry ossuary; island churches are counted once in the catacomb count".

### 2.2 Majors

**C5-03 — major — owner fidelity (owner: "1 to 3 floating land masses in the sky as a collection of neighborhoods or one
neighborhood"; item 2 "a very prosperous neighbourhood (or set)"). The "set of neighbourhoods" cannot happen at CITY.**
V01 §3.10.3 (2) counts an island's districts as `k = clamp(⌈allocated plot land ÷ (0.62 × the town's mean district
area)⌉, 1, 3)`. V5 §2.4 caps an island at `areaHaMax` 10 ha (CITY) and 20 ha (METROPOLIS), and itself notes that a CITY
district is about 12–16 ha of built land. A fully supplied CITY island (6.2 ha of plot land) therefore always gets k = 1. A
METROPOLIS island gets at most 2, and three never occur (receipt (3)). The fill's three-districts cap (V5 §3.5 (2)) never
binds.

*Required change:*
(1) Add `IslandRule.districtHa` to V5 §2.4: 3.5 ha at CITY and 4.5 ha at METROPOLIS of land per island district, about one
Neighbourhood patch (own, calibratable). `SkyHandover` carries it.
(2) List for V01's revision that a `sky` request's k reads `districtHa` in place of the town's mean district area.
(3) Add J-V5.45.
(4) T-V5-9 gains: "an island with `h_carried` = `h_town` = 1 has 3 districts at METROPOLIS and at least 2 at CITY on ≥ 90 %
of seeds".

**C5-04 — major — owner fidelity ("prosperity is a gradient and a band") and buildability (§4.7 S10a (ii)–(iii)). A single,
uniform island value cannot house the bands the island carries.**
V5 §3.11 gives an island one prosperity value, `S_carried`, plus ε at share 0.05 (J-V5.18). Yet the fill takes the next
band once the richest is used up (V5 §3.5 (2)), so a prosperity island usually carries two bands. S10a then houses
"households by band … by capacity per band under pass 2" and re-homes "any household S7b allocated outside [the district's
band range]". A uniform field gives all of the island's capacity to one band. The poorer band's households either have no
capacity or are re-homed to the ground. F-SKY-UNIT reports them as overflow, and the fill's band sums (`S_carried`, `A`)
no longer describe the people who actually live on the island.

*Required change:*
(1) Rewrite V5 §3.11 as a gradient: `P_I(x) = Q(r(x)) + ε(x) − w_sun · shadeSky(x)`.
* `r(x)` ∈ [0, 1] is a site rank over the island's land. A new row `IslandRule.field.rank` sets it: 0.6 × the terrain's
  elevation rank + 0.4 × a rim-view rank (the rim stretch that faces the ground's centre ranks first); own, calibratable.
* `Q` maps rank to value so that each band's share of the island's land equals that band's share of the carried plot land
  (Σ `plotArea(c, b)` × count). Within its slice, each band maps linearly between its two `FieldBands` cuts.
(2) An institution island stays uniform at `v(requirementBand)`. `SkyIsland.field.prosperity` becomes the gradient's mean.
(3) Revise J-V5.18.
(4) T-V5-9 gains: "on 500 seeds, 0 households re-homed from an island by S10a's band-range step, and each carried band's
share of the island's plot land within ± 5 % of its carried share".

**C5-05 — major — buildability (V5 §3.8 (1)). The arena's frame can be too small for an elongated island.**
The frame's side, `2.6 × √(1.5 · areaHaMax · 10⁴ ÷ π)`, is sized for a disc: 568 m at CITY and 803 m at METROPOLIS. But
§4.2 S1 (2)'s spindle, lozenge and crescent templates are elongated (the routine's clamp speaks of "elongated ovals"), and
the outline turns to the river's bearing. A CITY spindle with aspect ratio 3 has a 618 m long axis at its own area and
757 m at 1.5 × growth, both longer than the frame's 568 m side (receipt (2)). The L0 batch then fails, or the rim is
clipped by the frame.

*Required change:*
(1) Compute the frame after V5 §3.6 (3). Its side is 2 × (the outline's greatest distance from its centroid) × √1.5, for
growth headroom, plus 2 × `IslandRule.frameMarginM` (40 m, own), rounded up to 10 m.
(2) Keep §3.2 (9)'s frame growth for anything larger.
(3) T-V5-6 gains: "for every template at aspect up to its row's maximum, 100 % of outlines, grown to 1.5 × `areaHaMax`,
lie inside their frame".

**C5-06 — major — buildability and realism (V5 §3.7, §3.10, T-V5-8). Placement never checks that the island can be
reached.**
Over-water sites score highest (weight 1.0), and a rope line's run is at most 350 m. An island placed over a lake, estuary
or sea more than 350 m from admissible land therefore has no admitted access. F-SKY-ACCESS's last rung drops its accesses
and reports it `unreachable`, so T-V5-8's "≤ 1 %" depends on the site's geography.

*Required change:*
(1) V5 §3.7 (2) admits a candidate only if its translated rim has at least one point over an admissible ground face (for a
hoist or stair), or within the `ropeway` row's run of one. The test reads a 64 m distance transform to admissible land,
built once with the class raster (≤ 1 ms at METROPOLIS).
(2) F-SKY-PLACE keeps its rungs.
(3) T-V5-8's `unreachable` bar becomes 0 % for islands placed by rule. A declared island keeps the report.

**C5-07 — major — buildability (V5 §3.10 (4), J-V5.17). A ground landing's yard may have no street to face.**
The yard is claimed "touching the spine", which needs an L1 way spine within `wayReachM` (60 m) when SKY runs. At that
moment S8 has not yet laid the district's lanes, so only S3–S7's arterials and boundary ways exist. A foot more than 60 m
from those ways gets no spine, and no rung says what happens next. S8 treats a reservation as a hole (§4.6) and never seeks
its attach vertex.

*Required change:*
(1) V5 §3.10 (4): where no spine lies within `wayReachM`, claim the yard at the foot and register its attach vertex as an
S8 destination.
(2) Add a BCR to §4.6: "a `landing` reservation's attach is a destination the district's infill must reach; the bent cut's
first cut in that face runs from the attach to the nearest way, by §4.6's lane rule, as a gate's attach is reached".
(3) SKY-F checks it alongside the island-side check (V5 §3.12).
(4) T-V5-8 gains: "every ground landing's attach met by a way after S8 (100 %)".

**C5-08 — major — performance (§8.4 (c); V5 §5.3 (2), §6). One island lengthens every shadow ray on the whole map.**
§8.4 (c)'s window for the global light is R = (Z − z) ÷ (m − σ_s), where Z is the display list's `surfaceTopCm`, which
counts "a slab's top: an island's" (§8.1). An island at 150–250 m lifts Z from the roofs' 30–40 m to the island's top for
every pixel of every tile, so every ground ray's window grows five- to eightfold, not only on tiles an island covers. V5 §6
budgets covered tiles only, and V5 §5.3 (2) says "tiles no island touches are unchanged". An island's shadow also reaches
tiles its land never touches: under the lowest sample it lands up to 8 × its height away.

*Required change:*
(1) Add a BCR to §8.4 (c): the window reads the surface layers' own top, without slabs. Each slab that crosses the ray's
plan is tested exactly by its outline over the slab's own reach, (slab top − z) ÷ (m − σ_s). The traversal's value is
unchanged, because slabs are already tested exactly.
(2) V5 §6 gains the row "the light pass on tiles no island's land covers: ≤ + 5 % at METROPOLIS with three islands",
measured at P3. The fallback culls slabs per tile by the bounding box of their shadow sweep.
(3) Correct V5 §5.3 (2): a tile that no island's land touches takes no second base, but it is re-lit where an island's
shadow falls on it.

**C5-09 — major — casual-user UX (item 68: "the global light is the cursor"; J-V5.14). Panning an island into view jolts
every shadow.**
The cursor sun's floor, H = max(h₀·D·s, 1.5 × the top), holds "while a visible island is drawn". At a close zoom, h₀·D is
about 100–200 m against 1.5 × a 250 m top. Panning an island into or out of view therefore changes H for every building's
shadow on screen, and all of them shorten or lengthen abruptly mid-pan.

*Required change:*
(1) The floor holds whenever the sky layer is visible and the document has an unfallen island, whatever is in view. H then
depends on the zoom, which already moves it, and never on panning.
(2) Revise J-V5.14 and BCR-V5-13's (d) text to match.
(3) T-V5-13 (d) gains: "at a fixed zoom, panning changes H by 0".

**C5-10 — major — the picture against the model (J98; V5 §5.3 (3), T-V5-20). Rung 3's `drop` form breaks the root's
shadow into beads.**
Rung 3 projects each tier "in the `drop` form … from its underside", and a drop offsets the ring by one height only. A tier
spanning [b, t] therefore leaves the band between its offsets b·c and t·c uncovered. On an 8 ha `root` under sample 0,
each tier is 12 m thick and sweeps 96 m, while tiers 3–6 are 80, 35, 9 and 0 m across (receipt (4)). Their drops come out
disjoint, so the root's tail, which is the owner's J98 subject, draws as a row of beads. T-V5-20's "covers exactly the cells
the strip marks" then fails.

*Required change:*
(1) V5 §5.3 (3): project every tier in the `prism` form (the ring swept from b·c to t·c, then unioned), whatever
`vector.projected` says, because an island is a floating solid.
(2) Use `drop` only when §8.4's rung-3 budget fallback fires, and then subdivide the tiers so that no tier's sweep exceeds
its ring's inradius.
(3) T-V5-13 gains (h): "at 15°, the rung-3 shadow of the `root` is one connected region".

**C5-11 — major — coherence (V5 §5.1 against V09 §2.7, §5.4 and §6.4). The volume and V09 design the one shade view
differently.**
V09 draws the daylight share as six stepped veils and outlines "the strip a floating island's shadow sweeps … in a dashed
ink line labelled with the island's name". V05 draws each strip as three nested filled areas (for ≥ 1, 3 and 5 samples)
and uses a dashed outline for a *pending* strip. So a dashed line means "the island's shade" in one volume and "where the
shade will move" in the other, and V05's filled areas duplicate V09's veil.

V09's on-demand sampler (used when `w_sun` = 0) is also "invalidated by any event that changes a caster (… islands …)". It
would show a moved island's new shade as current, while at `w_sun` > 0 the view shows the held field. The same lag would
read differently depending on a weight setting.

*Required change:*
(1) V5 §5.1 draws outlines only. `sky.island.shadeStrip` becomes a solid ink line around the strip's ≥ 3-sample area at its
basis, labelled with the island's name. `sky.island.shadePending` is the dashed line. The veil is V09's alone.
(2) V5 §3.19 (6): the strips are computed at pass 2 and at each epoch whatever `w_sun` is; the fold multiplies them by it.
V09's accessor then reads the held strips (after C5-01, from `shadeBasis`) and never samples islands on demand.
(3) List both changes for V09's revision: its §5.4 sentence, and its §6.4 invalidation list, which drops "islands".
(4) T-V5-21 (b) gains: "an island edit leaves the shade view's veil unchanged; only the pending outline moves".

**C5-12 — major — coherence and wiring (V5 §2.3, §2.4, §3.12; V01 §2.1, §2.4, §3.10.3; V11 §3.5.11). The hand-over
contract with V01 and V11 uses names that do not exist and meanings that differ.**
* (a) The palace row's `fabricHint` is `axis`. That is not a `FabricStrategyId` (V01 §2.1 lists bent-cut, street, square,
  ring, quay, wallside, crossroads, landmark, bridgehead and stepped); `axis` is one of V01's `Anchor` values.
* (b) V5 §2.3 calls `fabricHint` "the weight row S7c reads", but V5 §3.12 makes it the request's `strategy` (`ring` from
  6 ha, else `bent-cut`). V01 §3.10.3 (4) runs S7c on each island district, so it is unclear whether the hint forces a
  template or weights one.
* (c) The `tower` row's tag `arcane_tower` is no V11 class. V11 §3.5.11 has `mage_tower` (dispersed) and `mages_guild`
  (singular).
* (d) V01 §3.10.3 (2) types a prosperity island's largest face as "the kind of the town's richest district", and an
