## §1 Evidence base for Dwellings — what kind of evidence exists, and how far each kind reaches

Dwellings is the reference whose evidence class sits between MFCG's and FTG's, with one thing neither has. Like MFCG, VG and NG it is **client-side Haxe/OpenFL**, so the profiler records a **complete call census** (every class and method that ran, how often, nested how), the build prints its **own stage trace** (`Blueprint.hx:26`/`Drafter.hx:59`, `House.hx:41–42`, the two `HouseType.hx:22` programme lists, the `House.hx:373` trapdoor line), and the model is **byte-deterministic per permalink** across browser sessions. Like FTG it has **no open-source ancestor** (the 2017 TownGeneratorOS has no floor-plan stage; Dark Clues, "from which Dwellings has grown out", is a locked post with two blurred previews) and its creator describes mechanisms in prose that names the idea and never the constants ("recursive bisection with a bend … just an idea, not an algorithm"). What it has and no other reference had is **the generator's own export of its model**: twelve houses' JSON (rooms as cell sets, doors of three kinds, windows, stair pairs, the exit, the spiral) and all-floors SVG, owner-approved, plus **vertex records of every storey view of 92 houses** — so the output side is pinned cell for cell, not only statistically.

### §1.1 Binding corrections applied in this assessment (critic rulings B1–B17 supersede the readers; the reader text they replace is named)

* **B1 Roofs.** Each above-ground storey's roof is the **straight skeleton of that storey's whole outline** (one hip face per outline edge; the spiral shaft adds a cone), storeys painted bottom-up with lower levels faded: roof faces = Σ outline corners in 82/92 walked houses, the other 10 exactly +1 and all spiral houses (`floors-geometry.out.txt`, read on disk). Supersedes `profile-dwellings.md` §4.12 and `dwellings-exports.md` §2.8 ("per block"); upholds `dwellings-posts.md` §2(j).
* **B2 Cost model.** `House.assignRooms` **includes furnishing**: `Room.fill` (furniture, `Clutter.*`, lights) is nested inside it in 38/38 profiled generations (`cost-nesting.out.txt`). Validation proper (`assignSet` ×2) medians 0.1 / 1.9 / 4.7 ms, furnishing and lights 0.6 / 0.9 / 0.8 ms, partition and doors (`House.build`) 0.3 / 1.0 / 0.8 ms, whole model 1.9 / 4.1 / 8.0 ms (small / medium / large, Enter). "Role assignment is the expensive part" holds only for large houses. Supersedes `profile-dwellings.md` headline 8 and §5.1's reading.
* **B3 Doors.** `Door.getPrice = 2 × Door.new` is **struck** (0/96, ratios 3.0–7.1, `calls-identities.out.txt`). `Door.new = Room.link` (96/96) and `Room.unlink` 0–15 stand as counts; the exported door set = rooms − storeys per house (a spanning tree) and is **not** the surviving `Door` objects (equal in 8/12 houses, R16 OPEN). The connection algorithm stays [INFERRED] "a priced spanning connection of the rooms".
* **B4 Topology.** Trees in 12/12 exported houses (CONFIRMED); the creator states loops occur "quite rarely" and gives one permalink [STATED]; nobody opened it. Cite "trees in 12/12; loops rare and unverified". Our rung may not assume a tree.
* **B5 `square`.** Requests a single rectangle (`Polyomino.create` = 0 in 5/5); square in 1/5. Corrects `dwellings-posts.md` §2(a)/§2(l).
* **B6 Basements.** A **target of roughly 9–20 cells (mean ≈ 14)** inside the ground floor, equal to the ground floor when that is small (small houses 6/6); probability **≈ 0.78 regardless of storey count** (11/14 single-storey, 18/23 multi-storey independent draws, `stairwell-basement.out.txt`; PLAUSIBLE at this n). The creator's 2023 "more likely with high ones" and 2024 "50 % for a single-storey house" are version statements, not rules of this build; `profile-dwellings.md` §4.3's "cap of roughly 15 cells" and its per-band shares (which count duplicate houses, B14) are replaced.
* **B7 Bathroom–bedroom attachment.** **Not present** in this build (0/12 bathrooms share a door or even a wall with a bedroom, `exports-graph.py`). The 1.3.0 sentence is not a rule to carry; the posts lane's "one adjacency hint" and the profile lane's schema field are dropped.
* **B8 Stairwell.** Stacked stairs are **rolled by the generator itself for about half of multi-storey houses** (9/21 independent untagged draws took the stairwell path, 7 ended stacked); the tag forces it (4/5). Supersedes `profile-dwellings.md` §4.6's "three untagged houses".
* **B9 Connected part.** A multi-part plan builds the part containing the **last filled cell in row-major order** (7/7 plans, 14 loads), not "the largest" (the creator's 1.3.3 sentence); holes are filled. Integrators must pass one connected footprint.
* **B10 URL surface.** `seed`, `tags`, `floors` (dialog 1–8, ≤ 12 accepted), `plan` + `w` + `h` (≤ 11 × 11, hex bitmask row-major LSB-first, R1), `entrance` (`y₁x₁y₂x₂` base-12, clockwise boundary edge, R2); rewritten after every generation; re-rolls not recorded. Storeys *can* be passed (corrects `dwellings-posts.md` headline 2's "storeys … never passed"); what MFCG / VG / NG / UP / Tiny Pubs actually send is **unmeasured** (critic gap 1).
* **B11 Tag semantics.** The build's own one-line descriptions are the authority (small / medium / large ground floor; `square` rectangular; `low` / `tall`; `slab` all floors the same shape; `mechanical` inner walls bisect the outer shape / `organic` follow it; `hallways` more corridors and nooks; `spiral`; `stairwell`; `basement`; `blank` / `transparent` as few / as many windows; `generic` no special rooms); conflicting pairs resolve to the first of size → height → partition → windows → stairs; unknown words are inert.

## §2 Capability matrix

Scale for the four confidences: probability in [0, 1] that an expert team, working clean-room from this evidence base only, achieves the stated goal for that capability. `functional` (F) = same feature, same option surface, statistically similar structure; `visual` (V) = a viewer places the output in the same quality/style class; `algorithm identity` (A) = our derivation matches THEIR algorithm, not merely its output ("deriving their code"); `seed-exact` (S) = same seed → same house, which needs their exact RNG, draw order and constants. Effort is expert person-weeks for parity of that capability in a Dwellings-alone build (not the owner's rung — that is the architecture lane's estimate; where the row's evidence carries over to item 28 the row says so). Confidences are deliberately non-uniform; each carries a one-line justification, the drivers that would move it and the cheapest experiment that would. Tags: [STATED] creator text or the build's own printed descriptions; [SHOWN] recorded, measured or visible; [INFERRED] reconstruction; [NAMED-ONLY] a class, method or feature named, no method.

**Seed-exact convention for Dwellings.** Unlike FTG, the reference **has** a seed model and is byte-deterministic per permalink (B12) — so the column has a target. But nothing about the stream is pinned: the PRNG behind `com.watabou.utils.Random` is a name (no orbit test was executed for Dwellings; every recorded seed lies below 2³¹ − 1, max 2,141,439,973 [SHOWN], which is consistent with the 31-bit Lehmer family the creator's other generators use — MFCG and NG CONFIRMED on 48271, VG ruled 0.40 — and is PLAUSIBLE only); `Random.float` runs 467–2,632 times per house with its draw sites inlined; `Random.reset` runs 4 times on a load and 3 on Enter with the seed path unknown; the next Enter seed is drawn from the live state after the whole generation has consumed it. Under R-B every generative row carries S ≈ 0.01–0.02 (the value of the geometry it consumes, with no CONFIRMED stream to except); rows that are pure data, formats or deterministic transforms of the model carry "—" and the annotation *det. given model* where the fact is useful to an architect; the one row where even the reference does not reproduce itself (E3 lights, unseeded `Math.random`) carries 0.00. In the structured summary "—" rows take `conf_seed_exact` = `conf_functional`, as the sibling lanes did, and the overall S is computed over generative rows only.

## §3 What cannot be known from outside

Nothing on this list blocks functional or visual parity: each item is a rule or a constant we would set ourselves and calibrate against the 318 storey views and the twelve exports. What the list blocks is *identity* — deriving Dwellings' code — and same-seed reproduction; the only evidence class that could close it is the one the clean-room ruling bars (the bundle), and the creator has said he will not open-source the generators.

## §5 One paragraph for the owner

Dwellings is the reference we understand best from the outside and least from the inside — and that is exactly the right shape for what we want from it. We know everything it *does*: every control and tag, the exact order of its steps (draw a shape, stack storeys, cut each storey into rooms, add stairs between neighbouring storeys, then try to hand out room names from a list, then windows, furniture, lights, roofs), and the exact form of what it makes, down to the cell, the wall thickness and the door type, because we hold its own exported files for twelve houses and the traced drawings of ninety-two more. A team could build something that behaves and looks like it with high confidence (about four chances in five on both counts) in roughly twenty expert weeks. What we do not know — and cannot without its code — is the handful of small rules that decide *where* each cut goes, *which* room becomes the bathroom and *why* a stair lands where it does; so "the same algorithm" is about a coin flip, and "the same house from the same seed" is effectively nil, as for every reference. For our own floor-plan rung this matters less than it sounds, because we are building the mirror image on purpose: we start from the footprint, storeys, entrance and purpose the town already knows, and fit rooms to a programme, where Dwellings starts from a random shape and names the rooms last — its author says himself that this order cannot guarantee a kitchen, cannot do inns or temples, and makes "house-like dungeons" rather than houses, and for taverns he wrote a second generator that works our way. What carries over is the useful skeleton: the cell-grid room model with doors, windows and stairs as edge and cell records (the export format we should better), the per-room-type record (size rank, windows, doors, which floor), the two stair devices that work (a stacked stairwell or a tower stair fixed *before* the rooms are cut), the vector rendering (thick outer walls, thin inner ones, lights at the "most spacious" point of a room, shading along walls, hip roofs from the outline) and the handoff idea (a settlement passes a footprint on a grid plus the entrance edge — which is what our click will do, with function and storeys added). The two cheapest things still worth doing are to click one building through from each of watabou's town generators and read the address it produces (it tells us exactly what a sender must supply), and, with your approval, to export the one house its author says contains a loop, to settle whether his plans are trees by construction.
